# The reasons behind the core: items 82 to 125

The index, and how an item is written: `archive/instructions.md`.

82. **11 October 2026** (Barkstreet Snakes, 11 October 2026). Asked: what computer players the owner wanted in
    the game. Decided: The game gets three enemy computer players. They must not have superhuman reaction speed, but
    they must be very clever.

83. **14 October 2026** (Barkstreet Snakes, 14 October 2026). Asked: whether the computer players should be made
    better. Decided: They are to be upgraded, if it can be done, by better planning of future steps. It must not be done
    by raising their reaction time, by making them all alike in strategy, or by lowering performance below good gameplay
    levels. If those limits can be kept, it should be done.

84. **Before 20 October 2026** (Barkstreet Snakes, set down on 3 November 2026; undated in its archive). Asked: the
    notes behind its round six A: every new rule needs a visible cue and a lesson, which the owner called a very
    important point. Decided: The owner said to add it.

85. **27 October 2026** (Barkstreet Snakes, 27 October 2026). Asked: whether that game should also test each
    new feature by playing computer players that use it against ones that ignore it, as Amarna Letters does. Decided: If
    the design session's own opinion is that it should, it goes ahead and does it.

86. **28 October 2026** (Amarna Letters, item 18). Asked: a report that found script kings using an item's features won
    no more than those ignoring them. Decided: That the mechanics do not benefit the computer players right now is a big
    problem and needs careful attention. No conclusion can be drawn yet, since the game is not fully constructed, but
    once it is feature complete this cannot be the case.

87. **28 October 2026** (Amarna Letters, item 21). Asked: the review questions of an item. Decided: The session works
    out the answers itself, knowing the owner's wishes by now. Models must also be able to alter clauses, since everyone
    plays the same game.

88. **29 October 2026** (Barkstreet Snakes, 29 October 2026). Asked: the new moves. Decided: It is very
    important that the common computer player is updated to understand all the rule changes, so that it makes the most
    of all the play rules.

89. **31 October 2026** (Barkstreet Snakes, 31 October 2026). Asked: a use-against-ignore test of
    two new moves that came out even; the owner could not believe super flash and super jump are not useful, so the
    computer players must not understand their utility, since super jump lets you fly about five tiles, changing
    direction in mid air, and super flash goes three tiles instead of the regular flash's two and can cut two tiles
    deep. Decided: The owner agreed to the design session's take: the computer players are taught the moves' uses until
    their users come out ahead.

90. **3 November 2026** (Amarna Letters, item 205). Asked: whether a model should manage to play a game that scripts can
    manage, even if not very competently. Decided: The owner agreed with the design session's answer that models play
    through text, so their failures can come from how the game tells them things. What the owner wants is interesting
    play: models should aim to play as competently as they can, which is what is incentivised, but interesting mistakes
    are no issue. The source of such mistakes must be the genuine complexity of the scenario and not the game
    communicating badly.

91. **10 November 2026** (the core). Asked: a yes on the core's rule-file audit and its shared scripts, and on two
    questions (#14: one shared rule for "conditional goods" and "don't prohibit, give it its real cost"; #16: may an
    orchestrator run on the owner's own computer). Decided: The owner agreed to the audit and the scripts. An
    orchestrator may run on the owner's computer, even though it is not the owner's preferred set-up. The two rules are
    not fully the same. One is free game design: an action is not disallowed just because it seems not sensible, such as
    marching through mountains in winter, since that inhibits player agency; the game mechanics instead reflect why it
    was rarely done, and that leaves room for a rare exception from history. The other is the principle that there are
    no solutions, only trade-offs. The owner thinks both principles already apply to both games, and asked the design
    session whether it agreed.

92. **28 October 2026** (Amarna Letters, item 19). Asked: the principles that answer most questions. Decided: One of
    them: do not prohibit an action, reflect its real cost.

93. **31 October 2026** (Barkstreet Snakes, 31 October 2026). Asked: the design session's line that the
    no-solutions-only-trade-offs principle would be approved. Decided: That principle should be included, so that the
    builder understands it.

102. **10 November 2026** (the core). Asked: whether the shared rules should hold some version of two principles of the
     owner's: where reality or history is interesting enough on its own, there is no need to invent fact; and the deeper
     the grounding for a cause, the more compelling it is (the owner's own simulationist tendency and bias). Decided:
     The owner asked for some version of both to be considered for the shared rules.

103. **10 November 2026** (the core). Asked: the design session's proposed wording, which stopped depth where a player
     could no longer follow the cause. Decided: The owner agreed, with a nuance. A model must not automatically assume
     that its inability to convey the mechanics clearly is the same as a player being incapable of following to real
     depths. The owner believes as deep a simulation as possible gives more compelling outcomes, even when the player
     cannot fully understand or appreciate the mechanics. The trade-off the design session rightly pointed out stays:
     there is a point of diminishing returns, where the rule must settle at a floor for simulation that gives the player
     the most without needing a data center to run. The owner also asked whether the rules the design session identified
     from Amarna Letters can apply more widely to all the owner's games: "Period first" (the period as it was, given up
     for playability only after trying to keep both), "Numbers come from their causes", "Every error has a motive" and
     "Nothing appears or vanishes without a cause".

104. **10 November 2026** (the core). Asked: the two principles reworded with the owner's nuance and with those Amarna
     Letters rules folded in as their forms. Decided: The owner agreed.

105. **10 November 2026** (the core). Asked: whether four more principles of the owner's games hold for every game the
     owner makes: Amarna Letters' "Different data, same rules" (its design pillars, decided 19 October 2026), "The order
