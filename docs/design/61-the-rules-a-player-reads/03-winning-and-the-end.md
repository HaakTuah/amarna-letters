# Winning and the end of the game

How the score is made, when the game ends, and what happens to a king who is taken or dies.

A realm's strength is ten times what its land produces in a season (its harvest at the price of grain, a shekel a
measure, plus its mines' gold), plus its gold and what its people and men hold besides, never counting what it owes. Each
province counts as far as it obeys you. Its growth is that strength against its strength when its king came to the
throne (at the start, for the kings the game begins with). At the end every person, kings and characters alike, stands
on one scoreboard, and each score blends two things: performance, a king's growth, ranked among the results of powers that started where his did (most of the score, so a
city that holds its own can win as well as an empire), a character's gain in gold since he came into play ranked among
what the best five characters of each trial game gained; and size, what he holds against the largest holding (a small part, 2
percent by default). The dead rank below the living, by what they held when they died. The highest on the board wins,
king or character.

Any season may be the last. Early on the end is very unlikely; it grows likelier every year, and half of all games
are over by 1320 BCE, about season 80. The last season is drawn in secret when the game begins: nobody can read it, and
your court's omens darken as it nears. A table can change how long games run, or play on with no end at all.

A king is a person. He may travel without his court, commanding from where he is, near the front and at the risk of his
life. Taken in the city where he stands, in a battle or on the road, he is his captor's captive, to be put to death,
ransomed, released or handed over, and he may get away by his own skill, helped by a man he sends for and hindered by his captor's watchmen; a court taken,
in its city or stopped on the road, loses its treasury and its captives, who go free, and flees by road to the nearest
city its realm still holds. While he is held, his player plays the captive:
he knows of his realm only what word from his court brings him, and writes letters that leave through his captor's
court, which reads them and may keep them, agreeing in a letter home to his ransom, to giving in or to a spy sent to free
him; a caretaker keeps his realm in his name meanwhile, defending it and keeping its dues but beginning nothing new. He dies only by a cause where he is (in battle, in a city's fall, put to death
or on the road), never of age, and then his player is out: his
heir reigns from that season, the next name on the record of the kings who followed (or past it a son or brother), with
a third of his father's standing among the people and the soldiers. A realm that holds nothing has fallen, and the heir of its last king lives on in exile with a claim to its throne; its armies
with a commander become his band, and the others disband or follow a captain of their own. A player who is out watches
the rest of the game in god view (a table setting).

There is one winner. Shared victory for sworn allies is a house rule, off by default.
