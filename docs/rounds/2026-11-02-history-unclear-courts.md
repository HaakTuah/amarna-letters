## Facts that changed

- **Enlil-kidinni** (`06-enlil-kidinni`, `14-enlil-kidinni`): in office about 1342-1336 on Brinkman's dating (*JAOS*
  124, 2004), so perhaps a few years after 1346 [C], not "in office in 1346 [A]". The game keeps him at Nippur, as the
  nearest attested holder of the office, and grades him C in `Governors1346` (`src/engine/src/rules/cast.ts`). The
  grade is data only: no draw or text changes.
- **Manapa-Tarhunta's letter**: early 13th century, probably to Muwattalli II [B] (*The Ahhiyawa Texts*, p. 7).
- **Mashhuiluwa** came to Suppiluliuma "as a fugitive" (AhT 1B §12'); that his brothers drove him out is in no text read.
- **Ugarit** stayed loyal to Hatti when Mukish, Nuhashshe and Niya turned against it (RS 17.340; de Martino 2022, p. 45).
- **The Libyans**: Egypt's foes in its 18th-Dynasty texts (Breasted II, §892); the names Ded and Keper the game uses
  are confirmed (Breasted III-IV).
- **Mari** survived as a small settlement, not a ruin; **Elam's** Babylonian marriage two generations before 1346.
- **Kizzuwatna**: Beckman's unchecked wording replaced by Luckenbill's translation of KBo I 5 (1921, p. 181).
- **Three name styles** (Sherden and Lukka, Nubians beyond Egypt, Suteans and Habiru) have no attested names: each is
  marked [G] invented for the game.

## Still unclear

Telipinu "the priest" as king of Aleppo and the Zida row rest on the *Deeds* or Beckman's *Hittite Diplomatic Texts*,
both lend-only; Luckenbill's Aleppo treaty does not name Telipinu. Three Linear B names in the Aegean style are marked
"not checked here" [C].
