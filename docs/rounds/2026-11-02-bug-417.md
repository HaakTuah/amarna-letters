# Bug #417: names as claims, the points left for after 17++

The last three points of #348's second review round (item 17+, "A power's name is a claim"), left for after item 17++
part C (#378): `docs/rounds/2026-11-02-from-348-round-2.md`, "Left for after 17++". PR #534.

## What changed

- **(a) Notices in their recipient's names.** The engine writes each power by its own land's name, while a court's
  view may give a contested name to another power. `notify` now turns a notice into the recipient court's names as it
  is written (`namedFor` in `model/power.ts`, beside the false personal names it already put in): every power whose name
  that court knows or gives otherwise is renamed, all in one pass, so the two claimants of one name never swap twice,
  and a house name ("Bit-Rib-Hadda") is left as it is. What the notice quotes (a sealed greeting, a letter, a king's, an
  envoy's or a captive's words) is left as written: it is the evidence of the name its writer used (Codex, round 1). The
  engine marks those words where it quotes them (`quoteWords`), so a writer's own double quotes cannot open them to
  renaming (Claude's reviewer, round 2); words it quotes unmarked (a trail's outcomes) are told by their double quotes. The one notice that speaks of a name itself, the claimant's own
  "A name claimed", keeps it (`notify`'s `named` false). Logs and the god view keep the engine's names.
- **(i) Late tablets.** A sealed dispatch carries the season it was sealed (`Dispatch.sealed`), and a court keeps the
  newest greeting it has read by that season (`HeardName { name, sealed }` in `GameState.namesHeard`, its own land's
  name included, which before was kept as no entry): an older tablet arriving late sets nothing back. The season of a
  claim (`NameClaim.since`), read by nothing, is gone: `GameState.names` holds the name alone.
- **A dismissed spy's stirring gold.** One no longer on his errand who still carries gold for stirring a province now
  counts it as carried ("gold kept on dismissal or capture"), as a caught spy and one dismissed on the road home already
  did (`turn/unrest.ts`).
- **A fallen claimant claims nothing, once the court hears of the fall** (Claude's reviewer, round 1; Codex and Claude's
  reviewer, round 2): a court went on knowing the name it read of a claimant after he fell, and gave it to both him and
  the land's own power. A fallen power now goes by its own land's name, or by its house where another still claims that
  name (`landFor`), but only for a court that has heard of the fall (`GameState.fallsHeard`, now in the registry, as
  the seat view's `fallen` reads it); before the word comes the court names him as it did. The registry is set again as
  each court hears of a fall. This also closes the same leak in the code before: `claimantsOf` left out every fallen
  power, so a fall changed another claimant's name before the court could know of it.
- **The page's registry holds only the claims heard** (Claude's reviewer, round 1; Codex, round 2): no court's record of
  a claimant's own land's name, and no court with none, and the falls of its one human seat alone.
- Save version 46 (45 went to #544, merged first). Design note: `docs/design/46-the-opening-as-it-stood.md` ("A court learns of a claim", "Naming as a
  court gives it"). Rulebook and README: a court's notices, too, call the other claimant by his house.

## Hidden information

`namedFor` reads only the recipient court's own knowledge (the claims it has read, the names it gives), the same registry
its seat view reads, so a notice tells it nothing its view does not. Every path reads the stored notice: the latest
season, past seasons and replays (the notice is written once, into the state, and replays write it again from the same
state), and the god view shows the logs, which keep the engine's names. The canary grows no case: its world has no name
claimed that the engine knows (its claim is a made-up name, kept for the export's stand-in check), and planting a
contested name there would move every one of its seeds for what `names-as-claims.test.ts` already shows on a smaller game.

## Readings taken

- **A notice's names are its court's when it is written,** not re-read later: a notice is a report the court received,
  as a letter is, and its names are those the court used then (the false personal names in `notify` work the same way).
  Principle: one fact, one place; the inbox is the record of what the court was told.
- **A land's name in a notice is read as the realm's,** a province of that name included (a city-state's land and
  capital share a name): a notice cannot tell which is meant, and naming the realm the court's way is the reading that
  never lets one name mean two powers. Seen only where a court has heard a claim to that name.
- **Unmarked quotations are told by their double quotes,** opened after a space, a bracket or the start and closed
  before a space, a stop or the end: only words the engine quotes without `quoteWords` (a trail's outcomes, which go to
  logs and views as well, where a mark would have to be taken out) are read so.
- **A renamed name is capitalised at a sentence's head:** at the start, after a stop (and any closing quote or bracket)
  and a space, or after a new line, an opening bracket or quote between. A quotation ending in a question or a cry
  that the same sentence runs on after would read as a sentence's end; no notice the engine writes does that.
- **Adjectives are left** ("Egyptian troops"): they name a people, not the claim.

## Benchmarks

`scripts/sim.sh --games 20 --seed 1 --verify-replay`, `develop` at d5ca4b0 against this branch: every seed plays the same
game (the same winners and lengths, 93.7 seasons on average; Egypt 6 wins, Hatti 4, Babylonia 3; the same growth for
every power) and all 20 replay. The one line that moves is the characters' gold: "gold kept on dismissal or capture"
0.0% to 0.1% of all they gained, the dismissed spies' stirring gold no longer counted as skimmed. No seed moved, so the
save version goes up for the new shapes of the names' record alone (46). Nothing here is a feature to use or ignore:
the fixes change what courts are told, not what they may do.
