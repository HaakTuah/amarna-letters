## What was built

See `docs/design/37-answers-to-13.md`. In short:

- **A realm comes apart as the news arrives.** The capital passes at once to whoever took it; every other province
  stays the fallen power's until word of the fall reaches it, at the pace word of a fall reaches foreign courts, a
  season at least. Until then its harvest is taxed into its stores as before, its troops hold where they stand, and the
  gold of its mines goes to the fallen capital, so to whoever holds it. When the word arrives: the conqueror's troops
  there make it his, a governor there rules it in his own name, the rest falls lawless; then the armies there hear it
  (their commanders' bands, a captain from the ranks, or disbanded, as item 13 built).
- **A governor's seat of his own.** Powers can now rise in play (`RisenPower`, `GameState.risen`, `AllPowers` growing
  as they rise), the first ones a fallen kingdom's governors: a power named by its province, its capital there, his
  purse its treasury, the troops there following him if he can pay them a season, his character staying in play as its
  ruler (duty `Ruling`), and a script seat with his temper. Courts that see his city learn of it at once, the rest as
  word travels. From then on it is a power like any city-state, falling with its capital.
- The rulebook (`llm/src/rules.ts`, step 10 of the season; `rules-command.ts`, governors) says both; the batch report
  counts the governors' seats (`risenReport`) and the signals `risen`, `fall-held` and `fall-lawless`; the web map draws
  risen powers in one shared tone with a city's tower.

## Readings taken

1. **The pace of the news** is the pace word of the fall reaches foreign courts (`travelTurns` from the fallen
   capital, a season at least), not the realm's couriers'. The item says "carried by road like any news", and the
   fallen court's couriers end with it; at the couriers' pace every home province of every Great King lies within a
   season (Akhetaten to Sumur included), so nothing would change. *Information moves in physical time*; one rule for one
   piece of news.
2. **Only the capital passes at once** (and, as before, a court taken in the same season down a chain of falls). Land
   where the conqueror's troops stand is his when the word reaches it, not before: until then "life there goes on as
   before". Unwalled land his troops march into is his at once anyway, by the ordinary rule of land where troops stand.
   *One thing happens by rule, the rest through the ordinary rules.*
3. **Life going on as before** is the land's: harvest, tax into the province's stores, its people. Its troops get no
   orders (nobody gives them), no pay (nobody pays them; they are not counted unpaid either, since nobody owes it), and
   they eat as troops do. The gold of its mines goes to the fallen capital as before and so to whoever holds it, as a
   load on the road from beyond the season's reach; with nobody holding the capital it stays with the province's
   people. *Nothing appears or vanishes; everything is somewhere.*
4. **"If he can pay them"**: the troops in his province or its waters follow him if his treasury covers a season of
   their pay, unit by unit, first guess. The rest hear the news as the rest of the army does. *Incentives, not quotas.*
5. **His purse is his treasury.** A ruler's gold is his realm's, and nothing appears. *Nothing appears or vanishes.*
6. **The governor stays a character**, duty `Ruling`, employed by his own power at its court: "he is a character
   already, so the same mind plays him". If his power falls he is a free man there, as a ceded ruler is. If he dies,
   the power goes on, as every city-state does without a ruler character (a successor is item 17's "A head rises").
7. **A risen power is named by its province** (with his name after it if a power already goes by the name), shares one
   colour on the map, and is a power like any other: on the one scoreboard ("One scoreboard for every power"), its
   growth reckoned from its strength when it rose, and it can win. Par is the fixed powers' (`ParResults`): a risen
   power has no starting position to set it from. *Different data, same rules.*
8. **A script seat plays it** in every game, the runner's own script king, with a personality from his traits; on
   replay the log's decisions. A model seat for it returns with model games ("No model games until the build order is
   done"; *Models only where they matter*: a governor-turned-king is a court like a city-state's, and the table's
   setting for city-states would decide once models play).
9. **The land he rules is not his homeland on the map** (the map's homes are the starting powers'), but it is his own
   people's and has nothing to absorb: he holds it in full from the start. *Numbers come from their causes.*
10. **A governor who has not yet arrived** when the word comes governs nothing: the province falls lawless and he walks
    home, as item 13 built.
