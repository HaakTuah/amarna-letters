## What was built

Haakon's answers to item 11b's review ("Agree to all, expertly"), each by "The game in one page":

- **A ransom demand is a message someone sends**: it waits at the captor's capital until someone sets out for the
  payer's court, his own people or any traveller, who carries it for nothing, or the captor hires a carrier; the demand stands eight seasons. Rumours
  and word of the world stay talk at the pace of the roads.
- **The spy decides**: no rule moves a spy whose reports wait. A passing traveller is paid on delivery by his court's
  standing order, else by the spy from its own purse if it is loyal enough; after its own wait (two seasons, one more
  if brave, one more if loyal) it carries them home itself.
- **The border hand-over**: a spy watching a province just across its master's border walks its reports to the nearest
  province of his, where the realm's couriers take them.
- **Armies hunt bands only on order**: `hunt` (next season, a province) or the standing order `huntBands` ("hunt any
  band in my land"), which the king may stop. Troops standing in a province without the order still halve what bands
  take there.
- **Robbers choose whether to kill**: a ruthless chief (disloyal or greedy) kills a robbed traveller who could name him
  where nobody hunts him; a careful one never does. The fixed odds are gone.
- **Anyone may sell what he knows**: servants abroad note news as free people do; a greedy, disloyal servant notes its
  own master's court and sells it, found out three times in ten (a fact its master knows, a notice).
- **Not built here, as the item says**: a band hired as soldiers (item 13); a pardon, which is words or a sealed
  promise, with no order of its own; and **a model chief's own request** for raids, ships and the way back (see
  "Left for a session of its own" below).

The rulebook's ROADS part says all of it; the kings' view shows the standing hunt; the parser takes `hunt_bands`,
`hunt_bands_always` and `stop_hunting_bands`; the web order form has a button for the standing hunt.

## Script players: why hunting and news did not pay, and the patch

Measured, not assumed, on seeds 1-50 with `--outlaw-play-half` (half the kings hunt and buy news, half do neither):

| Script kings that use the features | Users | Ignorers |
|---|---|---|
| Hunting by standing order as soon as a band of 40 works in their land, marching spare armies on bands, buying news of foes | 1.08 growth, 20 wins | 1.16, 30 |
| The same, without the marches | 1.09, 24 | 1.20, 26 |
| Buying news only, no hunting | 1.14, 26 | 1.13, 24 |
| **Patched**: a hunt only where it breaks the band or leaves it nowhere of ours to flee; marches likewise | **1.13, 23** | **1.14, 27** |

- **Why hunting did not pay**: a hunted band that survives flees to a land province next door with no army in it, most
  often another of the hunter's own, where it robs at the full rate instead of the half it took beside his troops; and
  the standing order hunts every band an army stands on. So most hunts moved the robbing rather than ended it (1,546
  hunts in the users' half of the games, for less growth than leaving the bands alone).
- **The patch**: script kings order a hunt (`hunt`, one province, the next season) only where an army of theirs stands
  with the band and the hunt breaks it (fewer than its least men left after the losses that army deals) or no province of
  theirs without troops lies next door for it to flee to; they never give the standing order; spare armies march on a
  band only where a hunt by an army of their usual size would pay. The rulebook now says where a hunted band flees and
  how many men a hunt kills, which the scripts weigh.
- **Why news did not pay**: the scripts bought news of any court they did not trust; now only of their war target or a
  hostile court, at most a fiftieth of their gold. Buying news alone is even with ignoring it (the third row): news
  sold about twenty times in fifty games, too rarely to move the score, since few sellers stand at a court with fresh
  news of its foes. Nothing yet makes the scripts act on news differently from a spy's report.
- **Result**: users and ignorers now come out even within the noise of fifty games (the news-only row shows how large
  that is). Not paying yet: troops standing in a province already halve what bands take, hunting or not, so the hunt's
  gain is small at these first guesses. Re-measure with policing (item 15) and command (item 13).
- **Secrets sold**: a script king dismisses a servant found selling his secrets ("Sold the secrets" among the
  blemishes and thefts it weighs).

selling secrets was known to its master at once, at any distance: the word now goes from the buyer's court by the roads,

## Readings taken and design questions

1. **A ransom demand is carried for nothing** by any traveller going to the payer's court (a message about the payer's
   own man, which the payer wants to hear); when nobody goes, the captor, by his order to demand it, hires a free person at his court and pays
   the whole fee now from his treasury. Alternative: the demand waits until someone goes, or the order names a carrier
   and fee. (Everything travels; every payment by order.)
2. **A spy pays a passing traveller from its own purse only if its loyalty is at least 30**; it carries its reports
   itself after two seasons, one more if brave (courage 60+) and one more if loyal (loyalty 60+). (The man decides,
   by his traits; first guesses for item 18.)
3. **The standing hunt covers the king's whole land**, and a hunt by order the one province for the next season.
   Alternative: the standing hunt by province.
4. **A ruthless chief** is one with loyalty under 40 or greed over 70; he kills a witness only where the land's king
   has no standing hunt and no army in or next to the province. (Robbers weigh being hunted.)
5. **A servant's secrets are caught 30 times in a hundred** (first guess), and only a greedy, disloyal servant sells
   them.
6. **A model chief still decides by formula** until the request of its own is built (below); so does a model spy, by
   `selfWait` and `payLoyalty`, until it has a choice in its own request.
7. **A king's own servant may sell him news of another court** it saw ("anyone may sell what he knows"). Alternative:
   a servant's news goes to its master for nothing, as part of its errand.
8. **The border walk takes its time but not its risks**: the report reaches the border after the walk's seasons and
   the spy stays at its post. Alternative: the spy walks it there, leaving its post and meeting the road's dangers.
9. **Only a king's servant is a witness worth killing**, and a chief weighs the land's armies he can see, never the
   king's orders. Alternative: every traveller a witness, or a chief who hears of a standing hunt by talk.
