## What was built

- **Biographies**: the follow view gains "In the record": every event naming the person (only while no one else bears
  the name), the tablets they sealed with their king's seal, their capture or death abroad, and their rise if they rose.
  A court's follow view lists only the events it saw.
- **Histories of tablets**: every tablet sealed or forged: proposed, sealed and by whose hand, what it says, forged by
  whom and for whom, repudiated, each copy that reached another court and how, and, for a tablet of peace, the first
  province one of its kings took from the other after sealing it as "Broken".
- **The rise and fall of courts**: the land at the start and at the height, every province taken, lost and ceded, the
  first entries into each other's land, tablets, falls, the chronicle's lines, and how it ended the age.
- **The record opens**: when a game ends, every seat may take god view; the Legends tab opens on each deception by kind
  (who lied, who skimmed, forged seals, go-betweens who bent words, false gold, what was sold, bribed envoys, stolen
  tablets, go-betweens carrying words), secret masters, and the season the end was drawn for. The rulebook tells kings
  beforehand that the record opens at the end.
- **Finkel's version**: the third time a court hears a king's own envoys beg it for gold, the record keeps the line,
  seen by the court begged, and the begged court's annals write it; a greedy script king short of gold may once in a game
  beg a stronger friend in Finkel's words.
- **A new Idrimi**: a governor who rises to rule his province (13+) is named so in his biography, both courts' legends
  and his own annals.

## Readings taken

Each rests on "The game in one page" or the item's sections.

1. **Legends are god view's**: the Legends tab shows to spectators and, once the record opens, to every seat; during
   play a court reads its own past from its events, inbox and archive, and the follow view keeps its court's filter.
   Principle: "Reputation ... not a record known to all at once"; the section says the record opens "when a game ends".
2. **The record opens by letting every seat take god view** at the end, and closes again if the table plays on into an
   open age (the game goes on, and with it the deception). Principle: the section's "every hidden fact becomes readable
   to every seat"; an open age is still a game.
3. **The drawn last season shows only once the age has ended**, to spectators too. Principle: item 6a, nothing shown
   reads it.
4. **"Broken" is read only for tablets of peace** (peace, no-attack, alliance) as land one king took from the other
   after sealing, never for ceded land or a repudiated tablet. Other promises (tribute, support) are kept or broken by
   orders whose absence the record cannot tell from a choice; the engine does not judge them. Principle: "the narrator
   may phrase but never invent"; "Keeping a promise is an act".
5. **A third plea is counted per king and court, as heard** (the envoy's delivered clauses at the audience), not as
   written, an impostor's never; only the court begged sees the line. Principle: "Everything travels"; "Words are words".
6. **Finkel's letter is the script king's free words**, so it travels only in full press; in a game of script characters
   (structured press, as the batch plays) the letter is its clauses and the echo is the chronicle line, which the item
   offers as the alternative ("a rare letter ... or a chronicle line").
7. **A new Idrimi reads the record of powers risen**, not a new event: since 13+ only a fallen kingdom's governor rises;
   a commander keeps his army as a band when his kingdom falls and rules no city (item 13), so no commander's path exists
   yet; an outlaw's band rises with item 17's seats and will read the same way. Principle: no stopgaps.
8. **A biography pins an event on a person only by a name no one else bears**. Principle: never invent.
