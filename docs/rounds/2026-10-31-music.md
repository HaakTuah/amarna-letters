## What was built

All of it is described in [the design note](../design/45-the-music-and-the-sounds.md); in short:

- **The template**: Empire Earth's eighteen music files are fourteen pieces, four in two versions (titles and lengths from
  fan uploads of the game's files; composers Steve Maitland and Ed Lima). One piece of ours for each, same role.
- **The dynamic music**: the mood from what the seat knows (calm, tension, war, siege, battle, the last seasons; menu on
  the first screen), changes at a phrase end with a 3 s cross, three variations a piece never repeated running, rests
  between quiet pieces, moments (triumph, the city falls, lament, solemn, the age's end) after their stings.
- **The sounds**: 21 short sounds x 3 variations, triggered by the player's acts and by what a season brings; placed on
  the map and heard by zoom (item 17+++'s rule); 3 stings; 4 world beds following the map's centre.
- **Loading**: AAC .m4a, music streamed, sounds fetched on first use and cached; nothing in the install, nothing before the
  first touch. About 28 MB of audio in all, of which a session downloads only what it hears.
- **Switches**: Music and Sound with volumes, in the game menu and on the listening page, remembered.
- **The listening page**: `/listen`, "Music" in the top bar.
- **Tests**: `src/web/test/audio-situation.test.ts` (9: moods, what the seat did not see, sieges and omens, foreign
  troops, a capital's sting and moment, the war horn by seat and the three-sound limit, the age's end, the bed by the map,
  hearing by zoom). Checked in Chromium (Playwright, a watched script game): calm played The River, the first fight
  brought the war horn and Battle, a capital's sting, the siege camp's bed, no errors.

## Readings taken

1. **Quiet by default** is on at a low volume, sounding from the first touch (browsers allow nothing earlier). Principle:
   the item asks for quiet, not silence; taste, so also question 1.
2. **Only what the seat knows chooses the music** (pillar 4, "Information moves in physical time"): its sight, the moves
   it saw, the owners as it knows them, its news; a capital's sting plays when the news of it arrives, not when it falls.
3. **"War is declared" is a war's first blow**: there is no declaration in the rules ("Physics, not politics"), so the
   war horn sounds when the seat fights after a season without fighting.
4. **Egypt's trumpets for Egypt's seat, a war horn for the others**: presentation and voice may name a power ("Different
   data, same rules").
5. **A king's death is a power's fall** until item 17b makes kings mortal ("A realm holds by its ledger"); then the king's
   sting follows the death itself.
6. **The capital's sting is for a great king's capital, or one the seat lost or took**: in god view every city-state's
   capital made it routine, and a great moment that comes every season is not one.
7. **Empire Earth's roles are read from its titles and lengths**: no source found says when the game played each piece.
8. **The event stream is not built yet** (items 17a, 17+++): until it lands, a season's sounds play together as it
   arrives, from the frame's events, moves, sieges and owners; positions come from what the frame knows (the province
   attacked, the city besieged, the land that changed hands). Principle: "No stopgaps": nothing invented for the
   meantime, the same mapping moves onto the stream.
9. **AAC in .m4a** rather than Opus: it plays in every current browser including older Safari; one format, no doubled
   files.
10. **The choir is synthesised**: no CC0 choir recording was found; every other instrument is a CC0 recording.
11. **The Hurrian Hymn's reading** (for the placeholders and as a start for the composer): strings 1 to 7 rising, in nīd
    qablim on A; the first string named sings, the second sounds beneath; the numerals are lengths in beats. Lines 1 and 2
    of the notation as Wikipedia's "Hurrian songs" gives them; lines 3 and 4 as Laroche's edition is usually reproduced, to be
    checked against Dietrich and Loretz (1975) and West (1994). The seven tunings read as the seven diatonic modes after
    Kilmer and Crocker.
12. **A phrase end more than 20 s away is not waited for** (some placeholder phrases are 30 s): the item asks for changes
    at a phrase's end, but a war that starts must be heard within seconds; the composer can shorten phrases.
13. **The reviewer's findings** (one Opus reviewer): four blocking (changes slipping past phrase ends, the first touch on a
    phone not starting the music, a stale pause silencing a reused deck, a siege sound placed out of the seat's sight)
    fixed with a test for the last; of the rest, the sight gate on sieges and unrest, land taken or lost unplaced, the
    bed at every zoom, a moment already playing cleared, the listening page heard whatever the switches say, and the mood
    set in an effect were done. Left as they are: cues replay when stepping forward through history one season at a time
    (useful in a replay); the envoy's lyre is played from the render that first shows the envoy (guarded by its key);
    the worker's audio cache is not pruned of old hashes (sounds only, under 2 MB a render); the repository keeps each
    render of the audio in its history (about 28 MB now), so the composer should render once a piece is settled.
14. **Network**: github.com's pages and API were blocked by the environment's network policy; git over the proxy worked,
    so the two libraries were fetched by partial clone, as `docs/audio/SOURCES.md` says.

## Handover to the composer

### What is built and where

| Part | File | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pieces (placeholders) | `scripts/music/pieces.py` | One function per piece, `piece(v) -> Score` for variation v = 0, 1, 2; `PIECES` maps id to function |
| Building blocks | `scripts/music/parts.py` | `held`, `pad`, `choir`, `drone`, `melody` (with ornaments), `drums` (rhythms in `RHYTHMS`), `lyre` |
| Tunings and the hymn | `scripts/music/hymn.py` | `Mode(tuning, tonic)` for the seven tunings, `hymn()` and `hymn_in(mode)` for the notation's pairs |
| Score and render | `scripts/music/score.py` | `Score(bpm, seed)`: `note`, `hit`, `sing`, `phrase(beat)`; the hall, mastering, `write()` |
| Instruments | `scripts/music/samples.py` | `CATALOGUE`: 35 instruments, gains and pans; the recordings levelled and pitch-measured |
| Choir | `scripts/music/voice.py` | Formant-wavetable singers, vowels a, o, u |
| Sounds, stings, beds | `scripts/music/sounds.py` | `SOUNDS`, `STINGS`, `BEDS` |
| Render | `scripts/music/render.py` | `python3 scripts/music/render.py music [ids]`, `... sounds [ids]`; writes `src/web/public/audio/` and the manifest |
| A look without ears | `scripts/music/inspect_audio.py` | Spectrogram, loudness curve, clicks |
| Manifest | `scripts/music/manifest.json`, `src/web/src/audio/manifest.ts` | Generated: every file, length, hash, phrase ends |
| Which piece plays when | `src/web/src/audio/situation.ts` | `MoodPieces`, `MomentPieces`, `moodAt`, `cuesAt`, `bedAt`, `hearing` |
| Playback | `src/web/src/audio/player.ts` | The director (phrase-end changes, variations, rests, stings), sounds, beds, the listening page's solo |
| Titles and the template | `src/web/src/audio/catalogue.ts` | Each piece's title, Empire Earth's, role, when, how made; `Placeholder` |

**Adding or replacing a piece**: write its function in `pieces.py` (any number of variations: set `VARIATIONS` in
`render.py`, or let the function ignore v), mark each place it may hand over with `s.phrase(beat)` (the director changes
only there, at most 12 s apart is best), render it (`render.py music <id>`), and, for a new id, add it to `PIECES`,
to a mood or moment in `situation.ts` and to `catalogue.ts`. The player needs nothing else. Layers within a piece are
its own (the score's parts); the system's layers are the mood's music, the moment's piece after a sting, the sounds above,
and the world's bed beneath. When the first real piece lands, set `Placeholder` to false.

### The sample library

CC0, from VSCO 2 CE and VCSL: string sections (violins, violas, celli, sustained, tremolo and pizzicato), a solo double bass,
folk harp, concert harp, psaltery, oboe (two articulations), clarinet, flute, tenor recorder, horn, trumpet, frame drums
(open, muted, high), darbuka (dum and tek), a large hand drum, timpani and rolls, gong, finger cymbals, sleigh bells,
tambourine, shaker, Nepalese hand bells, claps, slit drum, ocean drum, a cymbal scrape. Folders and how to fetch them:
`docs/audio/SOURCES.md`. Every recording is levelled at load (held notes by loudness, struck and plucked by peak), so
`gain` in `CATALOGUE` is a mix choice. Velocity changes timbre (the layers) and level (about 7 dB from 0.3 to 0.6).

### How to render and listen

Fetch the samples (`docs/audio/SOURCES.md`), then `python3 scripts/music/render.py music` (about 15 minutes for all 42
files) or `... music <id>` for one piece. Listen on `/listen` (`npm run dev --workspace src/web`, then
http://localhost:5183/listen), or on the PR's preview. In the game, any watched game (`/play?watch=3`) plays the
dynamic music; `window.amarnaAudio.status()` in the console says what plays and why.
