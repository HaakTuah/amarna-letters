## What was built

- **The people's strength by their number** (`resistanceOf`, `wallMenOf`): the men of fighting age at home, as hostile
  as they are to the mover, 5,000 for one full unit, as a unit's men count; in the open twice on rough ground and half on
  open plain.
- **In the open** they resist an army that enters, alone where no troop stands and beside the troop that holds there,
  added to its hold.
- **On the walls**: in a walled province the men stand on the walls, not in the open, so an army walks in and may
  besiege; a storm must beat the walls' level plus the garrison and the city's men, multiplied by the walls (walls 1
  double them, 3 quadruple them). The reports name the garrison, the city's men and the multiple.
- **Par's results** (`rules/par-results.ts`): each position's growth in 50 script games (seeds 101-150), a fall as 0,
  sorted, generated by `scripts/sim.sh --par-out`. Performance is the rank among them.
- **The final score** blends size (strength against the largest living realm's) and performance, each 0 to 1, at
  `sizeWeight` 50 percent (a first guess for item 18). The end event and the web app's Standings show every living
  power's place, score, size and performance; the batch report each power's mean score and the games with a kingdom
  fallen in the first two seasons.
- **The rulebook** (HOW YOU WIN, PAR, PEOPLE, WALLS AND SIEGES, TERRAIN), the view's RESISTANCE line, the About page.

## Script players

- **Reckoning**: the script kings and the baseline reckon the men in the open (added to a troop's defence) and on the
  walls (`Board.stormNeeds`, `Board.wallMen`); their own search runs the adjudicator with the view's figures.
- **Weaknesses found and patched**: (1) the storm's need is fractional since the men count by number, and the helpers
  for a storm were cut to whole units below it, so kings that reckon the men never planned a storm (found by the review);
  (2) the scripts valued a siege at 0.6 / (1 + 0.25 x walls) of a storm, from when storms were the way in, though now
  nearly every city falls to a siege: 0.85 / (1 + 0.2 x walls). Before both, kings that reckon the men grew 0.71 against
  0.76 for those that ignore them; after, 0.77 against 0.75.
- **Used**: storms 7 in 50 games (112 before), sieges won 485 (363), the people holding off a move 60 times (78).
- **Placement**: the scripts still weigh rivals by performance and size by strength (`Board.leader`); playing for a
  better place rather than against the leader is item 18's, with the weight (reading 12).

## Readings taken

1. **The men count as a unit's men count**, 5,000 for one full unit, as hostile as they are (the item's text: "counted
   by their number as every unit is").
2. **The walls multiply by one more than their level** (walls 1 double the defenders), and their level still adds to the
   price of a storm (the item's text: "the walls multiplying the defenders as they price the storm"; Numbers come from
   their causes: a wall with nobody on it is still a wall). Alternative: multiply by the level alone, so walls 1 add
   nothing to the men.
3. **One body of men, in one place**: in a walled province the men stand on the walls and do not also meet the army in
   the open, so an army can walk in and besiege (Everything is somewhere; the item: "falls to blockade, hunger or a
   breach").
4. **Only the men at home** fight: those away in a levy do not (`menAvailable`) (Everything is somewhere).
5. **In the open the men stand beside the troop there**, not only where none stands (the item lists "only where no troop
   stands" among the causes; "The people defend their land": "an army that enters a province not its own meets its men",
   and "a kingdom's land whose garrison has gone still fights"). Measured: without it the Kaska lands, which have no
   walls, fell to Hatti in season 2 on 14 of 16 seeds.
6. **No ground for the men on the walls**: the walls are their help there; a garrison's ground counts as before (no
   double help).
7. **Performance is the mid-rank, interpolated** between neighbouring results, a fall tied lowest (the item: "placed
   among the results", "a fall counted as the lowest").
8. **Par from seeds 101-150, the winners measured on seeds 1-50**, so no game is ranked against itself (Truth is
   expensive: a fair test).
9. **The rulebook gives each position's falls and its survivors' quarters**, not all 50 results (Requests are rules too:
   enough to reason from, no wall of numbers).
10. **Size is strength against the largest living realm's** at the end, the same measure as wealth (the section: "its
    wealth as held", "a share of the largest realm's").
11. **The weight is not given in the rulebook yet**: kings are told the score blends size and performance and that no
    court can measure the standings; the weight once item 18 sets it (the section: "once item 18 has set it").
12. **The scripts' placement play waits for item 18**: they weigh rivals by performance and size by strength already, and
    how a king plays for fifth place over first depends on the weight item 18 sets (No stopgaps).
13. **A shared victory compares the pair's scores**, each weighed by its starting strength, as before with growth.
14. **No people in a head-on fight**: a move that beats the troop coming the other way meets no men of the land, both
    being on the road (the battle is between the two armies; "The people defend their land" counts against an army that
    enters, and this one met its enemy before it did). In the rulebook's PEOPLE.
15. **The scripts' thresholds keep their scale**: standing is twice the rank, so 1 is the middle of a position's results
    and `LeadFloor` 1.15 now means a rank above 0.575 (No stopgaps: the scale the scripts were written for).
16. **Tests** that test another rule switch the people off (`peopleDefence: 0`) or score by performance alone
    (`sizeWeight: 0`), and one moved from seed 1 to seed 2 (the smallest game that tests the rule).

First guesses for item 18: 5,000 men for a full unit, the walls' multiple (1 + level), the size weight 50 percent, the
scripts' worth of a siege.

report gives a total that adds up from its rounded parts; the About page and a design note still spoke of growth alone;

(14); `LeadFloor`'s new meaning is noted (15). **Measured as asked**: the people in open land have no cap now, so the
falls per game and the spread of wins are below.
