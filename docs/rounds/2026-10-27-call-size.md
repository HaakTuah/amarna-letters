## What was built

0. **Haakon's answers to item 6a's review** (merged into `develop` while this item was in review, and put first in the
   item's text: "First, Haakon's answers to item 6a's review ... an open age with model seats asks for its spending
   limit by hand ("An open age"), and the table's median reaches every seat in words, script kings included"):
   - **The median in words**: three bands with no numbers (a median up to 30 seasons is an age "likely to be short",
     from 60 one "likely to be long", the usual words between; first guesses), the same in the rulebook, every seat's
     view and the web app's header; a script king believes the band's own median (24, 44 or 75 seasons), never the
     table's number.
   - **An open age's spending limit by hand**: with any model-played seat (a simulated API aside), the setup refuses to
     begin an open age while the limit is still the suggested one, and playing on asks for a limit above what the game
     has spent. The batch runner has no open age, so nothing changes there.

1. **The measure of call size** (`src/batch/src/measure.ts`, in the README). Script games in which every king is
   shadowed by a model king that sleeps: the script plays, so the games are the script games, and the model king's
   request for the very same view is built, split into its parts and counted; its record is written with notes, a
   summary and ledger lines of the lengths model kings write (the "realistic" variant of the simulation-fixes round).
   No model is called. Tokens are characters / 3.6. `--per-audience` holds courts one audience at a time, as before;
   the same file run on `develop` as it was gives "before". It also counts the reply schemas a game sends (each is a
   cached prefix of its own) and what each envoy brought.
2. **Compaction**, chosen from the breakdown below: the last season's notes in full and the two before folded to their
   first 400 characters; a tablet read in full at earlier orders shown by its first 120 characters, and at a court only
   the tablets bearing an envoy's court's seal in full; a letter still away in full only at the next orders after it
   was sent, then by its opening words, and in full again when the report comes; battle reports without the units that
   held; the explanations repeated in the view's headings moved to the rulebook's cached part ("Your view, part by
   part" under WHAT YOU SEE).
3. **Requests ordered so the stable part is cached.** A provider's cached prefix begins with the tools, and the reply
   schema is the request's one tool, so an audience schema that asked only what applied to its exchange made a prefix
   of its own for each variant. The court's schema is now the same at every court of a game (two variants: the king's
   orders standing or not), its fields saying when they apply ("Only if this envoy brought a tablet ..."); the parser
   still ignores what was not asked.
4. **One call per wake-up.** A model or file king answers every envoy at its court in one call, one answer per envoy
   by his id: one call for the first exchange of every audience, one for the second of those still open. Each answer is
   logged under its own audience's key, so replays, authorship and the message trail read as before; an envoy the reply
   leaves out is answered by the stand-in alone. Script and human seats answer audience by audience, as before, so
   script games are unchanged. The orders call stays its own.
5. **The rest of scheduled, conditional orders.** Conditions on scheduled entries and standing orders: troops of a
   power in a province (where the seat can see them), the treasury below or at least a sum, one of its people back at
   court and ready, a tablet sealed with a court; each an "if" or an "unless", at most 4, read from the seat's own view
   as the season begins. Held back, scheduled orders say so in the news and wake nobody; a standing order whose
   conditions fail does not fire and still stands. The entries given for one season now stand together, each with its
   own conditions (the last entry for a season used to win).
6. **Standing answers and phrasing calls**, for kings: given in the orders or court reply, at most 8, for envoys of a
   court (or any) who bring anything, only words, gold (at least a sum stated) or a tablet; they seal or not, send back
   a share of the gold, and give the gist of the words. The engine answers a covered envoy in one exchange, for nothing,
   and he wakes nobody; the king reads what was done in its news. Envoys who show evidence, or secretly serve the host,
   are never covered; a hidden backstop keeps what they send back in a season to a quarter of the treasury. The words
   are a phrasing call's, on the characters' model (or the king's own when characters play by script), from the
   clauses decided and the king's identity, without its record; a file seat, or a failed call, has the clauses read out.
7. **The output side.** Each job's cap on output in one table (`OutputCaps`). An envoy may pass his lord's message on
   as written and report the king's answer as given, writing nothing but that choice (`as_written`, `as_given`). No
   reply carries a free-text reasoning field (audited: the king's notes are its memory, an envoy's commitments are its
   promises).
8. **Script kings plan ahead when they sleep** (`--sleeping-scripts`; they play every season otherwise and schedule
   nothing): each envoy sent now is sent again, on the condition that he is back at court; a levy is repeated while the
   treasury holds twice its cost and unless the king's own troops stand there.
9. The setup screen's **cost estimate** rebased on the measured rates and sizes; **save version 9**.

The cached part grew: the rulebook by 830 tokens (CONDITIONS, STANDING ANSWERS, "Your view, part by part") and the
orders schema by 1,240 (conditions and standing answers), the court schema by 800; read from cache at a tenth of the
price, that is far less than the fresh tokens saved, and the court's prefixes, each written once at 1.25 times, fell
from five a game to two. Real model kings write longer tablets and letters than script kings, so the folding saves
more in model games. The output side cannot be measured without models: what `as_written`, `as_given` and the folded
notes save is measured in the model games after the build order.

What the 1,481 envoys received brought, the share a king could leave to standing answers: a tablet to seal 53%, only
words 30%, gold and no tablet 14%, evidence (never covered) 3%. Script kings give no standing answers, so their use is
measured in model games.

## Benchmarks and the script games

- **50 script games**, seeds 1-50, `--verify-replay`: 50 of 50 replay exactly, and every result is the same as before
  the item (wins, growth, the median game of 42 seasons, capitals fallen in 44 games, standing orders fired 100 times):
  script kings play every season, answer audience by audience and schedule nothing, so nothing they do changed.
- **`--versus solo`**, 40 seeds: unchanged (all kings 8.26 alone and 1.25 as baseline; 94% and 14% of wins).
- **Sleeping script kings** (`--sleeping-scripts`, 50 games, replays exact): with planning ahead, capitals fell in 34
  games (25 without), 47 kingdoms in all (39); wins by power within two games of before.

## Script players and the new features (the tenet "Every feature is every seat's")

- **Conditional orders.** Script kings that play every season have no use for them (they decide afresh each season, and
  cancel a standing order that no longer fits); sleeping script kings use them. In 20 sleeping-script games, 1,018 of
  2,675 dispatches (38%) were envoys sent again while their king slept, on the condition that each was back at court;
  repeated levies ran 7 times and were held back by the treasury condition most of the time (2,899 held-back notices,
  3,662 orders, nearly all of them envoys still away). **Using it against ignoring it**, on the same seeds
  (`--sleeping-scripts --plan-ahead-half`, 80 games, half the kings planning ahead, alternating by seed): mean growth
  2.63 against 2.34, wins 41 against 39. It helps, a little; Hatti did worse planning ahead (2.54 against 3.96), the
  others as well or better. Not tuned (first guesses).
- **Standing answers, phrasing calls, one call per court and the output side** are for model and file seats: script
  seats make no calls and need none (the design document says so of standing answers).

## First guesses (not tuned; item 18 tunes them)

- Notes older than the last season's folded to 400 characters; a tablet or letter read before shown by 120.
- At most 4 conditions on an entry, a standing order or an answer.
- At most 8 standing answers; their gist at most 300 characters; the hidden backstop a quarter of the treasury a season.
- Output caps: orders 16,000; a court 6,000 and 2,000 more for each further envoy, at most 16,000; a phrasing call 600;
  character calls 4,000 (unchanged).
- Script kings that sleep: an envoy sent again from the second to the seventh season ahead; a levy repeated for two
  seasons while the treasury holds twice its cost.
- The cost estimate: orders in 3 seasons of 10 (4,500 fresh and 16,000 cached tokens, 3,000 out), a court in 1 of 5
  (3,500 fresh, 14,000 cached, 1,000 out). For 44 seasons: about $0.50 (Budget), $5.60 (Standard), $10.50 (Showcase).

## The review

One reviewer (Opus, high effort) found two blocking problems, both fixed before the merge: an envoy reporting "as
given" had the engine's record of the audience written into his report, his own kept gold and gifts taken among it
(now only the king's words); and an envoy whose first covering answer passed the backstop fell through to a later
answer (now the king receives him). Also fixed from the review: the envoy's request still said "in your own words"
beside the as-written choice; the rulebook said conditions are read as the season begins, where an order's are read
once the audiences are over; a court nobody knows in a condition read as "any court" (now refused); with sleeping
seats off, letters were never folded.

## Readings taken and review questions

1. **One call per wake-up, read as one call per exchange.** An audience has two exchanges and the envoy answers in
   between, so a court is one call for the first exchange of every envoy and one for the second of those still open,
   not one call in all. The orders call stays its own ("Reopening orders still runs the orders call"), also when an
   order trigger woke the king in the same season as its envoys.
2. **"Since you last acted"** is not built as a view of changes only: the request holds no earlier copy of the map for
   a model to read changes against, so the whole picture stays, shortened (battle reports without the units that held,
   headings without the rulebook's explanations). The part that grows with sleep, the digest of slept seasons, is the
   part shortened.
3. **Folding is the engine's cut, not a summary.** Older notes, tablets read before and older letters are shown by their
   opening words; the king's own summary and ledger stay the place for what matters (the design: "the ledger keeping
   what was promised or threatened"). The rulebook and the notes field say so ("put what matters most first"). A
   summary by a cheaper model waits for "A cheaper model for condensing" (after the build order).
4. **The court's schema asks every field of every envoy**, each saying when it applies, against "ask only what
   applies", so that the fixed part of the request stays cached; the parser still drops what does not apply. Is that the
   right trade?
5. **Entries of one season stand together** (a rule change, in the rulebook): needed so that orders of one season can
   carry different conditions (two envoys, each sent again "if he is back").
6. **Known where it runs** is the seat's own view: for a standing answer as its court begins, for an order once the
   season's audiences are over (what was given and sealed there counts). Troops it cannot see count as not there; the
   treasury is its own ledger. Item 13 makes this local to where the order runs.
7. **Standing answers' vocabulary** is small: whose envoy and what he brings, seal or not, a share of the gold back, the
   gist of the words; always one exchange. An envoy with evidence, or secretly the host's, is never covered. The match
   uses the gold his lord sent, as the sealed inventory states it (an envoy who skims and swaps the inventory is still
   covered by it); the gold sent back is the share of what the weighers found. The first answer that covers an envoy is
   the one: if its gold would pass the hidden backstop (a first guess), the king receives him.
8. **Standing answers for characters are not built.** The design's examples ("turn down any bribe below a sum", "take
   any carrying job ... that pays above one") need carrying jobs, which come with item 11; and a bribe answered in
   advance saves no call, since the envoy still answers the king's words in the same exchange. Built with item 11, or
   should 6c add the bribe answer?
9. **A human seat's standing answers** have no screen yet: a human answers every audience itself, so they would save
   clicks, not calls. The web app's court screen is the place; not built in 6b.
10. **The phrasing model** is the characters' model, or the king's own when characters play by script, until item 6c
    makes it a job of the setup menu. A file seat's answers are its clauses read out (there is no small model behind a
    file seat).
11. **Script kings plan ahead only when they sleep** (`--sleeping-scripts`), the only case where scheduled orders mean
    anything to a script.
12. **The cost estimate** now assumes sleeping kings (orders in three seasons in ten); a table with sleeping seats off
    pays more than it says.
13. `docs/design/05-pacing-sleeping-seats.md` was already over 50 KB (56 KB) before this item and is now 60 KB: it
    should be split in its own pull request, with nothing else changed. `src/llm/src/rules.ts` (the rulebook) is at 49 KB.
