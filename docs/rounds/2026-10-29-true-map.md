## What was built

1. **The board in a true projection.** Mercator over 25.8°E-51.2°E and 20°N-42.3°N (`src/web/src/map/projection.ts`),
   each province at its town's latitude and longitude, each sea zone at a spot on its waters. Coasts, islands, the lakes of
   1346, the great rivers and the deserts come from Natural Earth (public domain), clipped and simplified by
   `scripts/map-geography.ts` into `geo-data.ts` (30 KB), finer on the Levant; the smaller rivers, the ranges, the black
   land and the alluvium are drawn by hand in latitude and longitude. The engine's provinces lost their board
   coordinates (`Province.x`, `y`): the drawing is the web app's alone, and the engine's lint forbids the logarithms a
   projection needs. Distances, routes and connections did not change with the drawing (they come from latitude,
   longitude and the attested roads); the map test of connections passed unchanged before part 3.
2. **The Levant on a phone, by zooming.** Names give way by importance at ten zoom steps, each name shown from the step
   where it touches no more important one (`NameLevels`); the zoom reaches 24 times the whole board (14 before); the map
   opens on the seat's own capital whenever a seat's eyes are taken. Checked at 390 pixels wide: the whole board, then the
   Levant at about 3x and 9x, where Byblos, Sidon, Tyre, Akko, Hazor, Megiddo and Damascus each stand clear with their
   names; a phone seated as Egypt opens on the Nile from the Delta to Nubia, with the Red Sea beside it.
3. **The Red Sea.** One zone (`redsea`), the waters of the Sinai and Thebes, which became coasts; desert roads to its
   shore (Thebes by the Wadi Hammamat, 210 km; the Sinai to the Gulf of Suez, 200 km, then the voyage down to Mersa
   Gawasis, 10 days); joined to no other sea, so only ships raised on its shore sail it (the rulebook's THE SEA says so of
   the Gulf too, which is true of it). Punt is a door: 5 in a hundred arrivals, the dossier's share, the other doors back to
   its 35, 25, 25 and 10.
4. **Six cities of Canaan** as city-states, each Egypt's vassal on a sealed tablet at the start: Akko (Surata), Hazor
   (Abdi-Tirshi), Gezer (Milkilu), Ashkelon (Yidya), Lachish (Zimreddi), the Hebron Hills (Shuwardata). 39 powers with item 11d's three, 61 land
   provinces, 18 sea zones.

## Readings taken

Each with the principle it rests on ("Reason from the pillars").

1. **The projection is Mercator,** north up everywhere and coasts in their true shapes, as Imperator's map reads; at these
   latitudes it differs from an equal-distance projection by about a tenth at the edges, which no rule reads. (The
   drawing serves the eye; distances are the engine's.)
2. **The Gulf's head is drawn reaching up towards Ur,** as it lay in the Late Bronze Age, since Ur is a coast in the game.
   (The period first; the map tells the truth of 1346.)
3. **The engine keeps no board coordinates.** The drawing is presentation and lives with it. (One fact, one place.)
4. **Thebes and the Sinai became coasts** whose waters are the Red Sea, as "touched by the provinces whose land reaches
   its shore" asks. What follows from being a coast follows here too, by the same rules: a harbour (trade), a fleet raised
   there, ships' grain; and a besieged Thebes or Sinai would count as supplied by sea unless the besieger held the Red
   Sea, which only a fleet raised on its shore can (neither has walls at the start, so no siege of them is likely soon).
   (Different data, same rules.)
5. **The voyage between the Red Sea's shores is charged** (from the review): the empty zone would otherwise join its two
   shores for nothing, and Upper Egypt's envoys would have left the Nile. With the voyage the Nile is the way from Thebes
   to the Delta and the Levant, and only the Sinai is nearer by the Red Sea, as the mining expeditions went. (Everything
   travels at the pace of the roads.)
6. **"Ships carried overland" is not built here:** the item names it for how a fleet reaches the Red Sea, and the section
   itself says it comes with shipbuilding in the economy's later phases. Until then a fleet reaches the Red Sea only by
   being raised on its shore. (No stopgaps.)
7. **Each new city yields 10 and the land it came from gives 5** (Megiddo for Akko, Damascus for Hazor, Shechem for
   Gezer, Gaza for Ashkelon, Jerusalem for the Hebron Hills, and Lachish from Gaza's and Jerusalem's land, each of those
   already giving 5). Canaan's yield rises from 100 to 135 a season, Egypt's own land from 928 to 913 shekels' worth (Gaza),
   and the five older cities' starting armies shrink by 250 men each (50 a shekel). A city-state below 10 would field 250
   men; carving the old totals exactly would leave the new cities at 5. First guesses for item 18. (Balance at the end;
   the period first.)
8. **The southern Levant's people** (70,000, the people dossier) are shared among all ten of its provinces by harvest,
   Hazor among them (a town of 12,000, the largest in Canaan); the Habiru's share (10 in a hundred loose) covers the six.
9. **Connections:** the road from Megiddo to Damascus now runs by Hazor (the dossier's "by Hazor", its 160 km split 60
   and 100), and the shore from Tyre to Gaza by Akko and Ashkelon, each with waters of its own; Gaza–Megiddo and
   Tyre–Megiddo are kept, the inland roads. Megiddo to Gaza is now quickest by Akko and the sea (5.5 days against 6.4 by
   the road). (Everything is somewhere; minimal change to what was.)
10. **Shuwardata's seat is Hebron** (the dossier's "Hebron hills"; Gath is the other candidate), named the Hebron Hills,
    rough ground. Waters: Gezer a spring (its Middle Bronze water tunnel), Akko, Ashkelon and Lachish wells, Hazor outside
    its walls, the Hebron Hills cisterns [G].
11. **Walls by the walls dossier,** as item 11d (merged while this was built) set every other city-state's: Hazor 3 for
    its great Middle Bronze rampart, still in use (as Qatna's and Qadesh's); the five the dossier does not list (Akko,
    Gezer, Ashkelon, Lachish, the Hebron Hills) 1, as 11d read the towns it knows nothing of. (Different data,
    same rules: walls from the city.)
12. **Par** for the six is unmeasured (1) until item 18 measures it with the rest; the older five's par was measured on
    their larger start.

## First guesses (item 18)

The six cities' yields (10 each) and the five parents' (each 5 less); Hazor's town 12 thousand and the others 1-3; the
Red Sea roads (210 and 200 km) and the voyage (10 days); the Punt door's 5 in a hundred.

## Script players

The script kings and characters needed no change to play the new map: the six cities are city-states like the
twenty-three before them (their vassal tablets, tribute and judgements are the same code), and the Red Sea is a sea zone
like the Gulf. In the 50 script games below every one of the six survived in most games (Hazor in 48, Ashkelon 45, Akko
41, the Hebron Hills 37, Lachish 34, Gezer 28) and each was won once or not at all, as most city-states are; tribute was
paid as by the other vassals. Nobody raised a fleet on the Red Sea, rightly: there is nothing on it to win, and no rival
to meet. No newcomer came through any door in either run (the pool did not fall below its start), so the Punt door was
not exercised by script games; its draw is covered by the door's share in the arrivals' table and the map test.
