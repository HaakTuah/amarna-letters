# Round report: Build order item 5, the siege package

Item 5 of the Build order (pillar 8, "war slower than diplomacy"), built on branch `item/sieges` from `develop` after the
fix pass. The specification is the item's text in the live design document (main tab "Tempo of war", "Walls and
terrain", "A kingdom falls with its capital", "A fallen player's game is over", read on 20 October 2026) and question 5
of `docs/rounds/2026-10-18-sleep-and-simulation.md`, with its terrain lists. The whole item is one save version, 6. No
model games were played and no model calls made: script games and stand-in repliers only. Each agent that built a part
of the item adds its own section below.

## Walls, sieges and capitals

Agent W's part, built first: walls that differ by place and can be strengthened; storming and besieging, with the
resolution rules question 5 plans (sallies among them); envoys leaving or coming home to a besieged capital; the battle
report line for an assault on a king's own walled city; a kingdom falling with its capital, its land passing to the
conqueror; slow absorption of conquered land; a fallen player's game being over, with god view as a table setting; and
the retirement of the temporary wake trigger for troops next to the capital. Terrain, deserts and islands are not in this
part. Each piece is its own commit, with its rule text in the rulebook and the seat view, its schema, its orders panel
control where it is an order, `docs/DESIGN-NOTES.md` and its tests (DESIGN-NOTES, "Walls and sieges" under Rules as
implemented, has every rule in full).

### What was built

- **Walls by place** (`66fb4cf`). Every land province has a wall level in the map data (`MapData.StartWalls`): the seven
  capitals 3; the fortresses Carchemish, Qadesh, Tyre, Megiddo, Gaza, Mari, Susa, Memphis, Kanesh and Nineveh 2; the
  walled towns Lukka, Isuwa, Ugarit, Amurru, Byblos and Damascus 1; the rest none. The walls standing now are game state
  (`GameState.Walls`), public, in every view ("mari (walls 2)") and on the map (a ring for each level). They replace the
  free cities' garrisons, the pretend units that held with strength 1.
- **Storming** (same commit). Walls add nothing to a battle. A move that wins its way into a walled province its mover
  does not hold takes it at once only if its strength also beats the walls' level plus the hold strength of the defenders
  it beat (`Assault`); otherwise the unit stands inside and takes nothing. Hattusa held by one army needs 5; an empty city
  with walls 1 needs 2, as before. A fleet never storms or besieges. A city taken loses a level of walls. A foreign unit
  within walls without a siege is a guest. Owners may enter their own walled provinces (until now they could not enter a
  bought city).
- **Besieging** (same commit). An army within walls it does not hold may besiege the city (`BesiegeOrder`, schema
  "besiege", "Besiege the city" in the orders panel). Each season it holds there wears the city down a step, two with an
  uncut support to hold; one order is enough; the city surrenders when worn down as far as its walls are high, a level
  lower. The siege is lifted, its progress lost, if the army leaves or is destroyed. A besieged city yields nothing, and
  each siege costs its besieger 15 a season. Kings read a "Sieges" notice each season, and a SIEGES line in their view.
- **The battle report line for an assault on the king's own walls** (same commit; the fix pass, minor finding 5): among
  his own lines, with his side and the strengths: "- Your walled city of Mari: Mitanni's army from terqa got within the
  walls but did not storm them (its strength 1 against your walls 2 and defenders 0): it stands inside, and Mari stays
  yours unless a siege wears it down." The attacker's own line says the same from his side.
- **A kingdom falls with its capital** (`ac24f74`), replacing the fall when no land is left: after the moves, a king
  whose capital another power holds has fallen; his remaining land passes to that power, his troops disband, his sieges
  are lifted, his debts die, and none of his later orders that season runs. A king cannot cede his own capital. The game
  records each fall (`GameState.Falls`).
- **The pace of war in the batch report** (`454676b`): capitals fallen per game, and the seasons from the conqueror's
  first entry into the fallen king's land (and anyone's) to the fall (`GameState.Invaded`, `BatchRunner.WarReport`).
- **Slow absorption** (`24b8b9b`): land taken by force yields, and counts in strength, a third in its first season, two
  thirds in the second, all of it from the third; a king's own homeland retaken, and land bought or ceded, count in full.
- **Strengthening walls** (`aeeb933`, refined in `e613482`): the fortify order raises a province's walls a level at the
  end of the season, up to 3, for 50 × the new level; levels above the map's cost 5 a season, and walls whose upkeep
  cannot be paid fall a level. Not while foreign troops stand within. An earthquake throws a level down.
- **Envoys and a besieged capital** (`088a86c`): an envoy whose courage the siege outweighs will not leave; one that goes,
  or comes home, may be caught by the siege's tightness and its skill, and becomes the besieger's captive, who reads the
  letter and mandate and takes the gold and tablets; he may execute, ransom or release it, not turn it. Its king hears
  only that it has not returned. Other kings' envoys pass.
- **Wake triggers** (`6ce4a3b`): the temporary trigger for troops next to the capital is retired; the capital, a walled
  province of the homeland, wakes the king as any home province does ("foreign troops came next to your homeland: ...,
  next to babylon, nippur, sippar"). Two triggers for the besieged are added: a siege of his city begun, and a city that
  would surrender at the end of the season if the besiegers just hold. The rulebook's sleep text says what troops next to
  a province can do now ("get into it the next season: take it if it has no walls, or stand within them and besiege it"),
  and no longer speaks of walls that stop a move.
- **A fallen player's game is over** (`57804ef`): once every human seat at the table has fallen, the players watch the
  rest in god view, a table setting on by default ("A fallen player watches in god view" when a game is started; carried
  in the log; `--fallen-view god|kingdom` in the batch runner); off, a fallen player keeps the view of what his kingdom
  knew when it fell. The web app says so when it happens, and in its rules tab.
- **Sallies** (`d1ae158`): while foreign troops stand within the walls of a city whose walls are 2 or higher, its holder
  may order a sally (schema "sally", "Sally from the walls of…" in the orders panel): the walls support one attack into the
  city on them, anyone's, for +1, a support that cannot be cut.

### Readings taken

1. **A strong enough move into anyone's walled city storms it, an ally's included.** No rule may check who is a friend
   ("No alliance flags"), so a move strong enough to storm takes the city whoever holds it; a guest is a unit that got in
   without storming. A relief army that should not take a friend's city goes in with no more strength than it needs. The
   rulebook says so plainly. Review question 1.
2. **Slow absorption counts in the score too.** Question 5 kept the score unchanged; the design document's later words,
   "a conqueror gains an empire to hold, not a lump of score", read as the score following the yield. Review question 2.
3. **A captured envoy cannot be turned.** Question 5 lets the besieger "ransom, turn, execute or release" it; a turned
   captive goes back to its post as a double agent, and an envoy caught at its own king's city has no post to go back to.
   Execute, ransom and release are built; turning is refused with the reason. Review question 3.
4. **"Besieged" for envoys means a siege under way at the king's own capital**, not a foreign army merely standing within
   its walls (a guest may be an ally). Only the besieged king's own envoys are caught; his spies are not (question 5 names
   envoys).
5. **The sally needs foreign troops within the walls**, besieging or not: a sally against an army that got in and has
   not yet besieged is allowed, since it is the same army in the same place.
6. **No walls are raised with foreign troops within them** (refused when given, failed if they got in that season),
   besides question 5's "not while besieged".
7. **A kingdom's whole army disbands when it falls**: the design document leaves the armies to their commanders once
   commanders exist, and until then "An army with nobody to command it disbands". If two kings take each other's capitals
   in one season, the land passing to a king who also falls is left free (the case needs two capitals taken in one season).
8. **The fallen player's god view, on a shared screen**, opens only when every human seat at the table has fallen: one
   screen cannot show one player what another still playing must not see. With the setting off, the fallen player sees
   what his kingdom knew when it fell and what is public since (who holds what, which kingdoms fell), as the seat view
   of a fallen court already did; nothing his court could no longer learn.
9. **Of question 5's five siege triggers, two are built** (a siege of the king's city begun; the city about to
   surrender), both for the besieged, as default triggers the engine judges to concern him. The besieger is woken by
   attacks on his army as any king is; a caught envoy is news that travels (the overdue rumour), and the captor decides its
   fate when next he gives orders, as for a caught spy.
10. **The walls' upkeep is paid after the troops'**, and walls that cannot be kept fall a level, the farthest from the
    capital first; the upkeep is shown beside the troops' ("upkeep 120, walls 10").
11. **A city that falls keeps its walls a level lower**, whether stormed or surrendered (question 5 named the siege;
    "a city that falls loses one wall level" in its stalemate notes reads for both).
12. **The baseline bot** (frozen, `BaselineLeader`) is adapted only where the rules made it wrong: it no longer supports
    walls holding, and an army of its standing within foreign walls it attacked besieges them, as its supported attack once
    tore a free city's walls down.

### The pace of war

Measured in 100 script games (seeds 1-100), at the base of the item and after this part. "Entry" before the item is the
captor's first conquest of the victim's land, since every move into land then took it (`before-wars.js` in the
scratchpad reads the record of conquests); after it, the conqueror's first move into the victim's land
(`BatchRunner.WarReport`), and the same conquest measure for comparison.

| | Before (base of item 5) | After |
|---|---|---|
| Games in which a capital fell (before: was taken) | 92 | 63 (67 in seeds 101-200) |
| Capitals taken / kingdoms fallen | 198 taken, 81 fallen (no land left) | 96 fallen, each with its capital |
| First capital of a game, season (median) | 10 (range 3-21) | 14 (range 8-28) |
| Seasons from first entry to the capital (median) | 4 (1-18) | 10 (3-28); 9 in seeds 101-200 |
| Within 5-10 seasons | 38% | 61% (63% in seeds 101-200) |
| Within 2 seasons | 36% | 4% (conquest measure) |

The aim was about 5 to 10 seasons from the first hostile entry to a capital's fall, with capitals still falling in most
games: the median is 9 to 10 seasons, and a capital falls in about two games in three. Nothing made games unplayable for
testing: no capital fell sooner than three seasons after its conqueror first got into the kingdom (four in seeds
101-200). The ending rule is unchanged
(no end before turn 15, then 15% a season), so the first fall comes at a median of season 14, near the earliest end:
item 18 sets game length.

Who falls: Mitanni 33, Assyria 25, Arzawa 30, Hatti 5, Babylonia 1, Egypt 1, Alashiya 1 (seeds 1-100). Alashiya's island
capital is reached only by armies carried by sea (fleets cannot storm or besiege walls), and Egypt's lies far from every
army; terrain, deserts and islands, still to be built, add to this.

Weaknesses found and patched, with the benchmarks before and after each:

- **Wall guards that never fired** (the fix pass, minor finding 5): its walls guard had troops support walls holding,
  which walls no longer do. A walled city of ours is now guarded as empty land is: a troop next to it marches in as the
  enemy does, so a lone attacker bounces. 28 such standing orders fired in seeds 1-100.
- **Besiegers that forgot the sally.** With sallies built but pictured only for its own moves, the look-ahead did not see
  a lone relief army with a sally drive its besieger out; capitals fell in 53 of 100 games. Picturing every king's sallies
  in the look-ahead (the besieger then supports its army to hold, which also doubles the siege's pace) raised that to 64.
- **Walls ordered where they could not stand**: the first version fortified any threatened province, nearly five orders a
  game failing because the province was taken that season (walls rise after the moves). Script kings now fortify only
  walled provinces or ones they stand in, and with no foreign troops within: 3.5 failures a game remain, where a troop was
  dislodged or walls stormed, and they cost nothing.
- **Gifts and tribute out of a besieged capital**: the gold would go with a caught envoy. A besieged king sends none, and
  does not ask an envoy who would not leave to go again for two seasons.
- **Turning a caught envoy** would be refused: a cunning captor ransoms it instead.

Found, not patched (left for the next agent's part or the audit):

- **Fewer gifts, bribes and skims.** Bribes 99 to 46, skims 298 to 182, debasement found 22 to 1 (seeds 1-100), and
  likewise in seeds 101-200. Longer wars, siege costs and walls leave less spare gold for the court. A run with script
  kings that never fortify (seeds 1-100: bribes 72, skims 222, debasement found 9; capitals fell in 52 games) shows walls
  take part of it. Fortifying itself plays neither better nor worse in the solo benchmark (growth alone 3.10 with it,
  3.13 without), so it stays.
- **The weak kingdoms win less**: Arzawa 11 to 2 wins (13 to 5 in seeds 101-200), and Arzawa falls in 30 games. A
  kingdom of three provinces falls with its capital; its walls of 3 buy seasons, not safety. Balance waits for item 18.
- The items the fix pass left to item 5's audit and not part of this one: fleet retreats that never succeed, Mitanni's
  numbers, script kings acting on an envoy's word that a host tried to buy it.

### Review questions

1. **Storming a friend's city.** A move strong enough to storm takes any walled city, an ally's included, since no rule
   knows who is a friend. Accept, or let a holder name kings his walls open to?
2. **Slow absorption in the score.** Built as the design document's "not a lump of score" (the score follows the yield);
   question 5 had left the score unchanged.
3. **A caught envoy cannot be turned** (question 5 allowed it). Accept, or let a turned envoy go on to its destination as
   the captor's secret servant?
4. **Capitals fall in two games in three** (63 and 67 of 100), against 92 before; the median from first entry is 9 to 10
   seasons. Is two in three "most games"? The review the item names ("a first look at how long wars and games now run")
   is for this.
5. **The weak kingdoms win much less** (Arzawa 11 → 2 and 13 → 5), and fall often: is a kingdom of three provinces
   falling with its capital what the owner wants (the design document asks the review to check it)?
6. **Less gold for the court** in longer wars: bribes, skims and debasement found fell by half or more. For item 18, or
   a script-king audit?
7. **Two siege triggers of question 5's five.** Enough, or should a besieger be woken when foreign troops come next to
   its besieging army?
