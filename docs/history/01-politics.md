# 1. Politics: who ruled where in 1346 BCE

For "Opening relations as they were", the opening positions (item 7) and city-states as powers (item 11+). Grades and
abbreviations: [README](README.md). <a id="01-year-1346"></a>Year 1346 is about Akhenaten's year 8 ("One chronology" in the
README; HKW, chronological table, p. 492: Akhenaten "1353-1336").

**In one line:** in 1346 Egypt holds Canaan and the coast as far as Ugarit through vassals and three administrative
centres; Mitanni still holds northern Syria and the Euphrates crossing, but is split by a rival king and about to be
broken by Suppiluliuma; Hatti has recovered from near collapse and is pushing south and east; Babylonia and a newly
independent Assyria quarrel over Assyria's standing; Arzawa has passed its peak; Alashiya trades copper with all.

## Each realm in 1346

- **Egypt.** The court has moved to Akhetaten (founded year 5) [A: boundary stelae]. Egypt rules Canaan and the Levantine
  coast through vassal "mayors" (*hazannu*) who swear loyalty and report, and through three administrative centres
  with Egyptian commissioners (*rabisu*): **Gaza**, **Sumur** on Amurru's coast, and **Kumidi** in the Beqaa [B: Na'aman;
  Moran]. Egyptian garrisons are small: vassals ask for tens of men, Egyptian and Nubian (Meluhha) (EA 70, EA 108,
  Rainey: "twenty men from the country of Meluhha (and) twenty men of the land of Egypt", EA 117), and Kushite troops
  serve in Jerusalem (EA 287) [A]. Nubia is ruled directly by the viceroy, the King's Son of Kush: a year-12 stela at
  Buhen names a viceroy whose name is lost, probably Thutmose, the only viceroy known under Akhenaten [B: H. S. Smith,
  *The Fortress of Buhen: The Inscriptions* (1976)]. Year 12 (about 1342) brings a great reception of foreign tribute
  at Akhetaten, shown in the tombs of Meryra II and Huya [A].
- **Hatti.** Under Suppiluliuma's father (Tudhaliya III) Hatti nearly fell: the Kaska and other enemies sacked the
  Hatti lands, Arzawa "made Tuwanuwa and Uda his frontier", and "Hattusa, the city, was burned down" (Hattusili III's
  decree, KBo VI 28, as in Bryce, Letters, p. 171) [A; the reign B: Bryce]; EA 31 has Amenhotep III saying "the
  country Hattusa is shattered" (Moran) [A]. Suppiluliuma, as prince and king, won it back, and in 1346 he is turning to
  Syria. His "one-year campaign" (the Great Syrian War) crossed the Euphrates, took Isuwa, sacked Washukanni and took
  Mitanni's Syrian vassals (Aleppo, Mukish, Nuhashshe, Qatna, Qadesh) [A: Shattiwaza treaty; Bryce, Letters, p. 28:
  "occupied and plundered the Mitannian capital Washshuganni"]. Its date is disputed, about 1344
  to 1340 [C]; the game starts just before it. Kizzuwatna (Cilicia) is Hittite by now [B].
- <a id="01-mitanni"></a>**Mitanni.** Tushratta holds the Habur heartland (Washukanni, Taidu) and the Euphrates crossing at Carchemish, with
  vassals in north Syria (Aleppo, Mukish/Alalakh, Nuhashshe, Qadesh for a time) and east of the Tigris (Arrapha, with
  Nuzi) [B]. Luckenbill 1921, p. 164 (6A): "overpowered Halpa and Mukishhe". He is Egypt's ally by a marriage alliance:
  his sister Gilukhepa (EA 17) and his daughter Tadukhepa (EA 22, EA 24) married Amenhotep III; Tadukhepa passed to
  Akhenaten, whose wife EA 27 and EA 28 call her [A]. These last letters (EA 27-29) are early in Akhenaten's reign: the
  docket on EA 27 reads "year 2" (or "year 12"), and Moran dates the Mitannian letters to "year 4-5" at the latest
  without a long co-regency, so about 1352-1349, some years before 1346 [B: Moran, Introduction, sect. 6]. A rival line
  (Artatama II, then Shuttarna III) courts Hatti and later Assyria [B]. Where Artatama II held is not recorded [S]: the
  treaty calls him "Artatama, the king of Harri" against "Tushratta, king of Mitanni" (Luckenbill 1921, p. 163). After
  Tushratta's death Shuttarna III took over Mitanni and stripped the palace at Washukanni, and Mitanni's nobles were
  crucified "in Taite" (Taidu) (Shattiwaza's version, 6B; Luckenbill 1921, p. 172) [A]. Within about fifteen years
  Mitanni is a Hittite puppet (Shattiwaza) [A].
- **Babylonia.** The Kassite kingdom from the Gulf to about Hit on the Euphrates, with Dilmun under a Babylonian
  governor (the Dilmun letters from Nippur, Ili-ippashra to Enlil-kidinni) [B]. Burna-Buriash has a daughter promised
  to Akhenaten, oil poured on her head (EA 11, which complains of an escort of only five chariots) [A]. He calls
  Assyria his vassal (EA 9: "as for the Assyrian, my vassal", Rainey) [A], though EA 9 is addressed to "Nibhurrereya"
  (Rainey's reading), which may be Tutankhamun, so after 1346 [C]; he will
  marry (he or one of his sons: C) Ashur-uballit's daughter Muballitat-Sherua, whose son Kara-hardash is murdered in a revolt that Assyria
  avenges [A: Chronicle P, Synchronistic History; after 1346].
- **Assyria.** Ashur-uballit I has thrown off Mitanni's overlordship [B] and writes his first letter to Egypt (EA 15)
  so his messenger "should see what you are like and what your country is like" (Moran), then a second (EA 16)
  calling himself Great King and Pharaoh's brother [A]. Moran dates both letters "late in the reign of Amenophis IV,
  if not later" (Introduction, sect. 6), so in 1346 they may still be to come [C]. His land is the Tigris triangle:
  Ashur, Nineveh, Arbela [B]. He later takes part of eastern Mitanni [B].
- <a id="01-arzawa-lands"></a>**Arzawa.** The great power of western Anatolia in Amenhotep III's day: he sought a daughter of Tarhundaradu for his
  harem, and their letters are in Hittite (EA 31-32) [A]. Its capital was Apasa (later Ephesus) [B]. By 1346 Hatti is
  recovering at its expense; Mira, the Seha River Land, Hapalla and Wilusa are lands of the "Arzawa lands", each with
  its own ruler, more allied than ruled [B: Bryce, Kingdom].
- **Alashiya.** Cyprus, with a king who calls Pharaoh "brother" (EA 33-39) and pays his way in copper [A]. Where his
  seat was is disputed: Enkomi is the largest harbour town, but the clay of the Alashiya letters comes from the
  southern foothills of the Troodos, near Alassa and Kalavasos [C: Goren et al. 2003]. A royal official, the
  "governor of Alashiya", also wrote to his Egyptian counterpart (EA 40) [A].

**Lands with no king** (start lawless or unruled, as "The edges of the map" and "People of the land" say):

- <a id="01-lukka"></a>**Lukka** (`lukka`): the Lukka men raid Alashiya year by year (EA 38, Rainey: "year by year, are
  taking a small town in my land") [A]; no kingdom, later Hittite subjects in name.
- <a id="01-hukkana"></a>**Azzi-Hayasa** (`azzi`, item 11f): the highlands beyond Isuwa (around Erzincan [C]) under Hukkana, who swore a treaty
  with Suppiluliuma and married his sister (Beckman no. 3) [A]; when the treaty was sworn is not certain, early in the
  reign [C], so the game opens with it sealed: Hatti's vassal. A generation before, its men sacked the Upper Land and
  made Samuha their frontier (KBo VI 28, Bryce, Letters, p. 171: "the Azzian enemy came and sacked all the Upper Lands
  and he made Samuha his frontier") [A]; its strongholds Aripsa and Dukkamma are a generation later (Mursili II's
  annals) [A]. Its men's names: Mariya (in the treaty), Anniya (a later king) [A].
- <a id="01-samuha"></a>**Samuha** (`samuha`, item 11f): Hatti's own, in the Upper Land on the Marassantiya (Kayalıpınar near Sivas,
  an identification its tablet finds have made secure [B: O. Soysal, *ZA* 2019]), the royal residence and army base
  where Tudhaliya III held court while Hattusa lay burned [B: Bryce, Letters, p. 28: "Tudhaliya was forced to flee to
  a city called Samuha"].
- <a id="01-libya"></a>**Libya** (`libya`, item 11f): the Tjehenu and Tjemehu west of the Delta, herders and raiders in Egypt's 18th-Dynasty
  reliefs [A]; no king, no town; the names Didi and Kaper are chiefs Egypt named a century and more later [A, later].
- <a id="01-shasu"></a>**The Shasu** (`shasu`, item 11f): herders of the Negev and Edom, in Amenhotep III's lists at Soleb ("the land of the
  Shasu") [A]; Seti I fought them on the Ways of Horus a generation later [A]. No king, no town; their names do not
  survive [G for the style].
- <a id="01-isuwa"></a>**Isuwa** (`isuwa`): a buffer that changed sides between Hatti and Mitanni; Suppiluliuma takes it in the Great Syrian
  War [A: Shattiwaza treaty; Luckenbill 1921, p. 164: "Ishuwa in its totality I devastated"]. Ruled by its own kings
  [B]; start it neutral.
- <a id="01-hana"></a>**The middle Euphrates** (`mari`): by 1346 the land of Hana (Terqa its centre) under local kings, within Mitanni's
  reach [B]. Mari itself is a ruin since Hammurabi (about 1760) [A].
- <a id="01-elam"></a>**Susa** (`susa`): Elam under the dynasty of the Igihalkids [B], unconnected with the letters. A generation before
  1346 a Kassite Kurigalzu took Susa [C: Kurigalzu I or II]; whether Babylonia still holds it in 1346 is unknown [C]. Start neutral, as now.
- <a id="01-kaska-and-suteans"></a>**Kaska lands, Sutean steppe** (item 7): tribes without kings ("The edges of the map").
- **Habiru** (*'apiru*): not a people but outcasts, runaways and hired fighters, everywhere in the vassals' letters [B].

## Design questions

1. **Labaya in 1346.** The design names Shechem "under Labaya". He was probably dead by 1346, his sons ruling. Reading
   taken: item 7 started Shechem under **Mut-Baal, a son of Labaya**, with Labaya's quarrel with Abdi-Heba inherited
   ("the sons of Labaya", EA 287, 289); since item 11f Labaya himself starts at Shechem, a stretch of some years, and
   Mut-Baal rules Pella (the Pella row).
2. **Suppiluliuma or his predecessor.** Starting with Suppiluliuma is the reading taken.
3. **Ugarit's lord.** It starts in Egypt's sphere (it wrote to Pharaoh as a servant) with a Hittite treaty to come.
4. **Alashiya's capital.** Enkomi stays the capital as the design says; the clay evidence for the Alassa-Kalavasos
   area argues for naming the western province "Kalavasos" if it takes in the south ("Map review").
