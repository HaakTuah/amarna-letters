# 1. Politics: who ruled where in 1346 BCE

For "Opening relations as they were", the opening positions (item 7) and city-states as powers (item 11+). Grades and
abbreviations: [README](README.md). Year 1346 is about Akhenaten's year 8 ("One chronology" in the README).

**In one line:** in 1346 Egypt holds Canaan and the coast as far as Ugarit through vassals and three administrative
centres; Mitanni still holds northern Syria and the Euphrates crossing, but is split by a rival king and about to be
broken by Suppiluliuma; Hatti has recovered from near collapse and is pushing south and east; Babylonia and a newly
independent Assyria quarrel over Assyria's standing; Arzawa has passed its peak; Alashiya trades copper with all.

## Each realm in 1346

- **Egypt.** The court has moved to Akhetaten (founded year 5) [A: boundary stelae]. Egypt rules Canaan and the Levantine
  coast through vassal "mayors" (*hazannu*) who swear loyalty and report, and through three administrative centres
  with Egyptian commissioners (*rabisu*): **Gaza**, **Sumur** on Amurru's coast, and **Kumidi** in the Beqaa [B: Na'aman;
  Moran]. Egyptian garrisons are small: tens of men, often Nubian (Meluhha, Kashi) troops (EA 287, EA 70, EA 108, EA 117)
  [A]. Nubia is ruled directly by the viceroy, the King's Son of Kush (Thutmose under Akhenaten) [B]. Year 12 (about
  1342) brings a great reception of foreign tribute at Akhetaten, shown in the tombs of Meryra II and Huya [A].
- **Hatti.** Under Suppiluliuma's father (Tudhaliya III) Hatti nearly fell: the Kaska burned Hattusa and Arzawa reached
  the Salt Lake region ("the concealed land" text, KBo VI 28) [A]; EA 31 has Amenhotep III saying "the land of Hattusa
  is shattered" [A]. Suppiluliuma, as prince and king, won it back, and in 1346 he is turning to Syria. His "one-year
  campaign" (the Great Syrian War) crossed the Euphrates, took Isuwa, sacked Washukanni and took Mitanni's Syrian
  vassals (Aleppo, Mukish, Nuhashshe, Qatna, Qadesh) [A: Shattiwaza treaty; Deeds]. Its date is disputed, about 1344
  to 1340 [C]; the game starts just before it. Kizzuwatna (Cilicia) is Hittite by now [B].
- **Mitanni.** Tushratta holds the Habur heartland (Washukanni, Taidu) and the Euphrates crossing at Carchemish, with
  vassals in north Syria (Aleppo, Mukish/Alalakh, Nuhashshe, Qadesh for a time) and east of the Tigris (Arrapha, with
  Nuzi) [B]. He is Egypt's ally by a marriage alliance: his sister Gilukhepa and his daughter Tadukhepa married
  Amenhotep III; Tadukhepa passed to Akhenaten's harem (EA 27-29) [A]. A rival line (Artatama II, then Shuttarna III)
  courts Hatti and later Assyria [B]. Within about fifteen years Mitanni is a Hittite puppet (Shattiwaza) [A].
- **Babylonia.** The Kassite kingdom from the Gulf to about Hit on the Euphrates, with Dilmun under a Babylonian
  governor (the Dilmun letters from Nippur, Ili-ippashra to Enlil-kidinni) [B]. Burna-Buriash has a daughter married to
  Akhenaten's house (EA 11, the escort of only five carriages) [A]. He calls Assyria his vassal (EA 9) [A] and will
  marry Ashur-uballit's daughter Muballitat-Sherua, whose son Kara-hardash is murdered in a revolt that Assyria
  avenges [A: Chronicle P, Synchronistic History; after 1346].
- **Assyria.** Ashur-uballit I has thrown off Mitanni's overlordship [B] and writes his first letter to Egypt (EA 15)
  "to see what you are like and what your land is like", then a second (EA 16) calling himself Great King and
  Pharaoh's brother [A]. His land is the Tigris triangle: Ashur, Nineveh, Arbela [B]. He later takes part of eastern
  Mitanni [B].
- **Arzawa.** The great power of western Anatolia in Amenhotep III's day: he sought a daughter of Tarhundaradu for his
  harem, and their letters are in Hittite (EA 31-32) [A]. Its capital was Apasa (later Ephesus) [B]. By 1346 Hatti is
  recovering at its expense; Mira, the Seha River Land, Hapalla and Wilusa are lands of the "Arzawa lands", each with
  its own ruler, more allied than ruled [B: Bryce, Kingdom].
- **Alashiya.** Cyprus, with a king who calls Pharaoh "brother" (EA 33-40) and pays his way in copper [A]. Where his
  seat was is disputed: Enkomi is the largest harbour town, but the clay of the Alashiya letters comes from the
  southern foothills of the Troodos, near Alassa and Kalavasos [C: Goren et al. 2003]. A royal official, the
  "governor of Alashiya", also wrote to his Egyptian counterpart (EA 40) [A].

**Lands with no king** (start lawless or unruled, as "The edges of the map" and "People of the land" say):

- **Lukka** (`lukka`): the Lukka men raid Alashiya year by year (EA 38) [A]; no kingdom, later Hittite subjects in name.
- **Isuwa** (`isuwa`): a buffer that changed sides between Hatti and Mitanni; Suppiluliuma takes it in the Great Syrian
  War [A: Shattiwaza treaty]. Ruled by its own kings [B]; start it neutral.
- **The middle Euphrates** (`mari`): by 1346 the land of Hana (Terqa its centre) under local kings, within Mitanni's
  reach [B]. Mari itself is a ruin since Hammurabi (about 1760) [A].
- **Susa** (`susa`): Elam under the dynasty of the Igihalkids [B], unconnected with the letters. A generation before
  1346 a Kassite Kurigalzu took Susa [C: Kurigalzu I or II]; whether Babylonia still holds it in 1346 is unknown [C]. Start neutral, as now.
- **Kaska lands, Sutean steppe** (item 7): tribes without kings ("The edges of the map").
- **Habiru** (*'apiru*): not a people but outcasts, runaways and hired fighters, everywhere in the vassals' letters [B].

## Design questions

1. **Labaya in 1346.** The design names Shechem "under Labaya". He was probably dead by 1346, his sons ruling. Reading
   taken: item 7 starts Shechem under **Mut-Baal, a son of Labaya**, with Labaya's quarrel with Abdi-Heba inherited
   ("the sons of Labaya", EA 287, 289), unless Haakon prefers Labaya himself for the story, in which case the start is
   marked as a stretch of about five years.
2. **Suppiluliuma or his predecessor.** Starting with Suppiluliuma is the reading taken.
3. **Ugarit's lord.** It starts in Egypt's sphere (it wrote to Pharaoh as a servant) with a Hittite treaty to come.
4. **Alashiya's capital.** Enkomi stays the capital as the design says; the clay evidence for the Alassa-Kalavasos
   area argues for naming the western province "Kalavasos" if it takes in the south ("Map review").
