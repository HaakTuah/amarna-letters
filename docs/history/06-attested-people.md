# 6. Attested people

For "Attested characters", "Nubians in Egypt's service" and "Names" (item 10), and the living pool (item 11). Grades
and abbreviations: [README](README.md). "In 1346" says whether the person was alive and in that role then: **yes**,
**likely**, **doubt** (the evidence fits either way), **later** (a name for later in a game, or for style), **dead**.

**In one line:** the letters name some three hundred people; below are those who could appear as kings, rulers,
envoys, officials, scribes, merchants and soldiers in 1346, by court, then the name styles for everyone else.

The roles are the game's: a ruler is a king or a vassal ruler; the others are characters ("Errands, not roles").
What each person is known for is a fact about him, for his traits ("Attested characters"), never a goal.

## Name styles for everyone else

Each region's characters are named in its own style ("Names"); where no names survive, the style is invented and
marked [G].

| Region | Style, with attested examples | Grade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Egypt | Theophoric names with Amun, Ra, Ptah, Thoth, under Akhenaten often the Aten: Ramose, Ptahmose, Meryra, Paatenemheb, Tutu, Nakht, Mahu, Huya, Penre | A |
| Babylonia (Akkadian) | Marduk-nadin-ahhe, Nabu-, Ili-, -iddina, Enlil-kidinni, Salmu | A |
| Babylonia (Kassite) | Kadashman-, Kurigalzu, Burna-, Kudur-, -Buriash, -Harbe, Shagarakti- | A |
| Assyria | Ashur-, Enlil-, Adad-, -uballit, -nirari, Ninurta- | A |
| Mitanni and Kizzuwatna, Nuzi (Hurrian) | -Teshup, Akit-, Kili-, Ehli-, Agib-, Tulubri, Pirissi, women's names in -hepa (Gilukhepa, Tadukhepa) | A |
| Mitanni's elite (Indo-Aryan) | Tushratta, Artatama, Shuttarna, Biridashwa; also in the Levant: Biryawaza, Biridiya | A |
| Hatti and Arzawa (Hittite, Luwian) | Arnuwanda, Tudhaliya, -ziti, Tarhunta-, Kurunta-, -muwa, Piyama-, Uhhaziti, Mashhuiluwa | A |

| Aegeans (Linear B, Greek) | a-ki-re-u (Akhileus), e-ke-ra-wo, a-pi-me-de, te-se-u (Theseus?), women a-re-ka-sa-da-ra (Alexandra) | A |

Women are attested chiefly as queens, princesses and brides, and as the writers of a few letters (EA 48, EA 273-274 from
the "Lady of the Lions", Baʿlat-nese, a ruler in southern Canaan) [A]; the game's characters follow the same rules
whatever their sex ("Different data, same rules").
