# 5. Economy: where each resource came from, and in what proportions

For "Mines are where they were", "Six resources", "Edge markets", "What each edge trades" and "Who has what" (item
9+), and the state of each realm (item 11+). Grades and abbreviations: [README](README.md). Map ids as in
`src/engine/src/model/map.ts`; item 7's new provinces by name.

**In one line:** gold came from Egypt's Eastern Desert and Nubia, copper from Cyprus above all, tin from beyond the map
by two roads, cedar from the Lebanon, horses from the northern grasslands, and grain from everywhere, with the surplus
in the flood valleys; absolute outputs are mostly unknown, so the shares below are proportions, most of them marked
estimates.

## Gold (precious metal: gold and silver)

| Source (province) | Share of the map's new precious metal | Grade | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lower Nubia, Wawat: the Wadi Allaqi and its desert (Nubia, item 7) | 45% | B | Thutmose III's *Annals* list Wawat's gold yearly, about 3,000 deben (some 280 kg) in his year 34; Wadi Allaqi's mines |
| Egypt's Eastern Desert, by Coptos (`thebes`) | 25% | B | Many New Kingdom workings (Klemm and Klemm, *Gold and Gold Mining in Ancient Egypt and Nubia*, 2013); the Turin mining papyrus (later) |
| Upper Nubia, Kush (Nubia, item 7) | 10% | B | The *Annals* list Kush's gold separately, in smaller amounts |
| Hatti's silver: the Taurus (Bolkardağ) and Keban by Isuwa (`tuwanuwa`, `kizzuwatna`, `isuwa`) | 12% | B | The Hittites reckoned in silver; the Taurus silver-lead mines (Yener) |
| River gold of western Anatolia, the Pactolus by the Hermus (`seha`) | 5% | C | Famous later (Lydia, 7th century); its working in 1346 is unproven |
| Others (Arzawa's hills, Cyprus's small silver) | 3% | G | |

Egypt's gold was what every other Great King asked for: "in my brother's land gold is as plentiful as dirt" (EA 19,
Tushratta) [A]; Burna-Buriash (EA 7-11) and Ashur-uballit (EA 16) ask for it too [A]. Babylonia under the Kassites
reckoned prices in gold, unusually [B: Edzard; Sassmannshausen], since Egypt's gifts brought it in; Hatti and the
Levant reckoned in silver [B]. **Relative value [C]:** gold about two to three times silver in Egypt and four to six
times in Babylonia and Hatti; the game's single precious metal keeps them as one.

third millennium].

## Tin

No tin was mined on the map in 1346, as far as the sources tell [B]; it came from beyond, as "Edge markets" says.

| Road | Share | Grade | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| East, overland from Central Asia through Iran, into Elam (`susa`), up the Euphrates (`mari`) to Syria | 50% | C | The Mari letters (18th century) show tin from Elam passing through Mari to Aleppo, Qatna and Ugarit [A, earlier]; Powell et al. (2022) put about a third of the Uluburun tin at Mušiston in Central Asia |
| West, by sea, arriving on Arzawa's coast, at Alashiya and in the Levantine ports | 50% | C | Berger et al. (2019, *PLOS ONE*) and Williams et al. (2025, *Antiquity*) match tin ingots from wrecks off Israel to Cornwall and Devon, about 1300 BCE ([Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/british-tin-bronze-age-civilizations)); Berger et al. (2023) argue part of the Uluburun tin shares that origin |

**The debate, weighed.** Powell et al. (2022, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abq3766)) attribute about **two thirds**
of the Uluburun tin to **Kestel in the Taurus**, on the map in Hatti's lands near Tuwanuwa and Kizzuwatna, and a third
to Mušiston [C]. Kestel's mining is otherwise dated to the Early Bronze Age (Yener), and Berger et al. (2023) reject
the attribution. The reading taken: **tin stays off the map, split evenly between the eastern road and the western
sea** [C], because the Late Bronze working of Kestel is unproven and the British tin of the Israeli wrecks and the
Elamite tin of the Mari letters are each attested. The doubt is a design question: an on-map tin source in Hatti
would give one court both metals, against "no court has both" ("Six resources").

**Proportion of copper to tin [A]:** about ten to one, as the Uluburun wreck carried them (some ten tons of copper in
354 oxhide and other ingots, about a ton of tin), the proportions of bronze.

## What each edge trades: first guesses

All amounts are per year of game time, as shares of what the map makes or needs; item 9+ turns them into numbers [G].

| Edge | Sells | Buys | First guess |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The eastern road, beyond Susa | Tin (half the map's) | Gold | Enough tin, with the western share, for about a tenth of the map's copper to be made bronze |
| Keftiu and Ahhiyawa, beyond the western sea | Tin (half the map's) | Gold, copper | Copper bought at about a tenth of Alashiya's output, as Cypriot ingots reached the Aegean [B] |
| Magan, at the Gulf's end | Copper (a twentieth of the map's) | Gold, grain | Small, as the Magan trade had shrunk [B] |

## Exchange rates the period gives (for the economy's later phases)

- Copper to silver about **150-200 to 1** by weight at Ugarit [C: Heltzer; Monroe, *Scales of Fate*, 2009].
- Tin to silver about **10-15 to 1** in the Old Babylonian Mari texts [B, earlier]; dearer in the west [C].
- Barley to silver about **one kor (300 litres) for a shekel** in Ugarit and Nuzi texts [C].
- Gold to silver: see "Gold" above.

These are evidence for the balance pass (item 18), not rules.
