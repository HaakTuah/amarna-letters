# 5. Economy: where each resource came from, and in what proportions

For "Mines are where they were", "Six resources", "Edge markets", "What each edge trades" and "Who has what" (item
9+), and the state of each realm (item 11+). Grades and abbreviations: [README](README.md). Map ids as in
`src/engine/src/model/map.ts`; item 7's new provinces by name.

**In one line:** gold came from Egypt's Eastern Desert and Nubia, copper from Cyprus above all, tin from beyond the map
by two roads, cedar from the Lebanon, horses from the northern grasslands, and grain from everywhere, with the surplus
in the flood valleys; absolute outputs are mostly unknown, so the shares below are proportions, most of them marked
estimates. **Every share in the tables below is a first guess [G]**; the grade column grades the evidence for the
source, not the number.

No published estimate divides the New Kingdom's gold by region; the shares stay first guesses [G]. The nearest proxy: Thutmose III's *Annals* give Kush about 6% of the Nubian gold (300+, 100+ and 144 deben against Wawat's [25]55, 2,844 and 3,144 in years 34, 38 and 40-41) [A]; Vercoutter (*Kush* 7, 1959, p. 135) reconstructs the two from the texts and finds Kush gaining from Amenhotep III onward [C, not read]; the game's 17% for Kush sits between. Klemm and Klemm (2013) count "more than 250 gold production sites" in the Eastern Desert and the Nubian Desert, most worked in the peak of about 1480-1340, without a split by region [B]. Hatti's silver: no production estimate found; the share is a share of value, counted shekel for shekel with gold [G].

Egypt's gold was what every other Great King asked for: "In my brother's country, gold is as plentiful as dirt"
(EA 19 (Moran), Tushratta) [A]; Burna-Buriash (EA 7, 9, 11; EA 9 (Rainey): "Send me much gold") and Ashur-uballit (EA 16)
ask for it too, and Burna-Buriash complains of short-weight gold (EA 10) [A]. Babylonia under the Kassites reckoned
prices in gold, unusually [B: Edzard; Sassmannshausen; Ross and Bettenay, *Cambridge Archaeological Journal* 34 (2024),
pp. 403-420, p. 412], since Egypt's gifts brought it in; Hatti and the Levant reckoned in silver [B].

<a id="05-gold-silver-value"></a>**Relative value [C]:** gold 2 to 3.3 times silver in Egypt (twelfth century, after Černý) and 4 to 8 times in Kassite Babylonia
(thirteenth century, after Del Monte), 3 to 4 in Ugarit's texts (Heltzer, *Iraq* 39, 1977, n. 16); no Hittite figure
was checked [S]. Ross and Bettenay, p. 405: "Gold-rich Egypt recorded a range of 2–3.33". Ross and Bettenay (*CAJ* 34, 2024, pp. 403-420) gather the period's values: Egypt never above 3.3, Kassite Babylonia 4 to 8, Ebla and Ugarit no more than 6.4; no Nuzi, Hittite or Mitannian figure. Their middle for the map, about 4 [G]. The game's single precious
metal keeps them as one.

No published estimate divides Late Bronze copper by region [G]. The proxies agree on Cyprus's lead: it made "the vast majority" of the oxhide ingots found at home and abroad (Kassianidou, snippet) [B], and Israel's LBA II ingots are Cypriot oxhides (Yahalom-Mack et al., *JAS* 45, 2014) [B]. Ergani's share rests on the deposit's size and on Alshe in the Shattiwaza treaty.

<a id="05-copper-magan"></a>**Magan** (Oman) sells a little copper at the Gulf edge [C: the Dilmun-Magan trade, smaller by this period than in

"Sumerian cuneiform texts of around 2300 BCE"; "Dilmun": "A decline is visible from around 1720 BC"; no text of 1346
naming Magan was found].

## Tin

No tin was mined on the map in 1346, as far as the sources tell [B]; it came from beyond, as "Edge markets" says.

| Road | Share | Grade | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <a id="05-tin-east"></a>East, overland from Central Asia through Iran, into Elam (`susa`), up the Euphrates (`mari`) to Syria | 50% [G] | C | The Mari letters (18th century) show tin from Elam passing through Mari to Aleppo and Ugarit, and on to Hazor [A, earlier: Dossin 1970, as cited by Johnson et al. 2020]; Powell et al. (2022) put about a third of the Uluburun tin at Mušiston in Central Asia (abstract: "ores from Central Asia (Uzbekistan and Tajikistan) were used to produce one-third of the Uluburun tin ingots"; they also note that southern Iraq after 1500 BCE "relied mainly on the reuse and recycling of bronze", which weighs against a strong eastern road in 1346 [C]) |
| <a id="05-tin-west"></a>West, by sea, arriving on Arzawa's coast, at Alashiya and in the Levantine ports | 50% [G] | C | Berger et al. (2019, *PLOS ONE*: "Cornish tin mines are the most likely suppliers for the 13th–12th centuries tin ingots from Israel") and Williams et al. (2025, *Antiquity*) match tin ingots from wrecks off Israel, some 3,300 years old, to Cornwall and Devon ([Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/british-tin-bronze-age-civilizations)); Berger et al. (2023) argue part of the Uluburun tin shares that origin |

The shares are a first guess [G] from the one 14th-century cargo: of Uluburun's 105 analysed ingots, 35 (group P) are Central Asian for Powell et al. (2022) and south-west English for Berger et al. (2023, "European deposits are currently the more likely sources, although an origin from eastern tin deposits cannot be excluded"), and 68 (group MC) Anatolian for Powell, perhaps Afghan or European for Berger. The western share by reading: 0, a third, or all; their middle, with Mari's earlier eastern tin and the Israeli wrecks' later British tin on either side, is about 45% west, 55% east. The game keeps 50/50, since tin is not in play.

<a id="05-tin-kestel"></a>**The debate, weighed.** Powell et al. (2022, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abq3766)) attribute about **two thirds**
of the Uluburun tin to **Kestel in the Taurus**, on the map in Hatti's lands near Tuwanuwa and Kizzuwatna, and a third
to Mušiston [C]. Kestel's mining is otherwise dated to the Early Bronze Age (Yener), and Berger et al. (2023) reject
the attribution (p. 10: "the Kestel mine was apparently not operating in the LBA"). The reading taken: **tin stays off the map, split evenly between the eastern road and the western
sea** [C], because the Late Bronze working of Kestel is unproven and the British tin of the Israeli wrecks and the
Elamite tin of the Mari letters are each attested. The doubt is a design question: an on-map tin source in Hatti
would give one court both metals, against "no court has both" ("Six resources").

<a id="05-copper-tin-ratio"></a>**Proportion of copper to tin [A]:** about ten to one, as the Uluburun wreck carried them (some ten tons of copper
in 354 ingots, 317 of them oxhide, and about a ton of tin: Pulak 2015, as Wikipedia's "Uluburun shipwreck" gives it), the proportions of bronze.

## Exchange rates the period gives (for the economy's later phases)

- <a id="05-rate-copper"></a>Copper to silver about **160 to 1** by weight in the Hittite laws [C: Heltzer, *Iraq* 39, 1977, n. 36,
  after Goetze: "1/4 shekel of silver for 1 mina of copper; ratio 1:160"]; no Ugarit figure was checked.
- <a id="05-rate-tin"></a>Tin to silver about **10-14 to 1** in the Old Babylonian Mari texts [B, earlier: Heltzer, *Iraq* 39, 1977, n. 33,
  after ARMT VII: "10.2 shekels of tin for 1 shekel of silver", "14 tin for 1 silver"].
- <a id="05-rate-barley"></a>Barley to silver about **one kor (300 litres) for a shekel** [B]: the Kassite standard rate, "1 kor barley = 1 shekel silver" (Del Monte, *Rivista di storia economica* 2009, a rate of account, not the market's), and the Hittite Laws § 183, "the price of 4 parīsu of barley is ½ shekel of silver" (Viano, *AuOr* 34, 2016, p. 150), about 280 litres for a Babylonian shekel's weight of silver, if the parīsu is 50 qû of about a litre [G]. Ugarit gives no barley price (Heltzer 1978, 17, as Viano n. 12 notes). In famine at Emar a shekel bought 3 qû, at worst 1 (Viano, p. 150) [A, via Viano]. An earlier tariff gives the same: the Laws of Eshnunna, §1 (early 18th century), as J. W. Welch quotes them
  (FARMS, 1999, p. 147, [pdf](https://archive.bookofmormoncentral.org/sites/default/files/pressing_forward_35_-_the_laws_of_eshnunna_and_nephite_economics.pdf)):
  "1 kor of barley [she'um] is (priced) at [ana] 1 shekel of silver".
- Gold to silver: see "Gold" above.

These are evidence for the balance pass (item 18), not rules.
