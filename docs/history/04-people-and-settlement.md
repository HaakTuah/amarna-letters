# 4. People and settlement

For "People of the land" (item 10+) and city-states as powers (item 11+). Grades and abbreviations:
[README](README.md).

**In one line:** ancient numbers are uncertain to a factor of two, so this dossier gives ranges; Egypt alone held
about as many people as all the other powers together, the Levant's famous cities were small (a few
thousand people each), and nine in ten people everywhere worked the land or herded.

<a id="04-density"></a>**How the estimates are made.** Few texts count people. Estimates come from the settled area that surveys and
excavations find, times a density of about **175 people per hectare** of town, in a range of 100-250 [G], plus villages, plus a share
for herders who leave little trace. "Usually, researchers apply a range of 100–200 persons/ha to the total settlement
area, based on ethnohistorical parallels (Kramer 1982)" (Wossink, *Challenging Climate Change*, 2009, p. 55); Broshi
and Gophna use 250 for Middle Bronze Palestine (*BASOR* 261, 1986); excavated houses can give far more (Postgate,
1994: 248-1,205 a hectare of living space at Early Dynastic Abu Salabikh, as Wossink, p. 56, reports). Most ranges below are this dossier's own first guesses [G] where no study gives a
figure; the basis says which rest on one. The game is calibrated within these ranges and against today's yields
("People of the land"); where a range is wide, start in its middle.

## Who people were

| Share | Range | First guess | Grade | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <a id="04-farmers"></a>Farmers and herders | 80-90% | 85% | G | Agrarian societies generally; no text of the period counts the share |
| <a id="04-townspeople"></a>Townspeople (craftsmen, traders, scribes, priests, officials, servants) | 8-15% | 12% | G | Town areas above against the region's total; town dwellers were 20-40% (Adams 1981, table 12: Kassite settlement 56.8% in sites of 10 ha or less and 30.4% in cities over 40 ha, by area; Ugarit's city 6,000-8,000 of 28,000-36,000), but many of them farmed |
| <a id="04-soldiers"></a>Soldiers in standing service | 1-3% | 2% (Egypt and Hatti 2-3%, the vassal cities 1%) | G | No text gives a share and no published share was found; the letters' counts below |
| <a id="04-herders"></a>Herders outside the towns' control (Suteans, Shasu, Kaska) | 10-20% in the Levant and the steppes, more in the Kaska lands | 15% where they live | G | Egyptian and Mari texts name them but count no share, and no published share was found (the game's loose share for the Levant is 10%, inside the range); Rowton's "enclosed nomadism" [B] |

**Soldiers, as the letters count them** [A]:
- <a id="04-vassal-garrisons"></a>A vassal asks for a few dozen to a few hundred: Biridiya of Megiddo asks for 100 men to
  guard the city (EA 244 (Moran): "a garrison of 100 men to guard his city"), Abdi-Heba of Jerusalem for 50 (EA 289),
  Rib-Hadda for 200 infantry and 50 pairs of horses (EA 71), 300 men (EA 93) and a garrison of 400 men with horses
  (EA 76), and Abi-Milku of Tyre for only 20 (EA 151 (Moran): "give me 20 men to guard the city"). No letter gives
  the whole strength of an Egyptian garrison; that they were of tens to hundreds of men is this dossier's reading of
  the requests [G].
- <a id="04-great-armies"></a>A Great King's army is of thousands: Thutmose III took "924 chariots" at Megiddo (his
  *Annals*); at Qadesh (1274) the Egyptian account gives the Hittites 2,500 chariots in one charge and, in the
  handbooks' reading, 37,000 foot in reserve, likely inflated [A, with doubt].
- Mitanni's chariot elite, the *maryannu*, are named as a class in the Alalakh and Nuzi texts [A]; that they held land
  for chariot service is the accepted reading [B]; Ugarit's texts list similar chariot men [A].

**The Habiru** were not a people but uprooted men of every origin (debtors, runaways, outlaws) who lived as hired
fighters and raiders; the vassals accuse each other of joining them (EA 286-290) [A, B]. In the game they are loose men
(item 10+'s crowd), not a land.
