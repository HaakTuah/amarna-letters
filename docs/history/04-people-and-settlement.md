# 4. People and settlement

For "People of the land" (item 10+) and city-states as powers (item 11+). Grades and abbreviations:
[README](README.md).

**In one line:** ancient numbers are uncertain to a factor of two, so this dossier gives ranges; Egypt alone held
about as many people as all the other powers together, the Levant's famous cities were small (a few
thousand people each), and nine in ten people everywhere worked the land or herded.

**How the estimates are made.** Few texts count people. Estimates come from the settled area that surveys and
excavations find, times a density of about **100-200 people per hectare** of town (Kramer; Postgate; Broshi and
Gophna) [C], plus villages, plus a share for herders who leave little trace. The game is calibrated within these ranges
and against today's yields ("People of the land"); where a range is wide, start in its middle.

## Who people were

| Share | Range | First guess | Grade | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Farmers and herders | 80-90% | 85% | B | Agrarian societies generally; the village lists of Ugarit and Nuzi |
| Townspeople (craftsmen, traders, scribes, priests, officials, servants) | 8-15% | 12% | C | Town areas against the region's total |
| Soldiers in standing service | 1-3% | 2% (Egypt and Hatti 2-3%, the vassal cities 1%) | C | See below |
| Herders outside the towns' control (Suteans, Shasu, Kaska) | 10-20% in the Levant and the steppes, more in the Kaska lands | 15% where they live | C | Egyptian and Mari texts; Rowton's "enclosed nomadism" |

**Soldiers, as the letters count them** [A]:
- A vassal asks for a few dozen to a few hundred: Biridiya of Megiddo asks for 100 men to guard the city (EA 244),
  Abdi-Heba of Jerusalem for 50 (EA 289), Rib-Hadda for 300 and for horses and chariots (EA 71, EA 76), and Egypt's
  garrisons in Canaan are of tens of men.
- A Great King's army is of thousands: Thutmose III took 924 chariots at Megiddo (his *Annals*); at Qadesh (1274) the
  Egyptian account gives the Hittites 2,500 chariots in one charge and 37,000 foot in reserve, likely inflated [A, with
  doubt].
- Mitanni's chariot elite, the *maryannu*, were a class of landholders owing chariot service [A: Alalakh and Nuzi
  texts]; Ugarit's texts list similar chariot men [A].

**The Habiru** were not a people but uprooted men of every origin (debtors, runaways, outlaws) who lived as hired
fighters and raiders; the vassals accuse each other of joining them (EA 286-290) [A, B]. In the game they are loose men
(item 10+'s crowd), not a land.
