# 11. Armies and fleets

For "Command at a distance" and the armies and fleets each power starts with (item 13, "Starting positions come from
the sources"), and the pace of war the balance pass tunes (item 18). Grades and abbreviations: [README](README.md); the
works this dossier adds are under [Sources](#sources-this-dossier-adds). Map ids are those of
`src/engine/src/model/map.ts`. The soldiers' share of the people, and the vassals' requests for a few dozen men, are in
[04-people-and-settlement.md](04-people-and-settlement.md) ("Who people were"); the commanders and officials by name are
in [06-attested-people.md](06-attested-people.md); journey times are in [02-geography.md](02-geography.md).

**In one line:** a Great King's field army was of thousands to a few tens of thousands, built round chariots and archers,
raised for a campaign each spring and sent home or into garrisons each autumn; a vassal city held tens to a few hundreds;
no power kept a war fleet as such, and ships were merchantmen and transports pressed into service, most of them owned by
the coastal cities.

## How sure the numbers are

No document of the period counts a whole army. The figures come from victory accounts (Thutmose III's *Annals*, the
Qadesh poem and reliefs, Mursili II's *Annals*), from treaties fixing a vassal's contingent, from the rosters and ration
lists of Nuzi, Alalakh and Ugarit, and from what the Amarna letters ask for. Victory accounts inflate the enemy and
round the booty [B: the handbooks agree]; rosters and treaties are the soundest. So every size below is a range graded
[C] unless a document gives it, and the start values are proportions between powers, which is what the game needs
first: item 13 turns them into its own unit of strength.

**Readings for the game [G]:** an army has a kind as well as a size, as "Starting positions come from the sources"
asks: its share of chariots (dear in horses and gold, fast, decisive in the open), and of foot (cheap, needed to hold
towns and to storm them). Hired men are loose men and bands under a fee ("Bands are a character's soldiers"), not a
kind of unit; levies are the levy item 10+ already raises.

Ranges are for a whole field army, not what a king had standing: most men went home after the season, and a king's
standing force (his guard, his chariots and the garrisons) was perhaps a fifth to a third of it [C]. **Start from:** the
proportions in the "Start from" column, so that Hatti and Egypt lead in men, Hatti and Mitanni in chariots, and Alashiya
and the small powers in ships ("Starting positions come from the sources": "Hatti and Mitanni strong in chariots").

**Start from:** the middle of each range for the cities that fight in the letters (Amurru, Damascus, Shechem, Qatna) and
the low end for those that only plead (Byblos, Jerusalem, Megiddo, Tyre). Egypt's garrisons in its centres (`gaza`,
`sumur`, Kumidi) are tens of men, the low end of a town's range [A: EA 287, EA 289].

## Command

- **Kings led in person** on the great campaigns: Thutmose III at Megiddo, Suppiluliuma in his one-year campaign,
  Mursili II every year of his *Annals* [A]. A king's absence was a risk at home: Mursili's *Annals* open with the
  enemies who mocked him as a child king [A].
- **Princes and great officials led the rest:** Suppiluliuma's sons Telipinu (Kizzuwatna, then Aleppo) and Piyassili
  (Sharri-Kushuh, at Carchemish) commanded in Syria as viceroys [A: *Deeds*; Beckman]; in Egypt the commissioners
  (*rabisu*) such as Yanhamu led the archers sent into Canaan and handed out grain (see the people dossier) [A]; the
  Hittite border governors (*bel madgalti*) held the frontier forts and their garrisons by written instructions [A:
  Miller, *Royal Hittite Instructions*].
- **A vassal led his own men**, and the treaties bound him to bring them when his overlord campaigned near, or to come
  in person (Beckman: the treaties with Sunashshura, Aziru and Tette of Nuhashshe) [A]. Egypt bound its vassals to prepare for its archers rather than to march with
  them [A: below].

## Logistics and the campaigning season

- **Spring to autumn.** Thutmose III left Tjaru in the fourth month of *Peret* (about April) and fought at Megiddo in
  May [A: *Annals*]; Mursili II campaigned each year from spring and went home, or sometimes wintered in the field, as
  in Arzawa [A: his *Annals*]. Winter closed the Anatolian plateau and the Taurus passes [B: see the environment
  dossier]. **Reading [G]:** a campaign is a season or two a year, as the game's seasons already make it; an army kept
  in the field through winter costs its pay and food and its sick, not a rule against it.
- **Food went ahead of the army.** Thutmose III had the Phoenician harbours stocked with bread, oil, wine and grain
  before each campaign from his sixth on [A: *Annals*]. The vassals write that they have made ready food, drink, oil,
  grain, oxen and sheep "for the archers of the king" (Ashkelon, EA 320-326; and many others, e.g. EA 55, 141-142) [A].
  An army that marched beyond its stores lived off the land, which is what the vassals feared [A: the complaints of
  plunder by Nubian troops, EA 287].
- **Rations and burden** [B: Engels, *Alexander the Great and the Logistics of the Macedonian Army*, for the
  arithmetic]: a soldier eats about 1 kg of grain a day, a horse about 4-5 kg of grain and fodder besides grazing; a
  donkey carries about 70-90 kg. So an army of 10,000 with 1,000 chariots (2,000-3,000 horses) needs about 20-25 tonnes
  of food a day, which is why armies kept to rivers, harbours and the stores of the towns they crossed.
- **Pay.** Soldiers were paid in rations and land; hired men in silver and goods [A: Rib-Hadda writes that he has sold
  his sons, daughters and furnishings for grain and has nothing left to keep his men (EA 74-138, e.g. EA 74, 81, 85);
  Egyptian soldiers' plots in the Wilbour Papyrus, later]. The same letters show unfed men going over to the Habiru
  [A].

## Fleets

No power of the period kept a war fleet as later navies did. Ships were owned by kings, temples and merchants and pressed
into service as transports and, rarely, for fights at sea [B: Wachsmann].

| Who | Ships | Grade | Basis | Start from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Egypt | Royal ships at the dockyard of Peru-nefer; "Byblos ships" (*kbn*) for the sea; river craft by the hundred on the Nile | A (that they existed), C (how many) | Thutmose III shipped troops and supplies to the Phoenician coast (*Annals*); the dockyard records of Peru-nefer (Papyrus BM 10056, Thutmose III's reign) [A] | A middling sea fleet, mostly transports; the Nile's river craft are the realm's own roads, not a fleet |
| Hatti | Almost none of its own: it used its vassals' ships, Ugarit's above all, and Ura in Cilicia as its port | B | The Hittite king asks Ugarit for a ship and crew to carry grain to Ura (RS 20.212, 13th century) [A]; Suppiluliuma II's sea fight off Alashiya, about 1200 (KBo XII 38) [A, much later] | The low end, as `kizzuwatna`'s fleet shows already |
| Ugarit | Many merchant ships; one letter has its king ordering 150 ships made ready | A (the ships), C (the 150: read as the whole fleet, about 1200) | Ugarit's texts (RS 18.148, as the handbooks cite it) [C]; the last letters (RS 20.238) [A] | The largest fleet of the coast after Egypt's |
| Alashiya | Its own merchant ships, carrying copper to Egypt and the Levant | A | EA 33-40, the king's letters on ships and copper; EA 39 asks that his merchants and ship be let pass untaxed [A] | A fleet larger than its army; the low middle in numbers |
| Byblos, Tyre, Sidon, Arwad | Merchant ships; Arwad's and Sidon's ships fought for Abdi-Ashirta's sons and against Tyre | A | Rib-Hadda: the ships of Arwad block Byblos and Sumur (EA 98, 101, 104-105) [A]; Abi-Milku: Zimredda of Sidon takes Tyre's mainland town with ships (EA 148-149) [A] | Arwad, Sidon and Tyre: a few dozen each [C]; Byblos fewer, pinned in by Arwad |
| Arzawa | Coastal ships on the Aegean shore; the Lukka and the Aegean raiders beside it | C | Arzawa's coast and its harbour at Apasa (see the geography dossier); no letter mentions its ships [S] | The low end [G] |
| Lukka | Raiders' boats | A | EA 38: the men of Lukka raid Alashiya yearly | Raiders, not a fleet: pirates in the game's sense ("Pirates") |

**Ships themselves** [A: the wrecks]: the Uluburun ship (about 1300) was about 15 m long and carried about 20 tonnes of
cargo with a crew of perhaps 5-10; the Cape Gelidonya ship (about 1200) about 10 m. Larger merchantmen are implied by
cargoes of grain reckoned in thousands of measures (RS 20.212) [C]. A ship of war, when ships fought, was such a ship
with soldiers aboard: archers and spearmen fighting from deck as at Medinet Habu (about 1177) [A, a century and a half
later]. **Reading [G]:** a fleet's strength is its ships and the men aboard, and its main work is carrying armies and
grain and keeping another's ships from a coast; when it can sail is in the environment dossier ("The sea").

## What item 13 should not read into this

The letters ask for troops as vassals ask a lord, in a voice of need ("Style, not strategy" in the README): that
Rib-Hadda begs for 300 men is a fact about Byblos's weakness, not a rule that Egypt should send them.
