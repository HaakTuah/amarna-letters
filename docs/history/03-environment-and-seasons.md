# 3. Environment and seasons

For "Land and climate", "Rivers" and "Seasons" (travel's seasons in item 7; fields, rivers and winter supplies in item
10+; the economy's harvests in item 9+). Grades and abbreviations: [README](README.md).

**In one line:** two farming worlds share the board: the flood-irrigated valleys (Egypt and Nubia on the Nile, Babylonia
on the Euphrates and Tigris) and the rain-fed lands (the Levant, north Syria, Assyria, Anatolia, Cyprus), and their
calendars differ; Egypt's three seasons fit them all as four-month blocks, since in 1346 the civil calendar ran within
about a week of the real year ("Seasons").

The game's seasons, as months of the real year (Egyptian civil calendar near its alignment) [B]:
**Akhet** about mid-July to mid-November, **Peret** mid-November to mid-March, **Shemu** mid-March to mid-July.

**Hard winters** (for "Winter in the field"): the Anatolian plateau and the Taurus passes are hardest; inland Syria,
the Habur and Assyria are cold with frost; the coasts, Egypt and Babylonia are mild [B]. The Hittite kings campaigned
from spring to autumn and came home for the winter festivals: the *Annals* of Mursili II end each year's campaign
with "I went into winter quarters" [A].

## Rivers and floods

- **The Nile** rises from about July, peaks in September and falls through October; its height decides Egypt's
  harvest [A]. A low flood means hunger; a very high one breaks dykes and floods villages [A: Egyptian texts across
  periods]. How often: the medieval Roda nilometer record suggests a poor flood about one year in five and a
  disastrous one rarely, perhaps once a generation [C: Hassan's studies of the Roda record]. No bad flood is recorded
  for Akhenaten's reign [S]. **First guess [G]:** a low Nile one Akhet in eight, a ruinous one one in thirty.
- **The Tigris and the Euphrates** rise with the snowmelt in the Taurus and Zagros, the Tigris peaking in April and
  the Euphrates in May, at or just before the harvest, so their floods do harm, breaking levees and changing course
  [A: Mesopotamian texts on floods across periods; B for the months]. The rivers also silt and salt the fields of the
  south [B]. **First guess [G]:** a damaging flood one Shemu in six on each river.
- **The Orontes, Habur, Balikh and Halys** are smaller rivers with spring maxima; not navigable for trade [B].

## The sea

- **Sailing season:** roughly April to October; winter storms and short days close the open sea from about November to
  March [B: later Greek and Roman evidence (Hesiod, Vegetius); Ugarit's letters about ships]. Coasting goes on longer
  in fair spells [B]. This is Peret = closed, as "Seasons" says.
- **Winds:** north and north-west winds prevail in summer (the Etesians, July-September), helping ships south to Egypt
  and slowing them north; so the circuit ran anticlockwise ("The sea" in the geography dossier) [B].
- **Currents:** an anticlockwise current runs along the Levantine coast northwards [B].

**Readings for the game:** 1346 is an ordinary climate, before the drying that preceded the collapse of about 1200,
so droughts and plagues are occasional events rather than a trend [B]. Rain-fed lands (Hatti above all, then the
Levant, Mitanni and Assyria) suffer drought; flood lands suffer bad floods; trading ports and besieged cities catch
plague, as the letters show. **First guesses [G]:** a drought on the Anatolian plateau one year in six, in the Levant
and north Syria one in eight; a plague in a port or a besieged city one season in a hundred per province, more in a
siege.
