# 2. Geography: routes, journey times, terrain, rivers, coasts

For "Map review", "Distance, not steps", "Historical routes and times" and "Finer seas" (item 7), and later distance
inside the realm (item 13) and roads (item 15). Grades and abbreviations: [README](README.md). Map ids are those of
`src/engine/src/model/map.ts`.

**In one line:** armies and caravans of the period covered about 20-30 km a day on land, ships about 50-100 km a day
along a coast; the attested journeys calibrate that, and the great routes (the Ways of Horus, the coast road, the King's
Highway, the Euphrates road, the Taurus passes) give the map its connections.

**Readings for the game [G]:** a land connection's length in days is its distance at **25 km a day** on good ground,
**15** in mountains, passes and forest, **20** across the desert, where water fixes the stages; a river boat downstream
covers about **60 km a day**; a coasting ship **70**, and open sea **100** in the sailing season ("Seasons" in the
environment dossier). These are travel times for a party; an army with its train is slower by about a third, as the
Gaza-Yehem march shows.

## The great routes

- <a id="02-ways-of-horus"></a>**The Ways of Horus** across north Sinai, from Tjaru (Sile, at the Delta's edge) to Gaza, with a chain of forts and
  wells [A: Seti I's relief at Karnak, some fifty years later; Papyrus Anastasi I, later still]. The only army road between Egypt and
  Canaan. ANET, p. 254 (Wilson), on Seti I's scenes: "the stations on the military road through Sinai, running close to the sea between the Egyptian frontier at Sile (near modern Kantarah) and Raphia"; [Archaeology Wiki](https://www.archaeology.wiki/blog/2022/03/02/the-pharaohs-water-wells-along-the-ways-of-horus/) (2022): "which started from ancient Egyptian Tjaru (the modern site of Tell Hebua I) and continued until today's Gaza", "with a series of fortresses and water wells built along it"; on Papyrus Anastasi I, ANET, p. 478, n. 47: "the main road across Sinai", and p. 477, n.: the satire's last section "deals with the road between Egypt and Palestine".
- <a id="02-coast-road"></a>**The coast road** (later Via Maris) from Gaza through the Sharon and the Megiddo pass to the Jezreel valley, then
  north by Hazor to Damascus or along the coast by Akko and Tyre [B: Thutmose III's army went from Gaza by Yehem through the pass to Megiddo, ANET, p. 235 (Wilson): "my majesty shall proceed upon this Aruna road"; Hazor "served as a commercial center with links to Babylon and Syria", Pleiades 779967430; the later name, Isaiah 9:1 (KJV): "by the way of the sea, beyond Jordan"].
- <a id="02-kings-highway"></a>**The King's Highway** east of the Jordan, from the Gulf of Aqaba by Edom and Moab to Damascus [B; the name is later: Numbers 20:17 (KJV), to Edom: "we will go by the king's high way"; Pleiades, "Via Nova Traiana" (147882288): the Roman road "may supersede an earlier one known as the 'Via Regia' or 'King's Highway'"].
- <a id="02-beqaa"></a>**The Beqaa** between the Lebanon and the Anti-Lebanon, from Kumidi to Qadesh [B, from the sites: Kumidi (Pleiades 10481849) lies in the southern Beqaa and Qadesh (Laodicea ad Libanum, 668291) "on the Orontes River in Coele-Syria", at the valley's northern end; no text of the period found naming the road].
- <a id="02-euphrates-road"></a>**The Euphrates road** from Babylonia up the river by Hana (Terqa) and Emar (where the road from Aleppo crosses: `aleppo`-`harran`) to Carchemish, the crossing to Syria and
  Anatolia, and the road by Harran to the Habur [B: Terqa "on the banks of the middle Euphrates", "the leading city of the kingdom of Ḫana" (Wikipedia, "Terqa", citing Podany 2014); Emar "An important Bronze Age trading center in northern Syria" (Pleiades 279984092); the crossing by Carchemish, ANET, p. 240, n. 18 (Wilson): "the Amen-em-heb inscription (p. 241) makes the crossing possible in the Carchemish district"].
- <a id="02-tigris-road"></a>**The Tigris road** from Babylonia by Ashur to Nineveh, and east by Arbela and Arrapha to the Zagros [B: Ashur "on the western bank of the Tigris River", Pleiades 893945; the road east is the builder's reading from the cities' places, no text found naming it].
- <a id="02-syrian-desert"></a>**The Syrian desert** by Tadmor (Palmyra), Mari's and Hana's shortcut to Qatna and Damascus, held by Sutean nomads [B: Palmyra "an ancient oasis and trading city", Pleiades 668331; Tiglath-pileser I, about 1100, ANET, p. 275 (Oppenheim): "I defeated them from Tadmar (Palmyra) which (lies) in the country Amurru, Anat which (lies) in the country Suhu"; the Suteans on the roads, EA 16 ("Neighbours on the map" below)].
- <a id="02-taurus-passes"></a>**The Taurus passes:** the **Cilician Gates** between the plateau (Tuwanuwa, Kanesh) and Kizzuwatna; the **Amanus
  passes** (the Syrian Gates, Belen) between Kizzuwatna and Mukish (`alalakh`)/Aleppo; the road from Kanesh to Malatya and Isuwa
  [B: Pleiades, "Kilikiai Pylai" (648670): "a pass through the Taurus Mountains connecting Cilicia to the Anatolian Plateau"; "Syriai Pylai" (658608): "(Belen Pass) is a narrow pass through the Nur Mountains"; the Malatya road is the builder's reading, no text found].
- <a id="02-elam-road"></a>**The Elam road** from Babylonia (Der) to Susa [B: Wikipedia, "Der (Sumer)": "east of the Tigris River on the border between Sumer and Elam"; there, in 720, "the Assyrian host was defeated near Der" by Elam and Babylon (citing Tadmor 1958)].
- <a id="02-sea-route"></a>**The sea:** the trading ships went round the eastern Mediterranean anticlockwise: from Egypt north along the Levant,
  to Cyprus, west along southern Anatolia (where the Uluburun ship sank, about 1300), to the Aegean and Crete, and back
  to Egypt across the open sea with the north wind [B: Wachsmann, not reached; Uluburun; the north wind from Crete to Egypt, *Odyssey* 14 (Murray): "with the North Wind blowing fresh and fair" ("Speeds" above); the longshore current runs north up the Levant, Marcus (1998), p. 95: "The Coriolis effect explains the counterclockwise movement of fluids in the Earth's northern hemisphere, creating the longshore current in the Mediterranean"].

## Terrain by connection (proposals for item 7)

- <a id="02-terrain-desert"></a>**Desert connections:** `mari`-`damascus` and `mari`-`qadesh` (the Syrian desert, Sutean land); `delta`-`gaza` and
  `gaza`-`sinai` (the north Sinai coast road, desert with forts) [B, from "The great routes" above: the Syrian desert by Tadmor; the Ways of Horus' forts and wells]. (Since item 11f+ the Ways of Horus run `delta`-`sinai`-`gaza`: "Neighbours on the map" below.)

- <a id="02-terrain-rivers"></a>**Rivers as connections:** the Nile (`delta`-`memphis`-`akhetaten`-`thebes`, and on to Nubia), the Euphrates
  (`carchemish`-`mari`-`sippar`), the Tigris (`nineveh`-`ashur`-`babylon` region) [B, from "Rivers" below]. Downstream is faster than up.
- <a id="02-terrain-plateau"></a>**Plateau:** central Anatolia (`hattusa`, `kanesh`, `tuwanuwa`, `sapinuwa`), long distances, hard winters [B: the winters, "Hard winters" in the environment dossier (Bryce, Letters, p. 180: "before the winter snows set in")].

## The land as it was in 1346 (item 11c+)

Where the land of 1346 differs from today's, as the map draws it:

- <a id="02-gulf-head"></a>**The Gulf's head** in 1346 was an estuary reaching up the Shatt al-Arab to about midway between Basra and Qurna,
  some 140 km below Ur [G: the middle of the published lines]. The sea had come close to Ur by about 4000 BC ("into close
  proximity of the large Sumerian towns"), but "the subsequent advance of the delta was very rapid" (Sanlaville 1989,
  p. 5). Ur was a river port on the Euphrates, "not on the Gulf itself, in much the same way as Basra is not" (Potts
  2009, p. 29). Its ships reached the sea through the Hammar's freshwater marshes, where a Kassite-period settlement stood
  on dry land (Hansman 1978, pp. 58-59). Published lines for the time run from about today's shore (Pournelle 2003) to a
  tidal embayment "directly to the south of Qurna" (Heyvaert and Baeteman 2007, p. 105; Geyer and Sauvage 2020: the coast
  near its present place "probablement au début du Ier millénaire"). Pournelle's 2003 line, today's shore from the late
  Uruk on, gives way to her own later and dated line (2013, the sea near Ur in the third millennium), and Bogemans et al.
  (2017) draw only the Iranian side, so neither is a line for the head in 1346. The dated lines run from about 60 km
  inland of today's shore (Sanlaville's timing, interpolated) to about 130 km (south of Qurna), and the map takes their
  middle, about 95 km inland; Ur, 140 km from it in a straight line, about 150 down the river's bed. Ur on the sea is
  third-millennium only [B]. Before the full check this line read "withdrawn some 150 to 200 km", the line at its height.
- <a id="02-bay-of-miletus"></a>**The bay of Miletus** (the Latmian Gulf) was open sea from Miletus, on its southern shore, past Myus to Heraclea
  under Latmos; the Maeander silted it from Roman times on, and Lake Bafa is what is left of it [A for the gulf, B for
  its shore line in the fourteenth century]. Pleiades, "Ionopolitikos/Latmikos Sinus" (599671): "a coastal body of water in Ionia that disappeared due to silting from the Maeander river"; "Latmos (mountain)" (599753): "along the north shore of the former Latmian Gulf".
- <a id="02-euphrates-old-bed"></a>**The Euphrates ran in its old bed** by Sippar, Babylon (through the city), Nippur's country, Uruk and Ur; it has since
  moved west to its Hindiya course, and Ur and Uruk lie far from it [A for the cities on the river, B for the line between
  them]. Herodotus 1.185 (Macaulay), of Babylon: "the river Euphrates which flows through the midst of their city"; at Ur, Charpin (2018, above): "at the beginning of the 2nd millennium BC, it flowed close to the city, to the south".
- <a id="02-delta-branches"></a>**The Delta's branches**: the New Kingdom Nile reached the sea by several branches, the Pelusiac (by Bubastis to
  Pelusium) on the east, the Tanitic, the Sebennytic and the Canopic on the west, besides the two that remain today
  [A for the branches, nine centuries later; B for their exact courses]. Herodotus 2.17 (Macaulay): "one, which is called the Pelusian mouth, turns towards the East; the second of the ways goes towards the West, and this is called the Canobic mouth", and between them "the Sebennytic mouth"; Pleiades, "Pelousiakos (river)" (727189): "the now-defunct Pelusiac branch of the Nile river".

What a border follows (item 11c+, `scripts/map-borders.ts`): a ridge (the ranges drawn), a great river where it was a
frontier (the Euphrates from Carchemish and Emar upstream, the Tigris between Taidu and Nineveh) and every lesser river,
and a desert's edge; a great river where it is the spine of the provinces along it (the Nile, the Euphrates below Emar,
the Tigris below Nineveh) is crossed within them. Crossings in the rules stay as `Geography` lists them until item 11f.
