# 7. Languages: who spoke what, and where

For "Where each language is spoken", "Who speaks what", "The court languages" and "Reading a tablet" (item 12), and
the starting characters' languages (items 10 and 11). Grades and abbreviations: [README](README.md). Map ids as in
`src/engine/src/model/map.ts`; item 7's new provinces by name.

**In one line:** Akkadian was the written language of diplomacy everywhere, spoken natively only in Babylonia and
Assyria; each land spoke its own tongue (Egyptian, Canaanite, Ugaritic, Hurrian, Luwian, Hittite, Elamite, a Nubian
tongue), and a court read what its scribes could write, which is a separate fact from what its people spoke.

Mitanni's ruling house used Indo-Aryan names, gods (Mitra, Varuna, Indra, the Nasatyas in the Shattiwaza treaty) and
horse terms (Kikkuli's manual) [A], but wrote Hurrian (EA 24) [A] and probably spoke it [B]: Wilhelm, *The Hurrians* (1989), p. 18, has Mitanni's Indo-Aryans "absorbed into this Hurrian-speaking society" and "probably soon abandoned their own language"; pp. 19-20 give the gods (in "two versions of a state treaty") and the horse terms (a "Hittite tract on training horses ... derives from a Mittani expert"). Indo-Aryan is not a living language of the game [B].

known kingdoms [G], except where a grade is given. "Court reads" is what the province's ruler can have read to him

<a id="07-arzawa-court"></a>**Note on Arzawa.** The design makes Luwian Arzawa's court language. Its people spoke Luwian [B], but its one surviving
letter to Egypt (EA 32, the reply to Pharaoh's Hittite letter EA 31) is in Hittite and asks Egypt to go on writing in Hittite [A]:
"The tablets that are brought here always write in Hittite!" (EA 32, Moran). Reading taken: Arzawa's court **speaks** Luwian and **reads** Hittite and Luwian, so Hittite speakers are valued at Apasa.

## Who spoke what (starting skills)

Item 12 sets the numbers; these are what the sources show about kinds of people [B unless marked]:

- <a id="07-kings"></a>**Kings** spoke their own court's language. Tushratta wrote in Akkadian, save one long letter in Hurrian (EA 24) [A]:
  Moran, Introduction, sect. 3: "EA 24 (Hurrian)". Nothing shows a Great King reading Akkadian himself; letters were read aloud to him [B]: EA 32 (Moran) blesses "the scribe who reads this tablet".
- <a id="07-scribes"></a>**Scribes** wrote Akkadian across the whole region, trained in the Babylonian curriculum (lexical lists and the epic
  tablets found at Amarna, Hattusa and Ugarit) [A]. A scribe in Canaan wrote a mixed Akkadian ("Canaano-Akkadian") [A], and his
  native tongue was West Semitic ("Canaanite") [B]. Scribes at Ugarit and Hattusa knew several languages [A]: Van De Mieroop (2nd ed., 2007), p. 169, of Ugarit's scribes: they "also knew how to write and compose in the Akkadian, Hurrian, and Hittite languages".
- <a id="07-envoys"></a>**Envoys** had interpreters where needed [B]: "If needed, an interpreter was at hand to translate his words into his host's tongue" (Bryce, Letters, p. 57). Pharaoh's interpreter Hane is named beside his envoy Mane (EA 21 (Moran): "Hane, my brother's interpre[ter]") [A], and
  the word for interpreter, *targumannu*, is attested (EA 21:25, Rainey: "tar-gu-ma-an[-na-šu]") [A].
- <a id="07-merchants"></a>**Merchants** of Ugarit and Alashiya traded with every coast; several languages are likely [B]: Van De Mieroop (2nd ed., 2007), p. 142, of Ugarit: "Traders from Cyprus, Egypt, Anatolia, and northern Syria resided there".
- <a id="07-soldiers"></a>**Soldiers and their captains** spoke their home tongue: Nubian troops in Canaan [A, that they served: Moran, EA 70 n. 2: "Nubians and Nubian troops are
  mentioned frequently as part of the Egyptian army"]; a *šerdanu* soldier at Byblos (EA 81, 122-123; Rainey on EA 123: "a Serdanu mercenary"), whom Moran's glossary doubts are the Sherden of the Egyptian texts [C].
- <a id="07-detained-envoys"></a>**Detained envoys** were held at foreign courts: Tushratta's messengers Pirissi and Tulubri in Egypt, and Pharaoh's envoy Mane at Tushratta's court in return (EA 28 (Moran): "I am going to detain Mane, [my brother's] messenger") [A]; EA 29 speaks of "my messengers, since 4 years ago" [A]; that they learned the language is the
  design's reading, not attested [G].
