# 9. The letters: relationships, voice and norms

For "A past before the game, then a past made in play" (item 10), the opening relations and states of the realm (item
11+), the council (item 16) and the voice of the requests and primer (item 17). Grades and abbreviations:
[README](README.md). The design document's "Voice of the age" (World and history) already holds the letter formula,
its deviations, the Habiru, the echoes from the archive, oaths, units and season names: this dossier does not repeat
them. It adds the relationships the letters show, a bank of lines by what they do, and the norms with the letters'
own proof that the norms were tools, not limits.

**Style, not strategy.** Everything here shapes how people speak and what they take as normal. None of it says what
anyone wants: every line below was written by someone playing to win.

## Relationships the letters show

These seed starting sympathies, grievances and facts ("A past before the game"). Each is a fact a ruler or character
can know, with its letter; how much it weighs is item 10's to set.

## Lines by what they do

Short quotations and close paraphrases from Moran's translation (EA numbers), for the dossiers, the primer and the
requests to quote or reshape ("A reference to use creatively, not a script"). Marked **(q)** where the words are close
to Moran's, **(p)** where paraphrased.

- **Opening between equals (q):** "Say to Nimmureya, the king of Egypt, my brother: Thus Tushratta, the king of
  Mitanni, your brother. For me all goes well. For you may all go well." (EA 19)
- **Opening from a vassal (q):** "Say to the king, my lord, my Sun: Message of Biridiya. I fall at the feet of the
  king, my lord, my Sun, seven times and seven times." (EA 242-248)
- **Gold talk (q):** "In my brother's country gold is as plentiful as dirt." (EA 19); "Gold in your country is dirt;
  one simply gathers it up." (EA 16)
- **A slight (q):** "Is such a present that of a Great King?" (EA 16)
- **Rank (p):** The Assyrians are my vassals; I did not send them; if you love me, let them do no business and send
  them home empty-handed. (EA 9)
- **Marriage and bluff (p):** Send me a beautiful woman as if she were your daughter; who will say she is not the
  king's? (EA 4, Kadashman-Enlil to Amenhotep III, quoting Egypt's "no daughter of the king of Egypt is given to
  anyone")
- **Distance as an excuse (p):** My brother did not hear I was ill, because the road is long; I asked my own envoy, and
  he said it is long. (EA 7)
- **The begging letter (q):** "Like a bird in a trap, so am I in Byblos." (EA 74, 78-81); "My field is like a woman
  without a husband, for lack of cultivation." (EA 75)
- **The accusation (p):** The land of the king is lost; the Habiru take the king's cities; may the king care for his
  land. (EA 286-288)
- **Protesting loyalty (q/p):** If the king wrote to me, "Put a bronze dagger into your heart and die," how could I not
  carry out the king's order? (EA 254, Labaya, while seizing his neighbours' towns)
- **A delay explained (p):** I could not come, for the king of Hatti is in Nuhashshe and I fear for my cities. (EA
  165-166, Aziru, who was negotiating with Hatti)
- **A neutral's denial (q):** "Men of Lukki, year by year, seize villages in my country." (EA 38)
- **Hard times (q):** "The hand of Nergal is now in my country; it has slain all the men of my country, and there is
  not a single copper-worker." (EA 35)
- **Language (p):** Write to me in Hittite. (EA 32)
- **Precedent (p):** When my ancestor wrote to Egypt, twenty talents of gold were sent; send me as much. (EA 16)

## Norms of the age, and the letters' own proof that they were tools

| Norm | Evidence | The same letters bending it | Grade |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Great Kings are brothers, equals who greet each other's households | EA 1-41 | Tushratta calls Pharaoh brother while accusing him of cheating (EA 27-29); Burna-Buriash calls him brother while threatening to cut ties (EA 7-10) | A |
| Gifts answer gifts, weighed and compared; a greeting gift with every embassy | EA 7, 10, 14, 16, 19 | Gifts weighed in the kiln and complained of; gifts asked for by precedent (EA 16) | A |
| Envoys travel safely and come home with an answer | EA 7, 8, 16 | Envoys detained for years as leverage, on both sides (EA 27-29) | A |
| A king answers for crimes against foreign merchants in his land | EA 8 | Burna-Buriash turns the killings into a demand for silver and a test of Pharaoh's power over Canaan | A |
| Egypt gives no princess abroad | EA 4 | Babylon asks for a pretend princess, calling the bluff | A |
| A vassal swears loyalty, reports, guards his city, supplies the king's troops | EA 51-378 | Every vassal reports his neighbour's treason while taking his towns; Aziru serves Pharaoh in words while going over to Hatti | A |
| Oaths before the gods bind treaties | Beckman, the treaties | Treaties are broken and replaced as power shifts (Ugarit, Amurru, Qadesh) | A |
| A ruler asks after his brother's health and household | EA 7, 17-29 | Not asking is a grievance (EA 7) | A |

**For item 17:** teach the norms as the shared language of the age, the way a demand is made to sound just and a
refusal reasonable; whether to honour one is each ruler's choice ("Style, not strategy": tools of play, never limits
on it).
