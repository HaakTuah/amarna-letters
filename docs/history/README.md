## How sure each claim is

Every claim carries a grade in brackets:

- **[A] Attested.** A primary source says it: a letter (by its EA number), a treaty, an inscription, a find.
- **[B] Accepted.** An inference most scholars share, from the sources cited.
- **[C] Disputed or estimated.** Scholars disagree, or the figure is an estimate from thin evidence; the range or the
  rival readings are given.
- **[S] Silent.** The sources record nothing either way.
- **[G] First guess.** The sources are silent; the builder's reasonable guess, for the game only. Item 18 may tune it.

Where a figure has to become a game number, the dossier says which end of a range to start from.

## One chronology

The game counts years from 1346 BCE (since item 6a). The dossiers use the **Egyptian low chronology** of Hornung, Krauss
and Warburton (2006), the one most handbooks now use, with Akhenaten reigning about 1353-1336 BCE. So 1346 is about
**Akhenaten's year 8**: Akhetaten (Amarna) was founded in his year 5 and the court had settled there by about year 8,
which is when the archive the letters come from fills up [B]. The letters span about thirty years, from late in
Amenhotep III's reign (about his year 30, c. 1360) to early in Tutankhamun's (c. 1332), so not every writer was alive
or in office in 1346; each dossier says where that is in doubt. Mesopotamian dates follow Brinkman (Kassites) and the
Assyrian King List as in the handbooks, which are tied to Egypt's only through the letters themselves, so they carry
about ten years of play [C].

## Sources and abbreviations

- **EA n**: the Amarna letters, numbered as in W. L. Moran, *The Amarna Letters* (Johns Hopkins, 1992), the standard
  English translation; A. F. Rainey, *The El-Amarna Correspondence* (Brill, 2015), the newer edition. The letters
  are the primary source for relationships, voice and norms.
- **Bryce, Kingdom**: T. Bryce, *The Kingdom of the Hittites* (new edition, Oxford, 2005).
- **Bryce, Letters**: T. Bryce, *Letters of the Great Kings of the Ancient Near East* (Routledge, 2003).
- **Cohen and Westbrook**: R. Cohen and R. Westbrook (eds.), *Amarna Diplomacy* (Johns Hopkins, 2000).
- **Podany**: A. Podany, *Brotherhood of Kings* (Oxford, 2010).
- **Liverani**: M. Liverani, *International Relations in the Ancient Near East, 1600-1100 BC* (Palgrave, 2001).
- **Goren et al.**: Y. Goren, I. Finkelstein, N. Na'aman, *Inscribed in Clay: Provenance Study of the Amarna Tablets*
  (Tel Aviv, 2004), and Goren et al., "The location of Alashiya", *American Journal of Archaeology* 107 (2003).
- **Beckman**: G. Beckman, *Hittite Diplomatic Texts* (2nd ed., Scholars Press, 1999): the treaties.
- **Deeds**: the *Deeds of Suppiluliuma*, written by his son Mursili II (Güterbock's edition, 1956).
- **Kemp**: B. Kemp, *The City of Akhenaten and Nefertiti* (Thames and Hudson, 2012).
- **Uluburun**: C. Pulak's reports on the wreck (about 1300 BCE); W. Powell et al., *Science Advances* 8 (2022),
  [doi:10.1126/sciadv.abq3766](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abq3766), and the reply by D. Berger et al., *Frontiers
  in Earth Science* (2023), [pdf](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2023.1211478/pdf).
- **HKW**: E. Hornung, R. Krauss, D. Warburton (eds.), *Ancient Egyptian Chronology* (Brill, 2006).
- **Butzer**: K. Butzer, *Early Hydraulic Civilization in Egypt* (Chicago, 1976): Egypt's population.
- **Van De Mieroop**: M. Van De Mieroop, *A History of the Ancient Near East* (3rd ed., Wiley, 2016).

The dossiers were written from these works as the builder knows them, with the doubtful points checked on the web
where a page was found (linked where it was). A claim with no page linked rests on the work named beside it; any
reader with the books can check it there.

## Style, not strategy

The letters set how people speak and what is normal, never what anyone wants ("Style, not strategy"). Nothing here is a
mission: "Egypt held Canaan" is a starting fact; that Egypt should want to keep it is for its king to decide.
