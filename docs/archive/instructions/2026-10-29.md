36. **07:36 to 07:40**, in the design chat, on how the winner is scored:

    > Both ways we have explored scoring a winner have incentive problems, in my view. The way we did it first, like
    > Paradox, with just the biggest fish: in a game where all players actually optimize towards the goal, as a player
    > should, that automatically biases both for and against the biggest at the start. They are ahead at the
    > beginning, but it also means everyone has an incentive to form a coalition against the one at the top. This
    > doesn't seem to describe the political situation of the period all that well.
    >
    > Then we have what we do now, where we base it on how well someone usually does, and how much better or worse
    > they did now. It sounds good, but has the same coalition problem: someone very small could take the lead, and if
    > everyone goes against them, they don't have the coffers and manpower to stand up to it, unlike Egypt when we
    > scored by just being biggest.
    >
    > So what if we combine the two conditions, and just weight them differently? Do testing towards the end, and find
    > a middle where every power has about the same chance of getting first place, and that's how we weight between
    > the two factors when scoring.

    Then, after Claude weighed a goal of one's own result against one true winner:

    > I want to have a true winner, but I want each player to optimize to get the best position they can. Screwing
    > over the one at the top isn't necessarily always the best start, then, if you want to jump from 8th place to,
    > say, 5th. I think the degree of the coalition problem is acceptable then. But I think this scoring system is the
    > best. Then you can see the other two at the end also, but this is what they are actually scored on and
    > optimizing against.

    Written into "One scoreboard for every power" (Victory) and build order items 11e and 18.
