# The owner's instructions, 30 October 2026

56. **30 October 2026, 01:13 UTC**, in the design chat, the owner agreed to lengthening the age (a median of about 80
    seasons, the cap at about 140 to 160, set now; from 30 script games where no Great King's capital but Assyria's
    fell before the age ended). Written into "Random ending" (Victory), the calendar (The world) and build order item
    17. On a busier opening from history the owner said: "But this game has no casus belli or the like. I have already
    said we should give them the money and armies they had at this time, so they are free to do exactly what was going
    on then. But we cannot dictate that someone is in conflict." (answered in the chat)
57. **30 October 2026, 01:27 UTC**, in the design chat, to an opening set from history (troop positions as the sources
    place them, letters already on the road, memories of the years before) and to the lands east of the Jordan (item
    11f+'s question 1): "1. Absolutely yes to troop positions and that. All that was material, we should represent as
    accurately as possible. But pre-writing letters would be to speak on a player's behalf, so doing that, and adding
    memories, I hesitate to. Any model playing will have some idea of their historical start, so I think they are
    biased sufficiently by that. However, if we have any documented debts at game start, that would be interesting to
    include. Really that would just be representing their finances accurately, which is what we're already trying to
    do. 2. If this is the best read we have of the historical sources, then I approve." Written into "Starting
    positions come from the sources" (The world) and build order item 17+: troop positions and documented debts from
    the sources, no letters or memories written for a court; the Shasu in the Negev and Edom [A], the Bashan
    Ashtaroth's and Gilead Pella's [B], Moab and Ammon outside the game.

62. **30 October 2026, 02:47 UTC**, in the design chat, the owner agreed to all of the design session's takes (what a
    king sees of his people follows the age of his last count, each court starting with the counts its sources
    suggest; a count covers work gangs as well as tax and levy, labour being a tax; five towns corrected in item 17+:
    Akhetaten, Kalavasos, Nesa, Gath for Hebron, Tunip). Written into "Counts as the scribes knew them" and "A census"
    (The world) and items 17+ and 17++. The owner asked too: "What are the consequences then of not counting again
    after a big depopulation event?" (answered in the chat)
63. **30 October 2026, 02:51 UTC**, in the design chat, to the design session's take that levies and work gangs be
    demanded against the last count, so a stale count after a plague squeezes the survivors: "Yes! Though a king
    should be able to be conservative and state how much he wants in tax or labour, if he knows he sits on bad
    information. He shouldn't be forced to squeeze the population. But if he demands too much, there are
    consequences. It impacts the next harvest. Or if he takes too much of the harvest in tax, then there is little
    left for the population." Written into "The king says how much he wants" (The world) and item 17++.
64. **30 October 2026, 02:56-03:05 UTC**, in the design chat, on how a king asks for tax: "Well, he could ask for a
    smaller amount. He knows a percentage will be off, since the number it is applied to is off. So he calculates a
    percentage for a much smaller population, for example. But it is just an estimate from his side then, and a bit of a
    gamble. He could be getting less than what was potential, or still unwittingly squeeze the population." Then: "But
    can he say a third, if he doesn't know what that third comes from? How does he know they paid their tax then?" Then:
    "But isn't that a census, essentially? So if the king hasn't done a census, how can he ask for a percentage? He can
    ask for a percentage of a stale census, but that's still an amount; he doesn't know if it actually aligns with the
    population he has now." Then: "Or does he always state an amount, and how he knows what an appropriate amount is, is
    through a census?" Then: "But should he be able to extract tax as effectively from land further away? Or do the game
    rules already make that more difficult?" The owner agreed to all of the design session's takes (always an amount,
    the census as how he knows, collectors who may skim or squeeze, the census measuring fields; distance through the
    existing frictions alone, checked in item 18). Written into "A census" and "The king says how much he wants" (The
    world) and items 17++ and 18.
65. **30 October 2026, 03:11-03:17 UTC**, in the design chat: "Who are the headmen, and how does he correct for this
    corruption? There must be levers for him to pull, or it is just RNG." Then: "A governor makes sense. But the rest, I
    feel, you are dictating terms I never agreed to. I don't think characters should collect taxes as a rule, unless
    they are governor of a province. My thinking is, a king can either dictate a percentage he wants, or a bulk amount
    of harvest and labour. If he dictates a percentage, but has an old census, then the city can screw him over, and
    claim his numbers are way off. So a percentage always ensures he doesn't squeeze a province, but almost guarantees
    he is squeezed if his census is outdated. Or he can gamble, and just state a fixed amount. Then he gets it, but has
    little way of knowing if it was fair, too much or too little." The owner then agreed to the design session's
    write-up of it. This replaces item 64's "always an amount" and removes the collectors (headmen) who skim or squeeze,
    which the design session had written in beyond what the owner meant. Written into "A census" and "The king says how
    much he wants" (The world) and items 17++ and 18.
