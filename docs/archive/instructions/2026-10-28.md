19. **07:24 and 07:33**, in the design chat, after answering items 9+a's, 9+b's and 10's questions:

    > I feel like the principles answer most of these questions, if one just reasons:
    >
    > - Don't prohibit an action, reflect its real cost.
    > - Information always travels in physics time.
    > - Skills govern the likelihood you can accomplish something, while the rest is up to the character, governed
    >   either by an LLM and a prompt telling them their goal and path, or by attitude traits for scripts.
    > - Deceit is the same as information: it must be gathered to find out if someone lies. You can't skill-check if
    >   someone uses a false name, you must investigate. A forgery can be detected, because it is actually a skill to
    >   evaluate the realness of documents and tablets.
    > - And the rest should follow physics really, the way it was. I don't think armies ever took exactly 1/3; it
    >   probably depended on a lot of factors.

    Then, agreeing to Claude's proposal to write them down as a tenet with five changes: "Code should probably note
    where it has made an assumption, like it does now with questions, so we can check it afterwards."

    Written into the design document as the core tenet "Reason from the pillars" (Overview), with "No menus" and
    "Period first" from the owner's earlier words, and into the builder rules ("Unattended work": every reading listed
    with the principle it rests on, checked by the design session at each milestone). Item 10+a opens with the five
    changes they imply.

24. **16:22**, in the design chat, answering items 11a's and 11a+'s review questions, which Claude had relayed with its
    takes, the owner agreed to all of Claude's recommendations, adding:

    > It seems Claude Code isn't quite getting the idea. Everything has to happen through giving a message and
    > following up on promises. A job doesn't have to be given at court, but it has to be given through a character,
    > somewhere, and the engine only records it if it is by signed tablet. And money paid out as promised is something
    > the king must choose to do. He can automate it, but he doesn't have to pay anything. But if a signed tablet
    > dictated it, then it is recorded that he broke a deal. Doesn't it come across from the principles and all the
    > rest?

    the reviewer reads with the diff (`CLAUDE.md`, "Starting a task" and "Models, effort and helpers"); the answers
    open item 11+.

25. **16:37, 16:43 and 16:45**, in the design chat, after the answers to items 11a and 11a+:

    > As for jobs, didn't we agree they were just errands, to keep it simple? Do this, this and this, by this time;
    > this is the money up front and this is the money after completion. Since almost every task can be submitted like
    > that. Meanwhile governorship, I guess, must be more of offering a privilege.
    >
    > And every piece of information has to travel. Imagine the sun disappearing. The earth doesn't know until the last
    > light has made its way here.
    >
    > And every action is possible (within the confines of what's possible for us to enable): a king may exercise as
    > horrendous an action as he wants. The consequences are not hardcoded, but follow from what that does to the

    >
    > And prices aren't fixed, characters negotiate them. And for wares it is supply and demand.
    >
    > And food can rot; there are no special conditions from being in a farmer's granary, it doesn't magically become

    >
    > Freedom to do, consequences that emerge.

    privileges." And, asking for all of it to be written: "Have we corrected and fixed all records then, so this will
