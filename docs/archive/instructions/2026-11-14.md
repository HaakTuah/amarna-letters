# The owner's instructions: 14 November 2026

250. **14 November 2026, 09:14 UTC**, in the design chat, after a small open model that runs in a browser was planned
     as a free player and test seat for another of the owner's games:

    > Why don't we use a model like this for amarna also?

    Told: a free model removes the first reason behind "No model games until the build order is done" (weekly usage),
    not the second (a weak model's play says little about the finished game); a small, literal model is a good
    canary for "Requests are rules too", since a field that implies something untrue misleads it first; it plugs into
    the simulation kit's file seats in place of the stub replier; and the rule is marked a safety rule in `AGENTS.md`,
    so it changes only on the owner's word. The wording proposed: no model games with paid models until the build
    order is done; a free local model may answer requests to check the requests and the plumbing, never as a measure
    of play.

    > Agreed, expertly. And we can use the best free model that can run inna browser, wherever it has utility, testing
    > wise, and maybe in the game also, but we'll have to see about the latter. Do you concur, expert?

    Written into the design document's builder rules (Build order tab, revision 379) as "No model games with paid
    models until the build order is done", with the best free model that runs in a browser and passes the licence
    check as its one exception, for testing wherever it helps; whether it plays in the game itself is decided later.

251. **14 November 2026, from 10:03 UTC**, in the design chat, after being told that the core would say that each
     stage of a game's build ends in a game someone can play from start to finish and enjoy:

    > Yes. But the bigger problem is the ui in amarna right now. It is a mess. No way you can play a game this complex
    > when you can't even tell what is going on on the screen.

    Told, after five seasons played as Egypt on the built app: the orders tab is one scroll about 15,000 pixels tall,
    with 63 drop-downs, about 200 buttons and 100 fields and the button that ends the season at its foot; the season
    and inbox tabs list every move in the world; nine tabs run off the panel; the standings cannot say whether a king
    is winning; the design document planned the screens of watching and none of playing. Proposed: a screen that
    answers four questions in order (what happened to me, what waits for my answer, what I can do, whether I am
    winning), actions on the thing selected, the map doing the showing, the rest behind one button, built first for a
    short first game of three kings.

    > Agreed, expertly! I think civ 5-6 solved it best for a game like ours, where it shows what hasn't received an
    > order maybe? Yet we are a fusion of civ's turn-based, and paradoxes' real-time. So you don't give constant orders
    > here like in civ, I imagine. So we must have our own system of sorts that fits this game. But looking at civ and
    > paradox does it, and adapting a fusion of it for ours, which also accounts for delay and that we simply just
    > often fully know the state, must be the goal? What is your take, expert?

    Told: from Civilization, the seal button that knows what waits (counting only what is new or finished, since
    standing orders carry everything on) and orders on the thing; from Paradox's games, the alert bar, the top bar
    and the player's choice of what wakes the seat; and of the game's own, the in-flight list of everything set moving
    and when it lands, and the age on every fact, since here orders and news take seasons.

    > These rules about playability must all equally apply to what a model seat sees in any game. All players must
    > play on as equal terms when it comes to readability of the game, as possible. Agreed, expert?

    Written into the design document as "The player's screen" (Interface tab, revision 51), with "A model seat reads
    the same": a model's request carries the same brief, waiting list, in-flight list, ages and actions that apply,
    in the same order, both drawn from one source with a test that fails where they differ; and into the build order
    as item 18b, a lane beside item 18 (Build order tab, revision 381). The core's "Readable by every player" was
    widened to what the computer players are shown, in every game.
