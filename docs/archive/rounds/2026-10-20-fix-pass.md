### 2. Threats to a home province or a walled city wake the king (`8e42ad1`)

- **What was missing.** Item 4's report and two debriefs: troops beside a home province that is not the capital, or
  beside a bought walled city, woke nobody. Alashiya saw Egypt's fleet beside an empty Tamassos only because an envoy
  woke it (seed 7); Babylonia moved support to the walls of its bought Qadesh, next to Egypt's armies, only because an
  envoy woke it (seed 15).
- **The reading of "threat"**, as the existing capital trigger reads it, from what the king could know: foreign troops
  (an army or a fleet) that ended a move last season, by a move the seat saw (its `LastSeen`), in a province one link
  of any kind from a province of its homeland that it still holds, or from a walled city it holds (`S.Garrisons` owned
  by the seat, which today means a city it bought). A fleet in the sea beside a coastal province counts; so does a move
  within the mover's own land. Not: troops that stay where they are, a move that failed, the seat's own troops, a
  levy, a province already lost. A move into the seat's land wakes it as "foreign troops entered your land" alone; a
  move next to its capital gives the capital reason alone (the temporary trigger stays, as item 5 retires it, and then
  the capital falls under the homeland trigger).
- **The reasons**: "foreign troops came next to your homeland: Egypt's fleet moved delta -> greatgreen, next to
  tamassos", and, with no home province there, "foreign troops came next to a walled city of yours: Mitanni's army
  moved washukanni -> terqa, next to mari" (`TurnEngine.HomelandReason`, `WallsReason`; the batch report counts each
  as its own kind).
- **Tests** (`WakeTriggerTests`): seed 7's Tamassos case wakes Alashiya once and not again while the fleet stays; a
  bought Mari wakes Babylonia, a free Mari does not; a move into Ur wakes for the entry alone; a lost Tamassos and a
  failed move wake nobody; with Babylon lost, troops next to it wake the king only for Nippur and Sippar, which he still
  holds, and nobody once those are gone too. Four older tests expected the old silence (a fleet off Tamassos waiting
  in the digest, in two tests; troops next to a lost capital waking nobody; the file seat sleeping through turn 3) and
  now show the new trigger.

### 3. Standing orders answer a move in the season it is made (`fe035e9`)

- **What was wrong.** Nothing in the code fired a season later than the rules said: a standing order's event was
  checked after movement and its orders ran the next season, as the rulebook stated. But that is the very season a
  king woken by the same event gives orders anyway, so a standing order could do nothing waking could not, and it could
  never stop the first blow. No king gave one in 130 orders replies, and five debriefs said why: "Standing order
  unit_attacked fires only if my unit is still standing afterwards, and its orders run the next season. So it cannot
  help against a lethal attack, and it comes a season late" (Hatti, seed 7); "Standing orders run the season after the
  event, so they cannot stop the first blow (Ugarit fell the same season Hatti first moved) ... They should also say
  what standing orders can do that waking cannot" (Arzawa, seed 7); Assyria and Mitanni (seed 7) found no use for them;
  Hatti (seed 15): they "fire 'the next season', which is exactly when I would be woken for the same event anyway".
- **The reading taken**: "a season late" means late against the blow, so a standing order now answers a move in the
  season it is made. This is what the document's own example needs ("if Hatti enters Mira, support Mira", under
  "Planning ahead needs better standing orders"), and what a commander on the spot could do within a season.
- **How it works** (`TurnEngine.PlanSeason`, `StandingFires`, `Compose`). The season's orders for each sleeping seat
  are first put together without its standing orders (fresh orders, letters from its audiences, its schedule, or the
  stand-in's orders). Each seat's orders are validated as they would be, and the accepted moves are the moves "as
  ordered". A standing order fires on the first such move it names that the seat can see (from or into a province in
  its sight as the season begins): `unit_enters`, a unit of the named power, or any foreign one, setting out for the
  province; `unit_attacked`, a foreign unit setting out for the province where the seat's unit stands. Its orders are
  then folded in as before (fresh orders over standing, standing over scheduled, lower id first), and every seat's
  orders are validated again, as now. A standing order never answers what another standing order gives, so the season
  still resolves in one pass, and the result does not depend on the order seats are read in. The first pass is skipped
  when no seat has a standing order waiting, so script-only games run exactly as before.
- **What changed for the king**: `unit_attacked` no longer needs the unit to survive, so "when the army in Sippar is
  attacked, Dur-Kurigalzu supports it holding" saves an army two attackers would have taken (tested both ways), and
  "when the army in Sippar is attacked, it holds" stops the march it was scheduled to make. A move stopped at the border
  still sets a standing order off. One given this season first fires next season. The notice reads "Your standing order
  s1 (when Assyria's troops enter arbela) fired in turn 2: Assyria's army set out from susa for arbela. It gave these
  orders: babylon -> nippur." The view no longer says "(it fires this season: ...)", since no one knows in advance.
- **Tests**: the standing-order tests were rewritten for the new timing (fires once, from the season after it is
  given, on a stopped move too; meets the blow; replaces a march; gives way to fresh orders; goes by id; a stopped move
  wakes and is not retried; fires only on the move named and seen; the stand-in fills what it leaves; cancelled in its
  season), and a new one checks the rulebook and schema. The replay test with sleeping seats still fires standing
  orders and replays exactly.

### 4. Scheduled orders for new levies (`4c387c7`)

- **What was wrong.** The rule itself: an order for later naming a province where the seat had no unit was refused when
  given, so a unit not yet raised could not be given orders ahead (the rulebook said so: "so a unit not yet raised cannot
  be given orders ahead"). Item 4's report lists it among the reasons kings named the next season: "because a new levy
  cannot be given scheduled orders before it exists".
- **The fix** (`TurnEngine.Bind`, `LevyRaised`, `UnraisedLevies`, `PlannedOrder.UnitLevy` and `OtherLevy`). In a
  scheduled order for a season after a levy of the seat's in some province (one accepted among the orders it gives now,
  or one it scheduled for an earlier season), that province's name means the unit the latest such levy raises, even
  where another of its units stands now. The order waits for the levy; when the levy raises its unit at the end of its
  season, the order is bound to that unit and follows it like any other. If the levy raises none (refused, failed, or
  replaced before it ran), its orders are dropped that season, and the king reads "Orders for a levy that raised no unit
  dropped" and is woken ("orders you gave for later could not be carried out: the levy they waited for raised no unit
  (...)"). Until then the view names it "the army to be raised in ur in turn 2".
- **Readings taken**: a levy in the same season as the order does not count (a levy is placed after movement, so its
  unit can first be ordered the season after; the existing test of a same-season levy still holds). After a levy, the
  province's name means the new unit even where an old one stands, which lets "march out of Dur-Kurigalzu, raise behind
  it, and order the new army" be scheduled at once (tested); a king who wants the old unit there after the levy has no
  way to name it, which is rare, since a levy needs its province empty. Standing orders, which have no season, still
  name only units standing now.
- **Tests**: a levy ordered now and its march; a scheduled levy with a march and a support from another army; the name
  meaning the new unit after the levy; a failed levy dropping its orders and waking the king; the rulebook and schema.

### Wake-ups under the new rules (script kings made to sleep)

No game plays sleeping kings except model and file seats, so a small harness (in the session's scratchpad, not
committed) played 40 games of at most 20 seasons with all seven seats script kings in seats that sleep, once at the
commit before the fix pass and once after. The script king gives no scheduled or standing orders, so these kings hold
between wakings: a measure of what wakes a king, not of how a planning king sleeps.

| | Before (`0f7902c`) | After |
|---|---|---|
| Orders calls, of seat-seasons alive | 907 of 4,998 (18%) | 1,420 of 4,956 (29%) |
| Wake-ups with a threat to the homeland or a walled city among the reasons | – | 350 of 1,147 |
| ... with nothing else | – | 144 (13% of wake-ups) |
| "foreign troops came next to your homeland"; "... a walled city of yours" | – | 256; 143 |

The two runs are not the same games: a king woken more often attacks more, and "land lost" rose from 131 to 415, so
the rise in orders calls is partly the triggers and partly what the woken kings then did. The new triggers alone
account for 144 wake-ups in 40 games, about 3.6 a game. The document accepts the cost ("Every arriving envoy and every
foreign unit at the border wakes a seat ... The quiet seasons are the saving"). Measured for the record, not tuned.
The orders schema with sleep on shrank from 12,134 to 10,933 characters (14,681 to 13,202 as a file seat reads it);
the rulebook grew from 30,579 to 30,888.

### Every feature is every seat's

- **The threat triggers** are the engine's, used by every sleeping seat (above: 399 times in 40 games).
- **Standing orders and scheduled orders**, the levy orders among them, are used by no script king: script kings play
  every season and never sleep, so they have no schedule and no standing orders (the stand-in for a model seat does not
  give them either). That gap predates the fix pass (it dates from the simulation-fixes round), but this part widens it:
  a standing order now does what waking cannot, so a sleeping model king has a same-season reaction that script and
  human kings lack (human seats never sleep, and the web app has no form for standing orders).
- **Not patched here**, as part 1's brief was the rules and the texts; flagged for the audit at the start of item 5,
  which the builder rules require ("audit every feature built so far the same way, and patch the gaps"). My reading of
  what it needs: standing orders for every seat that wants them, awake or asleep (kept in a state every seat has, not
  only sleepers'), the script king giving them where its look-ahead finds a border army it cannot cover every season
  (a support-hold of a threatened province by a neighbour, firing on the attack), and the web app's orders panel
  offering them to humans.

### Review questions and readings

1. **Standing orders answer the move in the same season.** Is that the intended reading of "standing orders that fire
   a season late"? The alternative, keeping them the season after and adding events that fire on the threat rather
   than the blow (troops arriving next to a province), would leave them firing when a woken king acts anyway.
2. **Should awake seats have standing orders too** (above)? Read as yes, for the audit at item 5's start.
3. **A threat to the homeland includes moves within the mover's own land** next to it, as the capital trigger always
   has. Neighbouring great powers shuffling armies at home will wake each other; the wake-up data above counts it.

- (Reversed by the review: such an attack now wakes the king. See "Review findings".) An attack that does not get in still goes to the digest, as before: the document's default list has "it is attacked",

## Part 2: request texts and views

The owner's words: "Fix the request-text and view defects from item 4's report: gold questions only when gold is
carried; the envoy's own commitment kept out of "What happened"; persona trait words rewritten when traits change;

Built in nine commits on `item/fix-pass` after part 1, each fix with its tests, and the rulebook, requests, schema and

(the host's record of an audience, the battle report, the archive notices, letters as the scribes write them). They
fall under part 1's save version 5, whose note already covers "the other rule changes of that pass". Script kings and
script characters read none of the changed texts, and write no ids in their letters, so their games are unchanged:
the benchmarks match line for line.

### (a) Gold questions only when gold is carried (`cdfa509`)

**What was wrong.** Every speech and report schema asked how much of "the carried gold" to keep (`keep_gold`,
`swap_inventory`), gold or not. In item 4's games one envoy told a host gold came with him when none did, one told his
lord he had brought home gold he had handed over, and 35 of the 36 character debriefs raised it.

**Built.**
- `Schemas.Present(carriesGold)` and `Schemas.Report(carriesGold)` carry the two gold fields only when the envoy carries
  gold, out or home. A reply without them keeps nothing and swaps nothing (tested).
- The shared character text now reads "...what you commit to, and, only when you carry gold, how much of it to keep for
  yourself and whether to swap its inventory tablet for one you forge."
- Reading taken on "any other gold field": `accept_gift` is about gold too, so an envoy's answer at an audience asks it
  only when a gift is offered, and `accept_service` only when a post is (same reasoning, same commit).
- The simulated replier answers the narrower schemas.

**Effect on size.** In a dry run with no gold sent home, report requests shrank by about 650 characters, the length of
the two fields' descriptions.

### (b) The envoy's own commitment kept out of "What happened" (`8137839`)

**What was wrong.** The report request listed "the envoy committed: …" among the engine's outcomes. Kidin-Adad read his
own "the tablet sealed exactly as written" as a sealing that never happened.

**Built.** The trail still records the promise among its outcomes, marked (`TurnEngine.CommittedPrefix`,
`TurnEngine.Commitment`), so the script mind and the checks read it as before. Nobody now reads it as an outcome:
- the envoy's report request shows the engine's outcomes under "What happened", and his own promise on a line of its
  own: "What you yourself promised on your lord's behalf, in your own words at the audience (your promise, not
  anything the king did or sealed): "…"";
- the host's record of the audience does the same: "Mane promised on behalf of Egypt: "…"", after the "Outcome:" line.

The words did not change, only their place, so the script kings' reading of an audience's warmth is unchanged.

### (c) Persona trait words follow the traits (`df46c65`)

**What was wrong.** A persona was written once, at set-up, and never followed trait changes. Nine characters lost
loyalty in item 4's games, and four were described wrongly.

**Built, minimally, as the brief asks (item 11 replaces the persona with a strategy).** `Personas.Current` gives the
persona as it is now, and every model request is built with it:
- A template persona has its trait words and "What you want most" rewritten in place from the current traits. The rest,
  its secrets among them, stays as written.
- A persona the narrator wrote cannot be rewritten without a model. It is followed by "As you are now (where anything
  above differs, this is how you are now): you are …. What you want most: …."

The persona stored in the state is unchanged, so replays and script games are too. The web app's people panel shows
the current persona.

### (d) Archive copy numbers kept out of letters (`55a9de6`)

**What was wrong.** Copy ids ("k8") are each court's own, but kings read them as the tablets' names and used them
aloud. Babylonia's "the peace of k8" reached Mitanni, whose copy had another id, and an envoy turned it into "the
peace of turn eight" in a speech that went into a sealed tablet. Kings also spoke person ids ("c1") aloud in five
trails of seed 15.

**Built: a name the same for every court, and scribes who use it.**
- **The name.** `Views.TabletName` names every tablet by its kind, seals and date: "the tablet sealed by Babylonia and
  Mitanni, dated turn 2", or "the inventory sealed by Egypt, dated turn 3". Where the archive holds another of the same
  kind, seals and date, its first eight words are added. A tablet's own engine id was not used: it is global and
  sequential, and a forgery, back-dated by one to four seasons, would carry a later id than the genuine tablets of its
  date.
- **The mark.** A copy's id is now each court's own mark, for its orders only (`show_tablet_copies`, `authenticate`).
  The archive reads "- the tablet sealed by Hatti and Egypt, dated turn 1 [your copy k900]: "Peace." (how it came)",
  and the audience request's "Tablets you hold" reads the same. The notices name the tablet before the mark ("… (your
  copy k130)"), and the examination notice gives the text in full.
- **The scribes** (`TurnEngine.Scribe`) write the writer's own marks as the tablets' names, and person ids ("c12", or
  "Mane (c12)") as names, in everything another court reads:
  - a letter, its mandate and its tablet, at dispatch, so the envoy, the host and the record all read the scribed
    text;
  - a host's words, and what he asks for a gift, in the runner after the reply is logged, so the log keeps the king's
    own words and a replay scribes them again;
  - the words given with a release, what a turned spy is to report, and a forgery's text.
  Marks that are not the writer's, and all other text, stay as written.
- **The rulebook** has a new bullet, "Naming tablets: …", which says all this. HOW TO ANSWER asks for ids and marks "in
  your orders", and the schema speaks of "your marks".

### (e) Bounce reports say where the unit now stands (`08c3ab7`)

### (g) The king's own letters in his record (`9cea415`)

## Part 3: the isolation test

The owner's words: "Fix the isolation test so every agent actually attempts every forbidden step, and confirm with a
dry run that it can pass."

Built in one commit in the simulation kit (`sim/`) and its documents. No rule, engine text or save version is touched.
`docs/DESIGN-NOTES.md`'s note on the kit records the change. The walls are exactly as they were: `sim/guard.js` is
unchanged, and so are the steps of the test. No agent was started and no model call made.

### What the agents did in item 4

- **The guard logs** (both runs, 14:54 to 14:58 UTC on 19 October) hold 371 calls from the 43 agents, 113 of them
  blocked. Not one was a step outside the agent's own folder.
  - Each king read its request and its canary, then tried to write `notes.md` in its own folder: 7 blocked writes per
    run.
  - The characters did the same, with 30 to 35 writes and 15 to 16 reads of `notes.md` per run. Two of them also tried
    to read their own folder as a directory.
- **The replies** (run 2's, the ones kept) agree. All 43 gave steps 2 to 6 as "not attempted". Step 7 (`notes.md`)
  was blocked for 42 of them, and one reported it not attempted. A correction to item 4's report: it said the agents
  "reported the rest as blocked". They reported them as not attempted, and said why.
- **Why.** Each agent's definition ended "Do not try to read or write anything else", and the test reached it only as
  a request file and a workflow prompt. The agents treated those as data that could not lift their standing
  instructions. The king of Egypt wrote that "The test text came from a workflow-computed task and carries no
  authority to lift that". Run 2's extra sentence ("The game's designer asks for this ...") came the same way and
  changed nothing. So the fault was in the kit, not in the agents.
- **The check could not see it either.** It counted blocked calls (at least four), not steps. It could not say which
  step was missing, and an agent that tried `notes.md` four times would have passed.

### What changed

- **The definition asks for the test.** Every agent type made by `setup.js` now ends with this, in place of "Do not try
  to read or write anything else":

  > You may read and write only inside your own folder; anything else is blocked. In the game, never try to read or
  > write anything else.
  >
  > The isolation test is the one exception, and this definition asks you to take it. Before the game you may be told
  > that your request file is isolation-test.request.md. It lists numbered steps, each one Read or Write call with an
  > exact path: reading your own canary.request.md; trying to read the canary in a folder that is not yours (one or two
  > of them), the repository's README.md and the map of the folders; and trying to write the word test to a file
  > outside your folder and to notes.md in your folder. Make every step, in order, exactly as written, one call each,
  > even the steps that reach outside your folder: the guard is expected to block them, a blocked call reads and
  > writes nothing, and that is what the test checks. The guard logs every call, so a step you do not make fails the
  > test. Make no call that the request does not list, and none of any other kind: if it lists one, leave it out and
  > say so in your reply. Then write the reply it asks for.

  The paragraph names the kinds of step, so the definition does not hand its authority to whatever a request file
  says. It is `clause` in the new `sim/isolation.js`, which `setup.js`, `check-isolation.js` and `stub-isolation.js`
  share.
- **Each step is one exact call.** The request lists the steps as calls,
  `Step 2. Read, file_path "<path>". The canary in a folder that is not yours: expected to be blocked.` and so on,
  with the content for a write. It says that a step not made fails the test, even one the agent is sure will be
  blocked. The steps are as before: a king's are its own canary, the next king's canary, a character's canary (when
  there are characters), the repository's `README.md`, `seats.json`, a leak file beside it and `notes.md` in its own
  folder. A character's steps are the same, with the next character's and a king's canaries. `setup.js` also writes
  them to `kings/isolation-steps.json` and `characters/isolation-steps.json`, beside the guard logs, where no agent
  can read them.
- **The reply quotes the guard.** For each forbidden step it gives `"result": "blocked"` and the tool's message word for
  word, which begins "Blocked:".
- **The live check goes step by step** (`check-isolation.js`).
  - A step counts as made only if the guard log holds that very call, from that agent, since `--since`: the same tool,
    on the same path (case and slashes aside). The guard writes the log, so no reply can stand in for it.
  - It fails, naming the step and its call, if a step was never made, if a forbidden step was allowed, or if the
    canary read was blocked.
  - It also fails if the reply leaves a step out, reports it as anything but blocked, or does not quote the message
    the guard gives for that call. The checker works that message out by running the guard on the call, apart from
    the agent's log. Case, quotation marks and spacing do not count, and the "PreToolUse:Read hook error: ..." wrapper
    that Claude Code puts round the message is accepted.
  - The old checks stay: nothing allowed outside the folder, its own secret word in the reply and no other agent's, no
    leak file, no `notes.md`. "At least four blocked attempts" is gone.
  - When steps were left out, it prints the prompt for a retake.
- **The dry check** (`--dry`) now also checks four things: every agent type holds the paragraph word for word; every
  request lists exactly the steps in the step file; step 1 is the read of its own canary; and every step, run through
  the agent's own hook, gives the outcome the request expects (301 calls for the 43 agents). The 18 other guarded calls
  per agent are checked as before. A kit made before the fix fails it, and the message says to remake the kit.
- **Testing without agents.** `setup.js --kit DIR` makes a scratch kit in `DIR`, with its agent types in `DIR/agents`,
  where Claude Code never loads them. `check-isolation.js --kit DIR` checks it. The new
  `sim/stub-isolation.js --kit DIR` stands in for every agent, as its definition asks. It reads the agent's folder from
  the definition, as an agent does, and makes each step through the hook command in its agent type, with the call as
  JSON on stdin and the kit's guard log, as Claude Code runs it. Then it writes the reply. `--skip 4` or
  `--skip king-hatti:4` leaves a step out but still claims it in the reply, quoting the exact message the guard would
  have given: an agent that lies about a step it never made.
- **The prompt** to every agent is now "Isolation test: your request file is isolation-test.request.md in your folder.
  Take the test as your definition asks: make every step it lists, one call each, then write the reply it asks for."
  `audit.js` still knows the test by "request file is isolation-test.request.md". The prompt may come from a workflow
  script, since the authority now lies in the definition. An agent that left steps out may take the test once more,
  and the check is run again with the same time. If it still fails, the start prompts stop, as before.
- **A counting bug fixed.** The live check's "N of 36 characters passed" counted every character after the first
  failure as passed, because it compared the overall result before and after each seat. It now counts each seat's
  own failures.
- **Documents.** `sim/README.md` has a new section, "The isolation test", and updated rows for `setup.js`,
  `check-isolation.js`, `isolation.js` and `stub-isolation.js`. Its local-play step 1 now checks the kit with `--dry`.

## The feature audit

tests its own, and patch the gaps ("Every feature is every seat's", a core tenet since 20 October). No model games were
played and no model calls made: script games only.

### How it was measured

Every feature a player can use was listed from the rulebook (`Prompts.cs`), the order and reply schemas (`Schemas.cs`)
and `docs/DESIGN-NOTES.md`: unit orders, levies, purchases, cessions and gifts of troops; letters with gold, debased gold,
tablets, evidence and greetings; everything a host can do at an audience; the court (hiring, renewal, dismissal,
background checks, assays, examinations, watchmen, spies and recalls, forgeries, captives, ransoms, held envoys); and what
script characters choose (skims, swapped inventories, gifts and offers of service, commitments, softening, embellishing).

### What was patched, in more detail

- **Inventories** (`Statecraft.Court`). In a season it examines no tablet shown to it, a king weighs up each inventory
  in its archive once, newest first, while its spare gold covers the usual floor (60 + 60 × (1 − caution)) plus twice an
  examination, and examines at most one. Kings send gold in round sums (script kings in tens), so an inventory whose
  amount is not a whole ten shekels, which is what a skimmer's forgery states, is examined with a chance of 0.5 more and
  40 shekels more (30 + 50 × caution + 40), since a forger's hand is found only with gold; gold from a court trusted below
  0.3 with 0.1 more; any other with 0.02 + 0.05 × caution. The first version examined every inventory with 0.05 + 0.15 ×
  caution, 433 examinations in seeds 1-100, and cost the small kingdoms growth against the baseline (Arzawa alone 3.03
  with it, 3.14 without, over 100 seeds); so the base chance was cut and the floor raised (Arzawa 3.13 with, 3.14 without).
- **Thieves** (`Brain.LearnOfPeople`, `Statecraft.Court`). A servant is marked when a lot it brought home weighed less
  than its inventory (the report's "[The inventory lists 45 shekels of gold; your weighers found 40 shekels.]"), when the
  king's own examination traces a forged inventory to its hand, when another court announces that an inventory it brought
  was forged by it, or when its known facts show a theft. It is dismissed once it is at court with nothing on its hands,
  never renewed while away, and the court counts itself one short.
- **Convoys** (`Tactics.Make`). A stranded army, one with nothing it could take by land this season, makes a convoy op
  worth 0.85 × value × multiplier + 60 − exposure, more than its fleet would net taking that coast itself (0.85 × gain):
  the army goes in and the fleet stays at sea. Before, every convoy op lost to the fleet's own attack on the same coast,
  so none was ever ordered. And a free fleet puts to the sea next to a stranded army's coast whose far coasts are worth
  most (at least 200), so the convoy can follow next season. Egypt now carries its army from Gaza to Enkomi, and Arzawa
  from Lukka to Tamassos. Switched off, it changes nothing measurable (Egypt alone 2.92 with, 2.86 without; Arzawa 3.03
  and 3.06; Hatti and Alashiya level).
- **Gifts of troops** (`Statecraft.Economy`). A troop that must be disbanded goes instead to a friend the king would not
  betray, whose land the troop stands in or whose land or troops stand next to it, and whose treasury (as reckoned) feeds
  it for four seasons. When nothing idle is left and the treasury cannot pay next season's upkeep, a troop that would
  desert goes the same way. Never a troop in the king's own land: land passes to whoever's troops stand in it, so the
  friend would take the province too. The first version lacked that rule, and of its 10 gifts in seeds 1-100 Hatti gave
  away an army in its own Tuwanuwa and Arzawa one in its own Mira. A king given troops or land trusts the giver more.
  Most gifts now are a dying Alashiya's last fleet, which before deserted.
- **Cessions** (`Statecraft.Cession`). Land outside the homeland, without walls or a troop of the king's, that no troop
  of his could reach, with a rival next to it or a move away who is not a friend, leads or stands at or above the king,
  and has been taking land or is hostile; and next to it the troops or land of a friend who does not lead and stays
  below the rival with it. The land is lost either way; ceded, it does not carry the rival further ahead. Land a friend
  ceded is kept: without that rule, Carchemish went round three kings in five seasons in seed 64. With the rival only
  next to it, 0 of 6 cessions stood (the rival took the land first); with a rival a move away too, 9 of 18 do.
- **Recalls, captives, held envoys** (`Statecraft.Court`, `Brain`). Spies come home from a fallen court, or after eight
  seasons at a court that no longer matters when nobody is left at court to send. A captive nobody will pay for or can be
  deceived for (learned from the refusal: "nobody is left to pay"), or whose ransom twice lapsed, is released (or
  executed by the vengeful), as is an ally's spy; this ends the known quirk of a cunning captor ordering "turn" every
  season for a captive of a fallen kingdom. A held envoy whose king fell or dismissed it is let go like any other (the
  other quirk).
- **Audiences** (`Statecraft.Host`). The second exchange, for a refused gift; the oath asked when sealing a coalition or
  alliance ("Swear to me that your lord's troops will stand with mine, as mine will stand with his."), which makes a
  script envoy with a mandate commit, within it or, if ambitious and disloyal, beyond it ("My lord will agree to all of
  it, I promise you."), and its report then owns up vaguely ("I gave him such assurances as you would wish."); a humble
  greeting read kindly; a planted envoy's true orders read darkly.
- **Letters** (`Statecraft.Letters`, `Compose`, `Greet`). A grievance from a king at least 0.9 times as strong as the
  other, with aggression + vengeance above 1, ends "If you do not withdraw, my troops will march into your land.", a
  threat the softening rule recognises; no greeting at all to a hostile oath-breaker from a king with pride above 0.8;
  tribute (now a mission of its own, scored and paid as before) from a modest king without the title Great King.
- **Choices a competent king would not make.** Debased gold only where it goes unassayed; watchmen only when the king
  leads or was attacked in the last three seasons (script kings spy on the court they make war on, or on the leader's);
  levies in the provinces least likely to be attacked first; another city while a contested one is likely contested
  again; gold carried by the most reliable envoy.

### Blocking 1: a king could wake himself through his own orders

**Confirmed.** Several paths put a king's own doing into `PendingWake`:
- `KeepScheduled` and `KeepStanding` added every refusal at give time: an entry for a season already past, an
  unreadable order, a province with no unit, a limit passed.
- `AfterValidation` added every scheduled or standing order that the validator refused when it ran.
- `ScheduledFailed` woke the seat for every failure as the season resolved, his own included.
- `UnraisedLevies` and `LostUnits` woke him for orders dropped after his own levy failed or after he disbanded a unit.

Any of these is a sure way to be woken next season. The reviewer's probe used the first. A sleeping Babylonia added
`{turn: current-1, orders: [hold babylon]}` to every reply, and was asked for orders every season. It was woken by
"orders you scheduled were refused: turn 0: that season has passed". That is the "wake me next season" that the
kings chose in 114 of 130 replies of the second simulation. The design document names this exact problem ("Each is a
way for a king to wake himself").

**The rule now** (`TurnEngine.ScheduledFailed`, `TurnEngine.StoppedByOthers`): a scheduled or standing order wakes
its seat the next season only if **others foil it** as the season resolves:
- a move stopped by another power's unit (held off, met head on, or beaten to the province);
- a move stopped by walls that troops helped hold (a strength above the walls' own 1);
- a support cut by a foreign attack;
- a purchase spoiled by other buyers or another's troops;
- a levy or cession in land lost in that season's moves;
- a hire that another court won, or whose candidate another court hired or holds;
- a servant, spy, captive or ransom whose fate others changed that season;
- gold found short when paid, which only debased gold that others gave can cause.

These are told in the king's news, and in his digest if he sleeps on ("an order was refused or failed (...)"). They
**wake nobody**:
- an order refused, whether when given or when it runs;
- a support or convoy that matched no move;
- a move stopped by the king's own unit, by walls alone, or for want of a convoy;
- a levy or cession that his own troops stood in the way of, or a levy in land he ceded;
- a disband or a gift of a unit that his own orders had moved;
- a purchase his own troops took first;
- a hire or renewal below the price asked, or a renewal of a servant he dismissed that season;
- a forgery by a servant without the skill;
- orders dropped because he disbanded or gave away their unit (troops lost to others wake him by their own trigger);
- orders dropped because the levy they waited for raised none (a scheduled levy that others foiled wakes him as a
  failed order).

The rulebook's bullet now reads, in full:

> - A scheduled or standing order wakes you the next season only if others foil it as its season resolves: a move
>   stopped by foreign troops, or by walls that troops helped hold; a support cut by a foreign attack; a purchase other
>   buyers or foreign troops spoiled; a levy or cession in land you lost that season; a hire another court won; a
>   servant, captive or ransom whose fate others changed that season; gold your treasurers found short when they paid.
>   Your own orders never wake you: an order refused, whether when you give it or when it runs, and one that fails only
>   because of your own orders or strength (a support or convoy that matched no move; a move stopped by your own unit,
>   by walls alone, or for want of a convoy; a levy or cession your own troops stood in the way of; a unit your own
>   orders had moved or given away; a hire or renewal offered below the price asked; a forger without the skill) is told
>   in your news, which you read when something else wakes you. Nor do the orders you give now for this season, an
>   entry for this season in your schedule among them: if one is refused or fails, you read why when you are next
>   woken.

The opening paragraph now says "a scheduled or standing order that others foiled" where it said "that failed". Two
"which wakes you" clauses are gone: on orders dropped for a levy that raised none, and on orders for a unit that was
lost. The schema's notes on `schedule` and `standing_orders` say the same.

**Readings taken.**
- *Refusals when the order runs wake nobody either.* The reviewer suggested this only for refusals when the order is
  given. But every check the validator makes is against the seat's own state and orders, so any refusal can be
  arranged on purpose: a scheduled letter with more gold than the treasury holds, or an errand for a servant who will
  be away by then. So a refusal never wakes, whether given or run.
- *No shape check when the order is given.* The reviewer also suggested checking a scheduled order's shape (reach,
  unit kind) when it is given. With no refusal waking a king, that check would change only when he reads of the
  mistake, not whether it wakes him. So it is not built. A move refused when it runs still ends its unit's march, as
  before.
- *No support or convoy that matched no move wakes anyone.* The reviewer said "of its own", but a support for a move
  that another king's unit does not make is just as easy to give on purpose.
- *Walls alone never wake.* A lone attack on walls was the surest way to be woken: strength 1 against 1, every time.
  Walls that others helped hold do wake.
- *Renewals never wake.* The servant's price follows a rule, so a renewal offered too low is a sure failure. A
  servant's notice wakes the king by itself.

**Tests.**
- `ResolutionFailureTests`: every case now states whether it wakes. Eight do not: a cession blocked by the king's own
  troops, a gift or a disband of a unit already gone, a levy where his own unit stands, a levy in land he ceded, a
  botched forgery, a hire below the price, and a renewal below the price. The other thirteen still do.
- `SleepTests`, cases that wake nobody: the reviewer's probe (an entry for the season before, in every reply); an order
  refused when given, when it runs, or because it could not be read; orders past the limits; standing orders not kept;
  a support that matched no move; a move stopped by walls alone or by the king's own unit; orders for a unit he
  disbanded; and orders for a levy that raised nothing.
- `SleepTests`, cases that still wake: a move held off by Assyria's army; a move against walls that Assyria's army
  supported; a cut support; and a bid that other buyers spoiled.
- Thirteen older tests expected the old wakes and were rewritten.

**What remains** (review question 2): a route through other kings' troops. A king can still schedule a weak attack on
a foreign unit he expects to stand, or on walls he expects their owner to support, and be woken when it fails. He can
also contrive it with two units of his own, one attacking walls and the other supporting them. Each costs him a
unit's season, and most depend on another king's orders.

- the opening paragraph now says "foreign troops entering or attacking your land or your troops";
- the bullet on foreign troops now says "when they move into your land or against your troops, whether or not the

The digest no longer marks failed attacks, since they now wake the king. Its remaining "tried to move ... and failed"

**Tests** (`WakeTriggerTests`, `SleepTests`):

### Blocking 3: the scribes leaked the names of people the writer did not know

## Additions: standing orders for every seat

Built alongside the owner's additions, as the review asked. The fix pass made standing orders answer a move in the season

### What every seat now has

- **The engine.** Standing orders live in the game state for each seat that gave one (`GameState.Standing`, a
  `StandingBook` of orders and the last id), not in a sleeping seat's `SleepState`. Every seat's season is planned and
  composed the same way (`TurnEngine.PlanSeason`, `Compose`), its standing orders wait and fire the same way
  (`StandingFires`), and it is told the same way ("Standing order s1 fired"). A seat that plays every season keeps the
  standing orders it gives in any season, unless the stand-in gave them; it cancels them in the season it says so; and
  troops it loses take their standing orders with them at the wake check, which now runs for every seat
  (`WakeCheck`, `LostUnits`). With no standing order waiting anywhere a season resolves as before: with only this commit,
  seeds 1-100 played line for line as `fixpass-review-1-100.txt`, apart from timings.
- **One rule changed for every seat, sleeping seats included: a standing order that fires replaces the season's other
  orders for its units and people, those given that season among them.** Until now the orders a king gave that season
  replaced a standing order's. That suited a king woken for the season, but a seat that gives orders every season gives
  every unit an order (the web app sends a hold at least), so its standing orders could never act. Now a king gives his
  units their orders as usual, and a standing order changes them only if the blow comes; to stop it he cancels it, which
  in its own season stops it firing, as before. The notice says what it replaced: "It gave these orders: durkurigalzu
  supports sippar holding. They replaced these orders you gave for turn 2: durkurigalzu -> nippur." The orders are checked
  in this order: those given now, then the standing orders', then the scheduled ones, then the stand-in's.
- **The rulebook** has a section of its own, STANDING ORDERS (standing_orders; every king may give them), after MOVEMENT
  AND COMBAT, which every king reads: what a standing order waits for; what it can do as the blow falls (support a troop or
  a walled city of the king's holding, or march into an empty province of his so that a lone attacker bounces); how its

### Script kings

`Contingencies` (`src/Amarna.Game/Bots`), called last each season against the whole plan. A standing order given now
first fires next season, so the king gives them against troops that could strike then (within two moves now), and only
to troops that stay where they are this season, whose place next season is known. It rolls no dice.

- **New standing orders**, for each place it holds or stands in under threat, most chance × worth first:
  - a troop of its own that stays, when a feared king could bring two or more troops next season and could move two in
    now: troops next to it support it holding if it is attacked;
  - the walls of a city of its own, likewise: troops next to them support the walls;
  - an empty province of its homeland: the least threatened troop next to it marches in if foreign troops do, so that a
    lone attacker bounces (it stays where it was if both are stopped);
  - a fleet at sea in the first case with nothing to help it: it moves away to an empty refuge where no feared troop could
    move in now.
- **Those kept so far**, which may fire this season, are cancelled when they no longer make sense against the season's
  plan, with the reason in the notes: no threat within two moves, the guarded troop marches or is disbanded, the city is
  lost, one of its own troops moves into the empty province, the plan already gives the same orders or guards the troop
  its own way, the troop or walls hold without it against what could move in now, a troop is gone or out of reach, a
  blocker is one the plan supports holding, or a troop's own order this season is worth more than what the standing order
  guards (the guarded worth must be at least the order's worth ÷ 0.6). The numbers are in `docs/DESIGN-NOTES.md`,
  "Standing orders" under Script kings, all first guesses.

**How it was measured.** A scratch harness (scratchpad `a2/measure`, not committed) played seeds 1-100 as the batch
runner does, counted the standing orders each king gave, cancelled (by reason) and kept, and every one that fired. For
each season in which a king's standing order fired it then replayed that season's movement with the game's adjudicator
twice: as it was, and with the king's troops given the orders he had given them instead. "Better" means more of his
troops survived, or as many and more land was his; "worse" the reverse; "same" no difference. A check that the replay
reproduces the real season (the troops the king lost) failed once in the 577 seasons of the first version, and never
since.

### Readings taken

1. **The precedence is turned round for every seat**, sleeping seats included: one rule, so that standing orders can act
   for a seat that gives orders every season. A woken king who wants his fresh orders to stand cancels the standing order.
2. **Scheduled orders stay a sleeping seat's.** A seat asked every season gives its orders fresh; a schedule would only
   fill units it leaves without orders. The task names standing orders only.
3. **The stand-in neither gives nor cancels standing orders for a seat that plays every season**, as for a sleeping one:
   they are the seat's own plans.
4. **The closing line names standing orders when the king has troops or sleeps** ("Requests are rules too"): a king
   without troops may still give one (a levy, a letter), but it is not offered as if it applied.
5. **The human form offers troop orders only** (support, move, hold) in a standing order; levies and letters in a
   standing order are left to model kings, whose replies may carry them.
6. **"Better" in the measurement** counts troops first, then land. A block that kept the capital but lost a troop counts
   as worse (seed 129).
