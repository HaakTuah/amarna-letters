## Engine and models: Turn structure and the deterministic engine

**Status of sleeping seats** (19 October 2026). Built: sleep by default for model and file seats, a hidden cap of 12 seasons, triggers that fire on changes, audience calls that may reopen orders, and orders that follow their unit; script and human seats play every season. Built in the fix pass after item 4: kings no longer naming a season to be woken or setting triggers of their own (the five seat-defined triggers removed), the missing triggers (a threat to a walled city or a home province), standing orders that no longer fire a season late, and scheduled orders for new levies. The rest of conditional orders comes with item 6.

- **Planning ahead needs better standing orders.** Orders that follow their unit, and conditional orders ("if Hatti enters Mira, support Mira"), move up.

**A temporary trigger: troops next to the capital** (decided 18 October 2026). Until sieges existed, homeland provinces had no walls, so an army next to a capital could take it the following season. Foreign troops ending a move next to a seat's capital therefore woke it, so a sleeping king did not first hear of the danger when his capital fell. Retired in item 5, with the siege package.

**What the replay showed** (18 October 2026). Replaying both seed-7 games under these ideas: a yearly court saved almost nothing, because nearly every envoy counted as pressing (37 of 110 brought a tablet to seal; the sender's troops stood at the host's border in 55, but had arrived there only in 14). What saves calls is answering an envoy without an orders call: an audience costs about 16k tokens, an orders call about 37.5k. Audience-only wakes save about 17%; standing instructions for routine envoys take it to 24–31%. These are upper bounds, since a king who really slept would send fewer envoys. Three conclusions:

If no audience reopened orders, the replay put the saving at about 35% against the triggers of 18 October and 46% against playing every season; the second agent simulation then measured how often kings reopen: at 3 of the 4 courts held while orders stood, at under 2% of their tokens, so reopening stays the king's own choice and needs no fallback.

## Characters: How scripts and models play characters

**Settled before building (19 October 2026).**

- **What a good general does better. Settled by the build order (item 13): the leading candidate below, competence setting the tactical look-ahead and loyalty how strictly he keeps to his limits, with siege progress as a second lever if needed.** Combat is deterministic (strength is one plus supports, ties bounce), so command competence needs a defined lever, or handing an army to a negotiator has no visible consequence. The leading candidate is already in the round report (question 7): competence sets how well a commander interprets a mandate far from the king, through the size of his tactical look-ahead, so a poor general misses moves and a good one finds them; loyalty sets how strictly he keeps to his limits. A second lever, if needed, is siege progress. A flat combat bonus is the last resort, since it compounds snowballing and blurs the adjudicator.

- **The purse economy.** First proposed as one rule, a living cost each season scaled by how rich the court is, with travel costs, trait-shaped spending, robbery and debts only if purses grew too large. Superseded on 19 October 2026 by "Where characters keep their gold" and "Lending to kings": purse, hoard and loans, escort and storage costs, robbery and debt tablets are all decided, with their sizes set by balance tests. The living cost each season stays, as the ongoing costs of build order item 11.

- **Make lives visible. Settled: the "follow this character" view is part of the visual overhaul (build order item 2).** Some sixty characters with lives of their own matter only if someone can watch them. A "follow this character" view shows one person's path on the map, employers, missions, claims and fate, and goes with the visual overhaul.

## War, travel and the seas: Tempo of war: further ways to slow it

- **Rare elimination (replaced 19 October 2026).** A king who loses his capital can hold out in another city or go into exile rather than vanishing. Replaced by "A kingdom falls with its capital": losing the capital ends the kingdom, and a king survives it only if he had moved his court before it fell.

## Ideas: Other ideas under consideration

- **Passage and access. (Decided in part: passage, basing and trade rights granted on sealed tablets come with envoy authority, build order item 9. Decided 19 October 2026: a host can stop anyone passing through his land, and what he does with them is his choice. A traveller who passes openly can be stopped at will; one who sneaks through is found only if a chance check discovers him, and the odds weigh his skills and what he carries against what the host spends on policing in that province. The check is part of interception on the road, build order item 7.)** Whether envoys, gold and armies may cross foreign territory, what the host may do when they pass, and whether sealed tablets can grant passage, basing or trade rights, including for allies marching on a common enemy.

- **Distance and travel. (Decided 19 October 2026, build order item 7: everything travels the map physically, by one measure of distance, steps along the province map weighted by terrain, so journeys differ greatly in length and in how likely they are to arrive.)** Whether travel time follows real distance rather than equal steps between provinces: decided 21 October 2026, it follows real distance, each connection having a length weighted by its terrain ("Map review").

- **Distance inside your own realm.** **(Decided 19 October 2026: the same time restrictions apply inside a realm as between courts. Built with "Command at a distance", build order item 13.)** Today a king sees and commands his whole realm instantly. Instead, news from provinces and orders to armies would travel to and from the capital, as when the pharaoh learned of the Levant weeks late. This weakens the size advantage, makes scheduled orders essential, and lets the map show the last known state of distant provinces with its date. Income arriving with a distance-based delay is a simpler first step than moving gold physically. A later layer: kings invest in infrastructure, roads and relay stations with upkeep, in the spirit of Egypt's fortified Ways of Horus across Sinai. It speeds messages and orders but also invading armies, can be captured or cut, and can be built jointly with a neighbor under a sealed tablet, though messages passing through a neighbor's stations may be read there. Every order a king gives inside his realm travels from where he is, levies included: a levy at the far end of the realm is raised when the order gets there, not in the season it is given, unless a governor there raises it under his own mandate ("Governors" under "Command at a distance"). This is what gives moving the capital its point despite the risk, and what makes roads, relay stations and policing worth paying for. Orders and reports inside the realm travel with the realm's own couriers (decided 21 October 2026): men in a unit, like caravans, not named characters, since they speak the realm's own language and need no audience at a foreign court. In hostile land they can be stopped, and a captured order can be read by whoever has a reader. A letter to a foreign court still needs a character ("No faceless channel"). What a king sees as current is whatever a courier could bring him within the season, and everything else shows its last report with its date. No circle is computed: his reach grows with roads, relay stations and river boats, shrinks in winter and when the passes close, and moves with him, and the map can draw it around him as a display.

- **Geography-based disasters. (Decided 21 October 2026 in "Land and climate"; see also "Rivers".)** Rivers and neighbors shaping where floods and famines strike and spread; Mesopotamian floods were less predictable and more destructive than the Nile's.

- **Varying output. (Decided 21 October 2026: "People of the land" and "Land and climate".)** Province yields that change with seasons, disasters and time.

- **Calendar. (Decided in part: every date in the interface shows the court's season and year, with the turn, in the visual overhaul, build order item 2. Decided 19 October 2026, first year changed on 20 October: years are counted from 1346 BCE, the first year of a game being 1346 BCE and each later year one less, so the game opens as Akhenaten's court settles at Akhetaten, and its median end, 1332 BCE, is the conventional end of the archive in the same reckoning (dates shift by a few years between chronologies). This is for the interface only: kings' prompts keep the game's own year ("year 3") and never name a year BCE, since a precise date invites models to replay real history (item 2 built it counting from 1345 BCE; item 6a moves the start to 1346 BCE).)** Showing year and season in the interface, starting in 1346 BCE, when Akhenaten's court has moved to Akhetaten (the letters begin around 1360, under his father, which would not fit this cast).

- **Script versus model characters. (Answered in build order item 4: script and model characters decide from the same traits so they can be compared, and the second agent simulation measured how they differ.)** Which characters gain from being played by a model, and which work as well as scripts.

## Ideas: Voice of the age: ideas for later

- **The scribe.** Tushratta spoke Hurrian, his scribe wrote Akkadian, and an Egyptian scribe read it out to Pharaoh; Finkel imagines a scribe softening an angry letter on the way. In the game the scribe does not relay or alter messages, unless he is in someone else's pay ("A king seals what his scribe wrote"): otherwise he has no motive to, and one mediator keeps the trail readable (see "Languages"). His role is what he knows. He writes his king's tablets and reads the foreign ones, so he holds the court's secrets: bribable, spyable, a source of leaks (see "What servants know").

## Ideas: Beyond the prototype

- **City-states as script bots.** **(Superseded 20 October 2026 by a larger decision: city-states are powers like the kings, on one scoreboard, played by the small or the leader model as their stakes rise; see "One scoreboard for every power", "Vassalage is words" and "Minds follow what is at stake", build order item 11+. This entry's script-only rules and "Promotion", below, give way to those.)** A tiered world: Great Kings are language models, characters are small models, and many vassal city-states are cheap deterministic scripts. Vassal rules mirror the letters: follow the strongest nearby power with a delay, pay tribute when threatened, remember who helped and who abandoned them, and switch sides when the overlord falters. A script decides what a city-state does; a cheap model may write its letters.

- **Promotion: level of detail for intelligence.** When a city-state matters (several provinces, a large treasury, courted by several kings, holding tablets), the engine promotes it to a model leader with a summary of its history, and demotes it if it shrinks. A rising city-state could even claim Great King status, as Assyria did and as Aziru of Amurru nearly did.

- **Fallen kings return as characters (dropped 19 October 2026: a fallen king is dead, and the dead do not return).** An eliminated player re-enters the pool as an envoy or spy for hire, with memories of who betrayed them. It keeps humans in the game and makes "only the dead may talk" literally true. Deposed rulers really did end up at foreign courts, like Urhi-Teshub, sheltered in Egypt.

3. **Small record items.** "Tablets are objects in a place" (archives at the capital; spies can copy from them; whoever takes a capital gains copies of its archive), "Swapped tablets" (an envoy replacing an inventory tablet with a forgery stating less, under the existing forgery rules) and "The richest characters" (a ranking by wealth at the end of a game; if characters' gold is not tracked yet, track what they gain and spend). All.

4. **Second agent simulation, with model characters.** First, three fixes, each with its own commit and tests, the first two left by item 2's report. (a) A leak: the engine's seat view lists a foreign spy posted at a court together with its employer. A king must see a foreign spy at his court, and who employs it, only once his court has caught it or otherwise learned it; add the case to the hidden-state tests. Agents would exploit the leak, so it is fixed before the simulation. (b) Phone performance, because watching on a phone is the first milestone: playing a season must not freeze the page (it blocks for up to about 3 s on a slow emulated phone, with the script kings' look-ahead on the single WebAssembly thread), and the first load must be much faster than about 20 s on slow 4G. Use the fixes the report names, such as running the engine in a Web Worker, ahead-of-time compilation, and splitting the SDK path out of Amarna.Llm, keeping seeds and replays exact; measure before and after on the same throttled profile. (c) A latent bug: a \`?? Power.Egypt\` fallback makes Egypt the court of a character with no employer or location; a character with neither belongs to no court. Then the simulation: extend the `sim/` kit so envoys and spies are agents too, isolated like the kings, and run seed 7 and one new seed for ten seasons with sleep as the default. Measure what the first simulation could not: whether model envoys lie, skim and cover up, and how they compare with script envoys of the same traits; how often kings reopen their orders after an audience; wake-ups and call sizes under the new triggers. **Stop for review:** the results show whether model characters are too honest, in which case they are given real stakes, never engine-rolled decisions or priming to misbehave ("Stakes, not dispositions"), and whether the message-kind fallback for reopening is needed. Before stopping, make the kit playable for Haakon: "Local play on a Claude subscription", a human seat in the locally running game against file-seat kings answered by guarded, isolated king agents, so that at this review he can start playing model kings himself.

5. **The siege package (pillar 8).** Walls that vary and can be strengthened, storming and besieging, envoys leaving a besieged city, terrain per province and per connection, deserts costing supplies, islands protected by the sea, and slow absorption of conquered land. A kingdom falls with its capital, its land passing to the conqueror ("A kingdom falls with its capital"); it comes in this package, since without walls a capital can fall in the third season. With it, "A fallen player's game is over": the fallen player watches the rest in god view, a setting on by default that a table can turn off, in which case he watches with what his kingdom knew when it fell. Specification: "Tempo of war" and "Walls and terrain", and question 5 of the old round report, with its terrain lists. Retire the temporary trigger for troops next to the capital. The desert cost follows "Different data, same rules": question 5 exempts Egypt's armies in Sinai, after the Ways of Horus; build it instead as "an army in its own land pays no desert cost", so Egypt is exempt because it holds Sinai, and so is anyone who takes it. Set first guesses aiming at about 5–10 seasons from the first hostile entry to a capital's fall, with capitals still falling in most games; tuning waits for item 18. Military timings start from the pace of letters and what is realistic for the period, and item 18 tunes them ("Distance-based marching"). **Stop for review:** a first look at how long wars and games now run; game length and the ending rule are set in item 18.
